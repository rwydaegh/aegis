% PREV: Street-level radiofrequency exposure at 15 GHz depends on the route geometry,
% PREV: surface materials, source support, and body orientation. This study uses
% PREV: registered street panoramas and a 250 m-radius photogrammetric support mesh to assign
% PREV: material evidence around five selected pedestrian routes. A common source
% PREV: measure sets the expected source count from areal density and weights the
% PREV: observed route-aligned roofline by physical arc length. All exposure values are
% PREV: normalized per unit areal density times effective isotropic radiated power.
% PREV: Transport has exact direct, exact first-order specular, and stochastic
% PREV: first-diffuse terms. Each stochastic first-diffuse path ends at that
% PREV: interaction.
% PREV: The directional fields are coupled to a 56,024-element Duke body surface. The
% PREV: five routes contain 73 standpoints. Each standpoint uses 16 independent
% PREV: replicas, 200,000 primary rays per replica, and 4,096 fixed first-diffuse angular cells. In
% PREV: a controlled open-square case, the maximum bounced-term difference between the
% PREV: adjoint estimator and deterministic quadrature is 0.0616 dB, and the maximum
% PREV: total-transport difference between the adjoint and forward tracers is 0.0344
% PREV: dB. The route medians of normalized whole-body specific
% PREV: absorption rate differ by a factor of 13.34. Direct transport is the largest
% PREV: term at all 67 nonshadowed standpoints. First-diffuse transport gives the only
% PREV: nonzero modeled contribution at six shadowed standpoints. The maximum 12-to-16-replica change
% PREV: in total transfer is 0.0436 dB across the five sites, although lower-tail
% PREV: estimates in Mexico City and Tokyo are less stable. The result is a conditional
% PREV: comparison of five selected routes, not a city ranking or a deployed-network
% PREV: estimate.
% PREV: \end{abstract}
