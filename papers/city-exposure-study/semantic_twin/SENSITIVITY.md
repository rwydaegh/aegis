# Sensitivity of the susceptibility to the deployment geometry

The three illumination models integrate over base stations of uniform areal
density lying in a height band and a range band. `DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md`
section 4.3 audits 3.86 million European antennas and finds zero deployed
cellular access above 6 GHz, so neither band can be cited against a
frequency-matched deployment. They can only be assumed. An assumption that
cannot be cited has to be reported as a sensitivity, and this document is that
report.

It replaces the zero-bounce proxy `K0` of `DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md` section 7.5,
which was computed from a sky-visibility raycast at the crop centre and which
that section itself flagged as an overstatement. Everything here is the full
traced `chi`, at all eleven sites, for all three illumination models, on the
250 m crop the study publishes at.

Everything is regenerated by

```bash
python make_sensitivity_study.py --harvest --stride 2   # trace, about 2 h
python make_sensitivity_study.py --analyse --figures    # sweeps and plots
```

and lands in `outputs/sensitivity/`: `sensitivity.json` holds every number below,
`sweep_*.csv` holds the swept curves as flat tables, and the five figure pairs
per illumination model are named `range_cap_*`, `endpoints_*`, `contrast_*`,
`crop_conflict_*` and `interaction_*`.

## 1. How a dense sweep was made affordable

`chi` is a linear functional of the illumination density. The tracer forms

```
chi = dOmega * sum over cells of (1 / n_cell)
      * sum over escaping rays of throughput * Q(u_exit)
```

and every band law is uniform in azimuth, so `Q(u)` depends on the exit
direction only through its elevation. Accumulating one vector per standpoint,

```
W[b] = dOmega * sum over escaping rays in elevation bin b of
       throughput / n_cell(ray)
```

reduces the whole trace to 4500 numbers, and `chi` for any illumination model
whatsoever becomes the dot product `W . Q`. One trace per standpoint then
supports an arbitrarily dense parameter sweep, exactly, with no Monte Carlo
noise between parameter values, because every value of every parameter is
scored on the same rays.

`W` does not scale with the ray count, so keeping it does not break the storage
rule of this study. No path is written. `HarvestTracer` extends the shipped
`_deposit` rather than replacing it, touches no random draw, and returns a
`chi` identical to the plain tracer's on the same seed.

### 1.1 Validation

| check | result |
| --- | --- |
| Harvested `chi` against the tracer's own, isotropic | 1.1e-9 to 2.1e-9 worst standpoint |
| Harvested `chi` against the tracer's own, rooftop | 3.8e-5 to 1.2e-4 worst standpoint |
| Harvested `chi` against the tracer's own, street small cell | 9.1e-4 to 3.1e-3 worst standpoint |
| Reduced normalising quadrature against the shipped 200 001 point one | 3.8e-7 rooftop, 4.2e-6 street |
| Harvested `chi` against the production runs on disk | 40 of 40 standpoints shared at every site, 0.0 to 1.8e-3 median |

The street model is the worst case for the elevation binning because its support
starts at 0.95 degrees, and 0.3 percent on the worst single standpoint is still
two orders of magnitude below anything reported here.

The last row is the one worth reading carefully. Every one of the forty
harvested standpoints is a standpoint the production run also traced, at all
eleven sites. Krakow and Toulouse reproduce their production `chi` bit
identically. The rest agree to between 3e-4 and 1.8e-3, which is 0.001 to
0.008 dB and below the noise floor of section 1.2, and the residual is the ray
tracer's own nondeterminism: a single hit test that resolves differently changes
the size of the surviving ray set, which shifts the random stream for everything
after it.

The runs it matches are the current generation, `city250_datum`, `city250_L3`
and `city250_rebuildb3`. It shares only 2 to 10 standpoints with
`city250_corrected` at ten of the eleven sites, because the ground datum
estimator was replaced after that run and the walk depends on the datum. So read
the absolute levels below as the current pipeline's rather than as
`city250_corrected`'s. The relative results are unaffected either way, since
every sweep is scored on one set of rays.

### 1.2 Against the Monte Carlo noise floor

`CODE_AUDIT.md` section 4.1 measures the standard deviation of the walk median
over eight disjoint seed streams at 120 standpoints: 0.0042 dB isotropic,
0.0136 dB rooftop, 0.0343 dB street small cell. This study uses 40 standpoints
per site, so scale by the square root of three: about 0.007, 0.024 and 0.059 dB.

Every effect reported below is at least an order of magnitude above that, and
the smallest of them, the 0.30 dB height-floor span at New York, is twelve times
the floor. The sweeps are also paired: two parameter values at one site are read
off the same escaping rays, so the difference between them carries no
independent Monte Carlo noise at all and the floor above is an upper bound on
its error.

Median over the eleven sites, relative to the published 250 m cap: **+4.11 dB at
100 m, +2.69 dB at 150 m, -3.36 dB at 400 m**. The street small cell model, whose
published cap is 150 m, moves further: +2.78 dB at 100 m, -3.57 dB at 250 m and
-6.31 dB at 400 m, for a median span of 9.24 dB and a worst of 11.18 dB.

**It does not saturate.** `range_cap_rooftop.png` sweeps 50 to 500 m in 5 m steps
and there is no plateau anywhere in it. Averaged across 100 to 400 m the median
site follows `chi` proportional to `d_max^-1.2` for the macro model and
`d_max^-1.5` for the street model, and the local slope at the published cap is
steeper still, -1.5 and -1.6. No cap can be defended on the grounds that the
answer stops moving past it, because it never does.

The mechanism is not that the cap scales the weight. It moves the lower edge of
the elevation support, and the support edge is where the mass is. For the macro
band, with no site involved at all:

| cap | support | measure below 5 deg | below 10 deg | median arrival |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 m | 7.69 to 60.11 deg | 0.000 | 0.032 | 22.09 deg |
| 150 m | 5.14 to 60.11 deg | 0.000 | 0.182 | 15.43 deg |
| 250 m | 3.09 to 60.11 deg | 0.094 | 0.549 | 9.49 deg |
| 400 m | 1.93 to 60.11 deg | 0.351 | 0.825 | 6.00 deg |
| 500 m | 1.55 to 60.11 deg | 0.538 | 0.888 | 4.82 deg |

That is why the per-site spans are so unequal. Moving the cap from 100 to 400 m
moves the median arrival from 22 to 6 degrees, and 6 degrees is where the
eleven scenes differ most from each other. New York, whose open-sky fraction is
nearly flat with elevation, barely notices at 2.67 dB. Madrid and Brussels,
whose sky closes hard below 15 degrees, move 9.9 and 9.2 dB.

## 3. The height band edges

Each endpoint moved on its own, everything else held at the published value,
reported as the dB swing of the site median across the endpoint's defensible
span. The spans come from `DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md` sections 11.2 and 12: for the
macro band, the floor over the two branches of section 4.4 at 8.5 and 16.5 m,
the ceiling over 33.5 to 43.5 m, the range floor over 10 to 50 m and the range
cap over 150 to 400 m.

| endpoint | rooftop, min to max over sites | median | street, median |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| height floor `h_min` | 0.30 to 0.97 dB | 0.88 dB | 1.50 dB |
| height ceiling `h_max` | 0.27 to 1.88 dB | 1.44 dB | 3.49 dB |
| range floor `d_min` | 0.35 to 1.17 dB | 0.51 dB | 2.85 dB |
| range cap `d_max` | 1.74 to 7.82 dB | 5.99 dB | 6.48 dB |

For the macro model this confirms the ordering `DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md` section
7.5 found on the proxy, and confirms it on the real `chi`: the height band is
worth under 2 dB across every defensible endpoint and the cap is worth six.

**For the street small cell model it does not hold as cleanly, and that is new.**
The range floor is worth 2.85 dB at the median site and 6.77 dB at Madrid, and
the height ceiling 3.49 dB. Section 12 of `DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md` lists the
small cell range floor as "low sensitivity", which is a macro-band result read
across to a band it does not describe. The street band's upper support edge is
`atan(h_max/d_min)`, and halving `d_min` from 10 to 5 m opens it from 33 to 52
degrees, which is a large change to a model that carries 88 percent of its
measure below 5 degrees.

## 4. The interaction, and an exact invariance

The range cap and the height band are not separable, because both enter through
`atan(h/r)`. The exact statement is stronger than that and is worth putting in
the paper.

**Every band law here is scale free.** Multiplying all four endpoints by a common
factor leaves `Q` unchanged, because the unnormalised weight and its
normalisation both scale as the cube of the length. Measured, doubling the whole
deployment box moves `chi` by 0.0 relative at every site and both models. So the
four endpoints carry three degrees of freedom, not four, and doubling the range
cap is exactly equivalent to halving the height band.

The check that follows is that the four elasticities must sum to zero. They do,
to 0.034 dB per natural log at the worst site and to under 0.001 at most.

Elasticity, dB per natural log of the parameter, at the published point:

| model | `h_min` | `h_max` | `d_min` | `d_max` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rooftop, over eleven sites | +0.42 to +1.64 | +1.24 to +7.35 | -0.65 to -0.22 | -8.36 to -1.40 |
| street, over eleven sites | +1.23 to +2.34 | +3.20 to +8.98 | -3.30 to -0.11 | -8.03 to -3.83 |

**This reframes the height-versus-range comparison of section 3.** Per natural
log, the height ceiling's elasticity is 65 to 90 percent of the range cap's. The
cap dominates the table above only because its assumed interval spans a factor
of 2.7 while the ceiling's spans a factor of 1.3. The correct statement is not
that `chi` is insensitive to the height band, it is that the height band is
believed more tightly. If a future argument widened the ceiling interval to the
same factor of 2.7, it would carry the same leverage.

The two-dimensional sweep is `interaction_rooftop.png`, `chi` over a grid of
range cap against height floor with the lines of constant `atan(h_min/d_max)`
drawn on top. **The contours do not follow those lines.** A single-variable fit
in the lower support edge explains 86 to 90 percent of the surface, while a fit
in the range cap alone explains 97 to 99 percent. So the tempting reduction, that
the two uncitable endpoints collapse into one citable elevation edge, is wrong,
and the paper should not make it.

## 5. Which parameter dominates, per site

The range cap dominates at all eleven sites and in both band models, on the
defensible spans of section 3. How far it dominates by is the part that differs,
and it differs between the two models more than it differs between cities.

For the macro rooftop band the cap's span is 3.8 to 5.8 times the largest height
endpoint's, tightest at Toulouse and widest at New York, and no site brings any
endpoint inside a factor of three. For the street small cell band the margin is
1.4 to 2.2, tightest at Tokyo, and at Madrid the range floor comes within 9
percent of the cap. So "only the cap matters" is a fair summary of the macro
model and an overstatement of the street model, where three of the four
endpoints are live at once.

Read together with section 2, the illumination assumption is concentrated in the
deep-canyon sites, which is the same structural point `DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md`
section 7.5 made on the proxy, now measured on `chi`.

The isotropic model is the control. It has no band at all, so its sensitivity to
every parameter here is identically zero by construction, and nothing in the
harness perturbs it.

## 6. The crop radius against the range cap, which are not the same thing

The geometry crop is how much mesh was reconstructed, 250 m of horizontal radius
about the square centre. The range cap is how far away sources are integrated.
A cap larger than the built extent integrates over sources whose intervening
geometry was never built, and the current code does conflate them: nothing
anywhere checks one against the other.

The two are not even comparable at face value, because the crop is centred on
the square and the standpoints are not. The walk reaches 90 m from the centre,
so a standpoint at the rim has 160 m of built mesh on its near side and 340 m on
its far side. Measured on the escaping rays themselves, in the 3 to 10 degree
window where the macro band puts most of its measure, the built extent has a
median of 239 to 279 m across the eleven sites and a p10 of 180 to 227 m.

Refusing every source that lies beyond the point where its own ray leaves the
built mesh gives the following. The share is of the site median `chi`, and it is
an upper bound on the true share because the built extent is binned and the
near edge of the bin is used.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Madrid Plaza Mayor | 0.0 % | 0.5 % | -0.02 | 5.0 % | -0.22 |
| Prague Staromestske | 0.0 % | 1.5 % | -0.05 | 14.3 % | -0.64 |
| Brussels Grand-Place | 0.0 % | 1.8 % | -0.05 | 11.0 % | -0.50 |
| London Trafalgar | 0.0 % | 2.4 % | -0.09 | 15.3 % | -0.73 |
| Toulouse Capitole | 0.0 % | 3.2 % | -0.09 | 14.0 % | -0.51 |
| Tokyo Hachiko | 0.0 % | 3.9 % | -0.26 | 28.1 % | -1.81 |
| Krakow Rynek | 0.0 % | 4.1 % | -0.17 | 16.2 % | -0.83 |
| Milan Duomo | 0.0 % | 4.2 % | -0.27 | 24.2 % | -0.99 |
| Mexico Zocalo | 0.0 % | 4.8 % | -0.21 | 18.2 % | -0.92 |
| Ghent Korenmarkt | 0.0 % | 7.0 % | -0.47 | 21.6 % | -1.14 |
| New York Times Square | 0.0 % | 20.1 % | -1.23 | 59.5 % | -4.17 |

Three conclusions.

**No published number extrapolates beyond its own mesh at a cap of 175 m or
below.** The share is 0.000 percent at every site at a 150 m cap and at most
0.03 percent at 175 m, then 5.6 percent at 200 m, which places the built extent
floor of the study between 175 and 200 m. The street small cell model, published
at a 150 m cap, is therefore clean.

**The 250 m rooftop number is a mild extrapolation.** Between 0.5 and 7 percent
of `chi` comes from sources outside the mesh at ten of the eleven sites, worth
0.02 to 0.47 dB. New York is the exception at 20 percent and 1.23 dB, because
its towers close off everything except a few narrow near-horizontal channels and
those channels run a long way.

**A 400 m column on a 250 m crop is not supportable.** Between 5 and 60 percent
of `chi` would come from unbuilt lines of sight, worth 0.22 to 4.17 dB. That
matters directly, because reporting `chi` at 400 m is what
`DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md` section 12 currently recommends.

The sign of the error is known and one-sided. Missing geometry means missing
blockers, not missing scatterers, so the published number is the upper end of a
bracket whose lower end is the "sources refused" column. The truth is between
them and closer to the top, since not every unbuilt line of sight would have been
blocked. Quoting the bracket is honest. Quoting the 400 m number alone is not.

## 7. Is there a better invariant than the absolute number

Four candidate reported quantities, each scored the same way: how far does it
move, per site, when the cap is swept across 100 to 400 m.

| reported quantity | rooftop, median swing | worst | street, median | worst |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| absolute `chi` | 7.40 dB | 9.91 dB | 9.24 dB | 11.18 dB |
| contrast to the cross-city mean | **0.51 dB** | 4.64 dB | 1.82 dB | 3.99 dB |
| `chi` over `chi_isotropic` | 7.40 dB | 9.91 dB | 9.24 dB | 11.18 dB |
| contrast to the most open site | 0.84 dB | 5.10 dB | 3.76 dB | 5.68 dB |

**The between-city contrast is the better invariant, by a factor of fourteen at
the median site for the macro model and five for the street model.** Eight of the
eleven cities hold their position relative to the cross-city mean to within
0.75 dB while the mean itself moves 7.3 dB. That is a strong relative claim
standing on a weak absolute one, and it is worth the paper leading with it.

It is not unconditional, and the exceptions have to be named rather than
averaged away. Rooftop contrast swings, in ascending order: Ghent 0.19, Mexico
City 0.33, Krakow 0.39, Prague 0.42, London 0.48, Milan 0.51, Toulouse 0.70,
Tokyo 0.75, then **Brussels 1.92, Madrid 2.60, New York 4.64**. Dropping New York
leaves a median of 0.74 dB and a worst of 2.14 dB.

Two further findings, both negative and both worth stating.

**Normalising by the isotropic susceptibility does not help.** `chi_isotropic`
carries no band, so dividing by it rescales each site by a cap-independent
constant and leaves the cap sensitivity exactly where it was. It is a useful
decomposition, separating how enclosed a site is from what the illumination
assumption costs, but it is not a robustness fix and should not be sold as one.

**The ranking is not cap-invariant.** Between the 100 m and 400 m orderings, 7 of
11 sites change rank for the macro model and 9 of 11 for the street model, with
a largest move of five places and a Spearman correlation of 0.81 and 0.84. The
cross-site spread also breathes, from 4.63 to 6.32 dB rooftop and 7.70 to
12.34 dB street. So "city A is more exposed than city B" is a claim that has to
be made at a stated cap, even though the size of the gap between a typical pair
is stable.

## 8. The reporting recommendation

1. **Report the curve, not a point.** `chi` against the range cap is one dot
   product per parameter value once a run is harvested, so there is no cost
   argument for a single number. If a table is needed, use 100, 150 and 250 m.
2. **Do not print a bare 400 m column at a 250 m crop.** Section 6 measures 5 to
   60 percent of that number as coming from geometry that was never built.
   Either build 400 m crops for the sites that support them or print the
   bracket. This supersedes the recommendation in `DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md`
   section 12, which was written before the crop and the cap were measured
   against each other.
3. **Lead with the contrast and carry the level as a band.** At a fixed cap the
   between-city contrast is stable to 0.51 dB at the median site against 7.4 dB
   of absolute drift. Name Brussels, Madrid and New York as the three that move
   more.
4. **Do not claim the ordering is cap-independent.** Quote the Spearman
   correlation instead.
5. **Settle the height band, and give the right reason.** Its endpoints are worth
   0.3 to 1.9 dB across their defensible spans against the cap's 1.7 to 7.8, but
   that ordering follows from how tightly each interval is believed, not from the
   physics. Per natural log the ceiling's elasticity is 65 to 90 percent of the
   cap's.
6. **State the scale invariance.** Four endpoints, three degrees of freedom, and
   doubling the deployment box changes nothing. It costs one sentence, it is
   exact, and it explains the whole interaction structure.
7. **Treat the street small cell band's own range floor as live.** Section 3
   measures it at 2.85 dB median and 6.77 dB at Madrid, which is not the low
   sensitivity the macro result would suggest.

## 9. What this does not settle

The height distribution inside the band is uniform throughout, as it is in the
shipped models. `DEPLOYMENT_GEOMETRY.md` section 10.1 measures the shape question
at under one degree of median arrival elevation between defensible shapes, so it
is expected to be small, but it is not measured here on `chi`.

The bands are global. Section 11.1 of that document recommends defining the
classes against the local roofline instead, which would make the height band a
per-site quantity and would change the cross-city comparison in a way no sweep
over a global band can anticipate.

Forty standpoints per site, not eighty. Every published standpoint traced here is
one the production run also traced, alternating through its list, so the site
medians are drawn from the same walk. The absolute levels carry the sampling
error of forty rather than eighty, which the section 1.2 floor accounts for. The
differential results do not, because they are paired on identical rays.

The crop bracket of section 6 is one-sided by construction. It says what `chi`
would be if every unbuilt line of sight were fully blocked, which is the far end
of the bracket rather than a correction. Measuring the true value needs larger
crops, and only Korenmarkt has a mesh beyond 250 m.
