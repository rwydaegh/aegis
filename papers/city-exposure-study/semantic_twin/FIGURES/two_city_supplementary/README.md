# Supplementary campaign analyses

Two figure sets, both mined from retained campaign artefacts and both generated by
the scripts in this directory. Nothing here is a new simulation.

- `five_city_supplementary.py` writes `C1` to `C6` and `five_city_numbers.json`.
  Primary subject, current production contract.
- `two_city_supplementary.py` writes `S1` to `S7` and `supplementary_numbers.json`.
  Historical, superseded transport contract, kept because it is the only paired
  sampler comparison that exists.
- `_supplementary_common.py` holds the shared IEEE single-column style.

Run either with the study venv:

```bash
cd papers/city-exposure-study/semantic_twin
.venv/bin/python FIGURES/two_city_supplementary/five_city_supplementary.py
.venv/bin/python FIGURES/two_city_supplementary/two_city_supplementary.py
```

Every number below is recomputed by those scripts and written to the two JSON
files. Both scripts assert the retained column schema before reading an array, so a
schema change fails instead of relabelling a curve. All exposure values are
normalised per unit `rho_A P_EIRP` and are not deployment-absolute.

Every CDF in both sets is an empirical CDF over the standpoints of one fixed
registered route. None of them is a population exposure CDF.

## Current five-city campaign

Contract `first_material_interaction_v1`: exact direct, exact order-1 all-specular,
and a stochastic next-event estimate of the first diffuse interaction only, with
200,000 IID primary rays and 4,096 output cells. Five cities on
`provider_corridor_v1` routes, seeds 7 to 22, looks 4, 8, 12, 16. Source is
`outputs/roofline_campaign/current_five_city_first_material_interaction/current_five_city_first_material_interaction.json`
in the production worktree.

The audit block in `five_city_numbers.json` reproduces the published 12-to-16
maximum total-transfer changes exactly: 0.0000819 dB Korenmarkt, 0.0001559 dB
Prague, 0.000408 dB Madrid, 0.043625 dB Mexico City, 0.019732 dB Tokyo. The three
components also sum to the retained total to 4.4e-16 relative error in every city.

### C1, per-standpoint transfer profile

Transfer in dB against route distance, one row per city, all four components.
Structural zeros are drawn as gaps, and the standpoints with no direct transport
carry a marker.

- Three cities barely move along their route. Total transfer spans 1.56 dB over
  10 standpoints at Korenmarkt, 1.18 dB over 22 at Prague, and 0.90 dB over 14 at
  Madrid.
- Mexico City spans 59.27 dB over 11 standpoints and Tokyo 39.32 dB over 16. Both
  spans are set by the shadowed standpoints, not by gradual decay.
- Order-1 specular sits a median 3.78 dB below direct at Madrid, the tightest of
  the five, against 5.55 dB at Korenmarkt, 5.68 dB at Prague, 6.99 dB at Tokyo and
  8.08 dB at Mexico City. The median is taken over standpoints where both terms are
  positive, so the zero-direct points do not enter it.

### C2, component share and multipath surplus

Stacked specular and diffuse share of total transfer, with the multipath surplus on
the right axis.

- Madrid has both the largest specular share, median 29.40 percent, and the largest
  surplus, median 1.534 dB. Korenmarkt is 21.60 percent and 1.108 dB, Prague
  21.17 percent and 1.061 dB.
- Mexico City has the weakest multipath of the five, median surplus 0.721 dB over
  the 8 standpoints where it is defined, and the lowest specular share, 10.58
  percent.
- First diffuse is a sub-percent term wherever direct exists: median share 0.95
  percent at Korenmarkt, 0.32 percent at Prague and Madrid, 0.56 percent at Tokyo.
  It reaches 100 percent only at the six zero-direct standpoints.
- Surplus is undefined at 3 standpoints in Mexico City and 3 in Tokyo, because the
  denominator is exactly zero. Those points are excluded from surplus statistics
  and reported separately, not imputed.

### C3, cross-city fixed-route distributions

Surplus, normalised wbSAR and peak-to-mean ECDFs, plus the campaign's own bootstrap
route quantiles with 95 percent percentile intervals.

- Route-median wbSAR spans an order of magnitude across the five cities: 0.12906
  (Mexico City), 0.06205 (Korenmarkt), 0.02238 (Madrid), 0.01307 (Prague),
  0.009674 m^2 kg^-1 (Tokyo).
- Within-route spread splits the cohort. Korenmarkt, Madrid and Prague span 0.096,
  0.085 and 0.116 decades of wbSAR. Mexico City spans 5.94 decades and Tokyo 3.78.
- Peak-to-mean of the replica-mean body field has a median of 2.255 at Korenmarkt
  and 1.521 at Madrid, and reaches 4.93 at Tokyo and 3.98 at Mexico City.
- The bootstrap route median is tight everywhere: relative 95 percent interval
  width 0.0047 to 0.0134 percent. The route q10 is not. It is 13.15 percent wide at
  Mexico City and 2.49 percent at Tokyo, against at most 0.020 percent for the
  other three. The lower tail is the only place finite replicas are visible.

### C4, convergence against look

Per-standpoint standard error of total transfer at each look, the p90 look-to-look
shift, and the tail-instability comparison the campaign records.

- At look 16 the p90 standard error is 2.6e-4 dB (Korenmarkt), 2.4e-4 dB (Prague)
  and 3.4e-4 dB (Madrid), but 0.0310 dB (Tokyo) and 0.1461 dB (Mexico City). Two
  orders of magnitude separate the closed squares from the two with shadowed
  standpoints.
- Convergence is not monotone in Tokyo. Its p90 look-to-look shift rises from
  0.00756 dB at 4-to-8 to 0.01731 dB at 8-to-12 before falling to 0.00632 dB at
  12-to-16.
- The route median is far more stable than its worst lower-decile standpoint. At
  12-to-16 the ratio of worst lower-decile shift to route q50 shift is 658 at
  Mexico City, 317 at Tokyo, 18.6 at Prague and 17.1 at Madrid. Only Korenmarkt is
  below one, at 0.90.

### C5, standpoints with zero direct transport

Component transfer per standpoint for Mexico City and Tokyo, with the zero-direct
standpoints shaded.

- Mexico City standpoints 0, 1 and 3 and Tokyo standpoints 13, 14 and 15 have zero
  direct transfer. In all six the order-1 specular transfer is also exactly zero,
  so the entire exposure is carried by the first-diffuse term.
- The median wbSAR deficit against the lit standpoints of the same route is
  52.07 dB at Mexico City and 25.68 dB at Tokyo.
- The shadowed points are not uniformly weak. Mexico City standpoint 3 has a
  first-diffuse transfer of 1.078e-4 m^-2, about four orders of magnitude above
  standpoints 0 and 1 at 1.50e-8 and 1.89e-8 m^-2.

### C6, 16 replicas against 64

The only seed-level test available. It uses the Mexico City and Tokyo 64-replica
directories, which ran the older `hybrid_max_bounces_v1` contract, so it is a
statement about replica count and not about the current transport model. Their
first 16 seeds are bit-identical to the 16-replica campaigns, maximum absolute
difference 0.0, which is what makes the nested comparison valid.

- Moving from 16 to 64 replicas moves the per-standpoint total transfer by a median
  0.00030 dB (Mexico City) and 0.00078 dB (Tokyo), but by 0.569 dB at Mexico City
  standpoint 0 and 0.749 dB at Tokyo standpoint 15. Both are shadowed standpoints.
- Quadrupling the replicas should divide the standard error by 2. Paired per
  standpoint, the measured median reduction is 1.31 at Mexico City and 1.35 at
  Tokyo, with individual standpoints between 0.24 and 3.07. The sample standard
  deviation keeps growing as seeds are added, which is the signature of a
  heavy-tailed estimator at the shadowed points, not of a converged one.

## Historical two-city paired sampler comparison

Superseded contract: `max_bounces=3` hybrid, 1.6 million rays, Korenmarkt 13
standpoints and Prague 68, each in IID and rotated Fibonacci launch, 16 seeds and
looks 4, 8, 12, 16. Source is the read-only package at
`/home/user/aegis-roofline-results-20260807T015530Z`. Retained here because it is
the only paired sampler evidence in the study. It is not the current production
contract and its per-city numbers are not comparable with the five-city section.

Recomputation from the per-seed shards reproduces the published headline exactly:
route median surplus 1.1215300 dB (Korenmarkt) and 1.1051877 dB (Prague), route
median wbSAR 0.06312971 and 0.01213792 m^2 kg^-1. Direct transfer has zero spread
across all 16 seeds in both cities, confirming it is a deterministic exact sum.

### S1, route transfer profile

Total, direct, specular and diffuse transfer against route distance, with the
unshadowed reference.

- Total transfer spans 1.57 dB along the 13-point Korenmarkt route and 3.41 dB
  along the 68-point Prague route.
- Diffuse sits a median 18.31 dB below direct at Korenmarkt and 22.54 dB below at
  Prague. Specular sits 5.45 and 5.49 dB below direct, nearly the same in both.

### S2, shadowing loss and multipath surplus

Shadow loss from the reference to the direct term, and the specular and diffuse
share of total with the surplus overlaid.

- Median shadow loss is 0.883 dB at Korenmarkt, maximum 1.228 dB. Prague loses a
  median 1.436 dB and a maximum 3.516 dB at standpoint 65.
- Standpoint 65 is also Prague's surplus maximum, 1.399 dB. More blocking of the
  direct term leaves a larger relative multipath share, but the correlation is
  weak: Pearson r of shadow loss against surplus is 0.504 at Korenmarkt and 0.130
  at Prague.
- Specular share is almost identical in the two cities, median 22.06 and 21.95
  percent. Diffuse share is not, 1.12 against 0.43 percent.

### S3, seed convergence

Per-standpoint standard error against look, and the p90 look-to-look shift.

- At look 16 the p90 standard error is 0.00839 dB (Korenmarkt IID) and 0.01051 dB
  (Korenmarkt Fibonacci), 0.004442 and 0.005237 dB at Prague. These reproduce the
  campaign report.
- The 12-to-16 p90 shift is 0.00298 dB IID and 0.00350 dB Fibonacci at Korenmarkt,
  0.00135 and 0.00236 dB at Prague.
- Standard error does not fall monotonically with look in either city. Korenmarkt
  IID goes 0.01841, 0.00988, 0.01116, 0.00839 dB over looks 4, 8, 12, 16.

### S4, paired IID against Fibonacci

ECDF of the absolute per-standpoint difference between the two samplers.

- Total transfer differs by at most 0.0324 dB at Korenmarkt and 0.1795 dB at
  Prague, with p90 0.00954 and 0.00756 dB.
- The difference is carried by the diffuse component. Its maximum is 0.0419 dB at
  Korenmarkt and 0.2117 dB at Prague, with p90 0.0349 and 0.1375 dB, an order of
  magnitude above the total.
- Direct transfer is identical between samplers, maximum difference exactly 0.0 dB
  in both cities.

### S5, fixed-route distributions

Surplus, wbSAR and peak-to-mean ECDFs for both cities and both samplers.

- Korenmarkt surplus runs from 1.013 to 1.512 dB across 13 standpoints. Prague runs
  from 0.644 to 1.399 dB across 68.
- Normalised wbSAR is 0.05326 to 0.06666 m^2 kg^-1 at Korenmarkt and 0.00988 to
  0.02179 at Prague, non-overlapping ranges.
- Peak-to-mean median is 2.193 at Korenmarkt and 1.616 at Prague. The narrow street
  concentrates the body field more than the open square.
- The IID and Fibonacci curves are visually indistinguishable at this scale in
  every panel, which is the point of the figure.

### S6, body endpoint relations

Three of the six retained body endpoints are fixed multiples of the area-mean
`Sab`, so they carry no independent information: absorbed power is 1.8724979 m^2
times the mean, whole-body SAR is 0.025863231 m^2 kg^-1 times the mean, and
arriving power density is 15625 times the raw transfer. Each of the three ratios is
constant to at most 5.6e-16 relative deviation across both cities and all 81
standpoints. Peak `Sab` and the coupling efficiency are the endpoints that add
something.

- Peak-to-mean is a city property more than a standpoint property: median 2.193 at
  Korenmarkt against 1.616 at Prague, with per-city ranges 1.609 to 2.423 and 1.255
  to 2.311.
- Coupling efficiency, area-mean `Sab` divided by arriving power density, has a
  median of 0.1181 at Korenmarkt and 0.1337 at Prague. It correlates negatively
  with surplus at Korenmarkt, r = -0.640, and weakly positively at Prague,
  r = 0.219.
- The body does not absorb the surplus the geometry delivers. The body-absorbed
  surplus exceeds the transfer surplus by a median 0.018 dB in both cities, up to
  0.077 dB at Korenmarkt and 0.094 dB at Prague.
- The mean of per-replica peaks and the peak of the replica-mean field agree to a
  median 0.0 dB and a maximum 0.0038 dB, so the two peak definitions are
  interchangeable in this campaign.

### S7, sampler variance ratio by component

ECDF of `log2` of the Fibonacci-to-IID variance ratio over 16 seeds.

- Rotated Fibonacci does reduce diffuse variance at Korenmarkt: median ratio 0.628,
  below 1 at 77 percent of standpoints.
- It does not carry through to the total. Total variance ratio has median 0.974 at
  Korenmarkt and 1.267 at Prague, with q90 of 15.21 and 51.19 and a maximum of 2169
  at Prague. The distribution is too wide in both tails to justify a sampler change.
- At Prague the diffuse advantage disappears entirely, median ratio 1.153.

## What is not retained, and is therefore absent

- Per-seed shards for the five first-material-interaction campaigns are not in the
  results tree. Seed-level resampling for the current contract is not possible from
  these artefacts, which is why C6 falls back to the hybrid-contract directories.
- A per-replica ensemble-field peak. The campaign records it as unavailable for
  bootstrapping, so no interval is drawn for that metric.
- Separate sampled-suffix transfer in the two-city package. Only acceptance counts
  and a chi diagnostic were persisted, so its numerical impact stays unresolved and
  is not estimated here.
- Per-replica body `Sab` fields at every look in the two-city package. Only the
  cumulative sums at looks 4, 8, 12 and 16 survive, which fixes the mean field but
  not a seed-resampling interval on the peak.
- Corrected stage timing for the two-city package. Those artefacts carry a known
  timing attribution defect and no timing claim is made from them.
