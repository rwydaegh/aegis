Naturally, as discussed with Filip, the draft would need some changes if it were to generalize beyond dosimetry. Filip first raised radar and microchip design, and the analysis I have done since has gone quite a bit wider than that. Below are some technical points and suggestions. Some of these may or may not make sense in patent form, so please treat them as raw material and defer to your judgement on what is worth claiming and how.

1. Genericize the subject matter of the independent claim. The independent claim currently recites electromagnetic absorption in a body, and the dependents hardwire biological tissue and the human body. The same forward computation works for any electrically large, smooth scatterer under any linear wave field. It might be worth having the independent claim recite a generic scatterer and a generic wave-interaction power quantity, with dosimetry (biological tissue, the scalar T0, ICNIRP) kept as a dependent embodiment. That widens applicability toward antennas, radar and other fields without giving up any of the dosimetry protection we already have.

2. Consider anchoring the independent claim on the full inventive combination. The current characterizing feature, a surface integration that reduces the dimensionality of the computation, is close to long-established surface methods such as physical optics, the boundary element method and the method of moments, all of which reduce a volume problem to a surface integral. For a broader and more defensible claim, it may be stronger to recite the actual novel combination in the independent claim: (a) a per-surface-element local plane-wave interaction law, (b) visibility gated by a rectified or smooth projection factor without volumetric ray tracing, (c) computed as an end-to-end differentiable graph, and (d) yielding a closed-form quadratic operator (the "Q" operator) whose eigen-decomposition or QCQP solution gives an optimal excitation or geometry. This is simultaneously broader, since it covers more applications, and sharper, since it recites what is genuinely new rather than the plain surface-integral idea.

3. Consider claiming the companion operators alongside the absorption operator. From the same forward model and surface machinery you can read off several power quantities, and each opens a distinct market:
   - the scattered-power operator (Q_re): radar cross section, radar-signature and low-observable shaping, reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) and reflectarrays, and backscatter sensing.
   - the captured or coupled-power operator (Q_in), and in particular the antenna-to-antenna cross term (Q^(u,v)): installed-antenna performance and co-site coupling, i.e. the S-parameters between antennas on a shared platform. This is the strongest embodiment for licensing to the EM-simulation and EDA vendors. Wireless power transfer sits here too.
   - the energy-partition identity Q_ab + Q_re + Q_mi = I, which may itself be claimable structure since it ties absorbed, reflected and missed power into one conserved decomposition.

4. Consider a generic differentiable inverse-design claim. Independently of the application, the method optimizes a design variable (geometry, source position, antenna pattern, excitation or precoder, or material) against a quadratic power-functional constraint, either by gradients through the differentiable graph or by a closed-form QCQP when the constraint is a quadratic form. A single umbrella claim of this kind would cover the design use cases across dosimetry, antennas and radar in one place.

5. Consider a non-electromagnetic dependent embodiment. The same surface-operator machinery holds for any linear wave at an interface if the electromagnetic refractive index is replaced by an acoustic impedance. That captures focused-ultrasound dose planning and sonar target strength, and it makes the point that the method is wave-agnostic rather than specific to radio. A short dependent claim to that effect would broaden the embodiment list cheaply.

6. A note on defensible novelty and on prior-art exposure, since you will want both. The bare idea of differentiable physical optics or differentiable ray tracing is not clean ground. Differentiable ray tracers exist (Sionna RT) and differentiable rendering exists (Mitsuba), so the broad claims should not lean on "differentiable surface method" as the novel element. The genuinely novel structures to anchor on are: the differentiable functional characteristic modes, meaning the eigenstructure of the Q operator, which contrasts with classical Theory of Characteristic Modes (sold in FEKO and Ansys) that works in current space, is structure-only and is not differentiable; the energy-partition (POVM-like) structure of point 3; and the inverse-geometry, shape-from-scattering result. These are the pieces no incumbent ships. The honest flip side is that broadening will widen the freedom-to-operate search, which would now also touch differentiable-rendering, characteristic-mode-analysis and EDA-vendor literature rather than only the dosimetry and device-SAR portfolios in the IDF. I would value your view on how you want to handle that widened search, since it is a real cost to weigh against the larger market.
