\section{Distal occlusion: the angular-clearance metric}\label{sec:cmetric}

The local gate of \cref{sec:fock-local} smooths the body's own terminator, the
edge where a facet curves into its own shadow. The other half of self-shadowing
is distal: one body part blocks the line of sight to another across a finite gap.
A hand over the cheek, the arm across the torso, the chin over the throat, the gap
between the legs. \cref{sec:geom} wrote this as the binary occlusion factor $O$
(\cref{def:occlusion}), exact as $\lambda\to0$ and discontinuous across the shadow
edge. This section makes it wave-correct with the same Fock transition $g(\xi)$
(\cref{def:fock-g}), now driven by an angular clearance to the occluder edge
rather than the obliquity to the body's own terminator. The clearance is a single
signed scalar field on the direction sphere, the C-metric. The central result is
that the C-metric and the local terminator angle $\theta$ are one object measured
against two boundaries, so distal and local diffraction are the same gate.

\subsection{The signed angular-clearance field}\label{sec:cmetric-def}

Fix a surface point $\rr$. On the unit direction sphere $S^2$, equipped with the
geodesic metric $d_g(\bm{\omega},\bm{\omega}')=\arccos(\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{\omega}')$,
let $B(\rr)\subset S^2$ be the blocked set, the directions along which $\rr$ is
occluded by another part of the body (the open ray $\rr+t\bm{\omega}$, $t>0$, meets
the mesh before escaping). Its boundary $\partial B(\rr)$ is the occlusion horizon
seen from $\rr$, the union of occluder silhouettes, a curve of arbitrary topology.

\begin{definition}[Signed angular clearance, the C-metric]\label{def:c-metric}
The signed angular clearance is the signed geodesic distance on $S^2$ from the
incidence direction $-\khat$ to the occlusion horizon,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-def}
  \clr(\rr,\khat) = s(\rr,\khat)\,
  \inf_{\bm{b}\in\partial B(\rr)}\arccos\!\bigl[(-\khat)\cdot\bm{b}\bigr],
  \qquad
  s = \begin{cases}
    +1 & -\khat\notin B(\rr)\ \text{(source visible)},\\
    -1 & -\khat\in B(\rr)\ \text{(shadowed)}.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
A positive $\clr$ is lit, or clear: the source is directly visible and $\abs{\clr}$
is the angular margin before an occluder edge cuts the line of sight. A negative
$\clr$ is in the geometric shadow of the occluder, $\abs{\clr}$ deep.
\end{definition}

The clearance refines the binary occlusion of \cref{def:occlusion}. Its sign is
$O$, its zero set is the exact occlusion boundary for any number of disjoint
blocked regions, and its magnitude is the clearance the diffractive penumbra
needs,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-to-O}
  O(\rr,\khat) = \ident\!\bigl[\clr(\rr,\khat)>0\bigr]
  = \lim_{\sigma\to0}\half\Bigl[1+\erf\!\bigl(\clr/\sigma\bigr)\Bigr].
\end{equation}
Away from the medial axis (the cut locus of $\partial B$, where two horizon arcs
are equidistant), $\clr$ is a true signed distance and satisfies the eikonal
equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eikonal}
  \abs{\nabla_{\!S^2}\,\clr} = 1 .
\end{equation}
This is a spherical signed-distance function. AEGIS bakes it per triangle on an
octahedral direction grid by a fast-marching eikonal solve with the great-circle
metric, then interpolates it at query time, so the runtime gate is one lookup per
path and the expensive ray casting is paid once per body.

\subsection{One metric, two boundaries}\label{sec:cmetric-unify}

The clearance $\clr$ and the local terminator angle $\theta$ of \cref{eq:theta-mu}
are not analogues. They are the same construction.

\begin{proposition}[Unification of the local and distal metrics]\label{prop:unify}
Both $\theta$ and $\clr$ are the signed geodesic distance on $S^2$ from the
incidence direction $-\khat$ to a shadow boundary. They differ only in the
boundary curve.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Local: the boundary is the terminator great circle
    $T=\{\bm{\omega}\in S^2 : \bm{\omega}\cdot\nhat=0\}$, the locus of grazing
    directions for the facet normal $\nhat$. The signed geodesic distance from
    $-\khat$ to $T$ is
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-as-distance}
      d_g^{\,\pm}(-\khat,T) = \arcsin\!\bigl[(-\khat)\cdot\nhat\bigr]
      = \arcsin\mu = \theta .
    \end{equation}
  \item Distal: the boundary is the occluder silhouette $\partial B(\rr)$, and the
    signed geodesic distance is $\clr(\rr,\khat)$ by \cref{def:c-metric}.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently a single transition $g(\xi)$ gates both, with $\xi$ built from the
relevant clearance, and the only difference is which curvature radius enters the
Fock parameter $\mfock$: the facet's own in-plane radius $\Rfock$
(\cref{prop:fock-radius}) for the local gate, the binding occluder's edge radius
$\Rocc$ for the distal gate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Statement (ii) is \cref{def:c-metric}. For (i), the great circle $T$ has pole
$\nhat$ and is the equator at geodesic distance $\pi/2$ from $\nhat$. A direction
$\bm{\omega}$ lies at geodesic latitude $\phi$ above $T$ with
$\sin\phi=\bm{\omega}\cdot\nhat$, and its geodesic distance to the equator is
$\abs{\pi/2-\arccos(\bm{\omega}\cdot\nhat)}=\abs{\arcsin(\bm{\omega}\cdot\nhat)}$.
Taking $\bm{\omega}=-\khat$ gives $\bm{\omega}\cdot\nhat=\mu$, and the signed
distance, positive toward the lit hemisphere $\mu>0$, is $\arcsin\mu=\theta$ by
\cref{eq:theta-mu}. The two quantities are the same functional of $-\khat$, applied
to two boundaries.
\end{proof}

The unification is exact and has a clean reading. The angular clearance is purely
kinematic, a great-circle distance on the direction sphere. The curvature radius
is a physical property of whichever surface diffracts the ray. The local
terminator is the special case in which the diffracting body is the observation
point's own body and the boundary is its own terminator, which happens to be a
great circle, the geodesic of zero geodesic curvature. The distal case replaces
that great circle by the silhouette of a separate occluder, whose finite edge
curvature sets $\Rocc$. Local self-shadowing and distal cross-shadowing are one
mechanism at two scales.

\subsection{The distal Fock detour}\label{sec:cmetric-detour}

The diffracted ray that reaches $\rr$ past the occluder grazes the occluder
silhouette. Let the binding edge, the point of $\partial B(\rr)$ nearest $-\khat$,
have in-incidence-plane radius of curvature $\Rocc$, obtained from the occluder's
principal curvatures (\cref{eq:euler}, \cref{prop:fock-radius}) at the grazing
point. Write $d_2=\abs{\rr-\bm{r}_{\mathrm{edge}}}$ for the edge-to-point distance
and $d_1$ for the source-to-edge distance, so the diffracted path is source
$\,\xrightarrow{d_1}\,$ edge $\,\xrightarrow{d_2}\,\rr$.

\begin{proposition}[Distal detour and gate]\label{prop:distal-xi}
The distal detour parameter is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-distal}
  \xi_{\mathrm d} = \frac{\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\,\clr}{w_{\mathrm{nf}}},
  \qquad
  \mfock_{\mathrm{occ}} = \Bigl(\tfrac{k\Rocc}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3},
\end{equation}
with $w_{\mathrm{nf}}$ the near-field wavefront factor of \cref{sec:cmetric-nf}
($w_{\mathrm{nf}}=1$ in the far field, so $\xi_{\mathrm d}=\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\clr$).
It feeds the same transition $g(\xi_{\mathrm d})$ as the local gate
(\cref{def:fock-g}). The distal gate attenuates only the would-be-lit response,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distal-gate}
  G_{\mathrm d}(\rr,\khat) =
  w_s\,\abs{g_{\text{soft}}(\xi_{\mathrm d})}^2
  + w_p\,\abs{g_{\text{hard}}(\xi_{\mathrm d})}^2,
  \qquad
  g(\rr,\khat) = \begin{cases}
    g_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu)\,G_{\mathrm d} & \mu>0,\\[2pt]
    g_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu) & \mu\le0,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $g_{\mathrm{loc}}$ the local Fock gate of \cref{eq:fock-local} and $w_s,w_p$
the TE/TM power weights of \cref{sec:pol}. The absorbed power density is the
path sum $\Sab(\rr)=\sum_n\Sinc^{(n)}T_0\,g(\rr,\khat_n)$, the wave-correct form
of \cref{eq:Sab-occluded}. The gate has the limits
\begin{enumerate}
  \item clear line of sight, $\clr\to+\infty$: $\xi_{\mathrm d}\to+\infty$,
    $g_{\text{soft}},g_{\text{hard}}\to1$, so $G_{\mathrm d}\to1$ and no
    attenuation;
  \item deep distal shadow, $\clr\to-\infty$: $\xi_{\mathrm d}\to-\infty$,
    $\abs{G_{\mathrm d}}\sim e^{-\sqrt3\,q_1\abs{\xi_{\mathrm d}}}$, the creeping
    leakage past the occluder;
  \item geometric-optics, $\lambda\to0$: $\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\to\infty$, so for
    any $\clr\neq0$, $\xi_{\mathrm d}\to\pm\infty$ and
    $G_{\mathrm d}\to\ident[\clr>0]=O(\rr,\khat)$. The distal gate degrades
    gracefully to the binary occlusion of \cref{def:occlusion}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Justification of the transferred machinery]
Fock's boundary-layer analysis of the shadow boundary is local. Near the grazing
point the occluder is, to leading order, a smooth convex body of in-plane radius
$\Rocc$, and the diffracted field depends only on the wavenumber $k$, the
grazing-point radius, and the surface impedance. It does not depend on whether the
diffracting body carries the observation point (local) or is a separate occluder
(distal). The uniform composite $g=\Philit+\Psishad$ (\cref{eq:fock-g}), the
creeping eigenvalues $q_p$ (PEC, \cref{eq:q-soft,eq:q-hard}, or impedance-shifted,
\cref{eq:leontovich}), the soft/hard split, and the $\sqrt3\,q_p$ decay law
therefore transfer verbatim under $\Rfock\to\Rocc$ and $\theta\to\clr$. Limit (i)
and (ii) are \cref{prop:fock-limits} applied at $\xi_{\mathrm d}$. For (iii),
$\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\propto k^{1/3}\to\infty$ drives $\xi_{\mathrm d}$ to
$\pm\infty$ off the boundary, where $\Philit\to\ident[\clr>0]$ and $\Psishad\to0$,
recovering \cref{eq:c-to-O}. The construction is valid when the occluder edge is
smooth and convex on the boundary-layer scale: $\Rocc\gg\lambda$, with the edge
radius varying slowly across the penumbra width $(k\Rocc)^{-1/3}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sign of the detour]\label{rem:distal-sign}
The clearance and the detour share their sign. A clear line of sight ($\clr>0$)
maps to the lit branch ($\xi_{\mathrm d}>0$), matching the convention of
\cref{sec:fock-local} ($\xi>0$ lit) with no inversion, so $G_{\mathrm d}\to1$ in
the clear as it must. An implementation that stores a positive shadow depth
$s_{\mathrm{sh}}=-\clr\ge0$ writes the same quantity as
$\xi_{\mathrm d}=-\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\,s_{\mathrm{sh}}/w_{\mathrm{nf}}$, the
negative branch into shadow. The leading minus is a property of the shadow-depth
argument, not of the signed clearance.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The knife-edge limit and the two penumbra widths}\label{sec:cmetric-knife}

Two distinct mechanisms set the distal penumbra, and the lit transition $\Philit$
of \cref{eq:phi-lit} is common to both. They are not two limits of one formula.

\begin{proposition}[Knife-edge reduction and width scalings]\label{prop:knife}
Surface curvature and path detour each smooth the shadow boundary.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Curvature, Fock.} A smooth convex occluder of finite $\Rocc$ gives,
    from $\xi_{\mathrm d}=\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\clr$ through
    $\Philit(\xi_{\mathrm d})=\half[1+\erf(\clr/\sigma_{\mathrm{F}})]$, the
    curvature width
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-fock}
      \sigma_{\mathrm{F}} = \frac{\sqrt2}{\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}}
      = \sqrt2\,\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{k\Rocc}\Bigr)^{1/3}
      = 2^{5/6}\,(k\Rocc)^{-1/3}
      \ \propto\ (k\Rocc)^{-1/3},
    \end{equation}
    plus the exponential creeping shadow tail of $\Psishad$.
  \item \emph{Path detour, knife edge.} A sharp or flat-faced occluder edge
    ($\Rocc\to\infty$, no surface to launch a creeping wave) diffracts by the
    Fresnel-zone path detour alone. The transition reduces to the bare
    error-function gate
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:knife-gate}
      g \longrightarrow V(\clr) = \half\Bigl[1+\erf\!\bigl(\clr/\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}}\bigr)\Bigr],
      \qquad
      \sigma_{\mathrm{ke}} = K\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\,d_1}{d_2\,(d_1+d_2)}}
      \ \propto\ (k\,d_2)^{-1/2}\ \text{(far field)},
    \end{equation}
    with no creeping tail. $K$ is a modeling constant ($K=1/\sqrt2$ is
    knife-edge-exact at the boundary).
\end{enumerate}
The crossover is at $\sigma_{\mathrm{F}}\sim\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}}$, that is at an
edge radius $\Rocc^\star\sim \ell\,\sqrt{k\ell}$ (order of magnitude, leading
constant near unity), with $\ell=d_2(d_1+d_2)/d_1$ the reduced Fresnel distance
($\ell\to d_2$ in the far field). For $\Rocc\ll\Rocc^\star$ curvature dominates
(smooth limb occluder, the Fock regime). For $\Rocc\gg\Rocc^\star$ the Fresnel
zone dominates (sharp edge, flat panel, or distant gently curved occluder, the
knife regime). At 28\,GHz with a hand-to-cheek standoff of a few centimetres,
$\Rocc^\star\approx 10$\,cm, so a finger or limb ($\Rocc\sim1$\,cm) is firmly Fock
and a flat wall edge is knife. The physical penumbra follows the broader
mechanism, $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}\sim\max(\sigma_{\mathrm{F}},\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}})$.
A smooth quadrature blend is a modeling refinement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part (i) is the identification of the $\erf$ argument: writing
$\Philit(\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\clr)=\half[1+\erf(\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\clr/\sqrt2)]
=\half[1+\erf(\clr/\sigma_{\mathrm{F}})]$ gives
$\sigma_{\mathrm{F}}=\sqrt2/\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}$, and substituting
$\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}=(k\Rocc/2)^{1/3}$ yields \cref{eq:sigma-fock}. For part (ii),
the diffracted ray with transverse miss-distance $h$ from the edge has extra path
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detour-path}
  \delta(h) = \sqrt{d_1^2+h^2}+\sqrt{d_2^2+h^2}-(d_1+d_2)
  \approx \frac{h^2}{2}\Bigl(\frac1{d_1}+\frac1{d_2}\Bigr),
  \qquad h\ll d_1,d_2,
\end{equation}
so the Fresnel integral carries phase $e^{\mathrm{i}k\delta}=e^{\mathrm{i}(\pi/2)\nu^2}$
with the Fresnel parameter
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresnel-nu}
  \nu = h\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}\Bigl(\frac1{d_1}+\frac1{d_2}\Bigr)}.
\end{equation}
Its magnitude transitions from $1$ (lit) to $0$ (shadow) over $\nu\sim O(1)$, with
the half-value at $\nu=0$ ($h=0$), the geometric shadow boundary. The
real-argument error function is the monotone surrogate for this complex Fresnel
integral: they share the leading penumbra profile and the boundary value, and
differ only in the lit-side Cornu ripples and the algebraic shadow tail, both of
which incoherent path summation and 4\,cm$^2$ spatial averaging remove. Mapping
the clearance to the miss-distance by $h=d_2\sin\clr\approx d_2\clr$ and setting
$V=\half[1+\erf(\clr/\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}})]$ gives
$\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}}=K\sqrt{\lambda\,d_1/[d_2(d_1+d_2)]}$ with $K=1/\sqrt2$, which
is \cref{eq:knife-gate} and reproduces the validated self-shadowing gate. The
crossover follows from equating \cref{eq:sigma-fock,eq:knife-gate} with
$\lambda=2\pi/k$: $(k\Rocc)^{-1/3}\sim(k\ell)^{-1/2}$ gives
$k\Rocc\sim(k\ell)^{3/2}$, hence $\Rocc^\star\sim k^{1/2}\ell^{3/2}=\ell\sqrt{k\ell}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the knife edge is a separate mechanism]\label{rem:not-a-limit}
Taking $\Rocc\to\infty$ in the Fock formula alone is not the knife edge. The
curvature width $\sigma_{\mathrm{F}}\propto(k\Rocc)^{-1/3}\to0$ and the creeping
decay rate $\sqrt3\,q_p\,\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\to\infty$, so the Fock gate collapses
to a step. A flat occluder still casts a finite penumbra because the Fresnel path
detour of \cref{eq:detour-path} does not vanish: its width $\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}}$
is set by the propagation distances, not by the surface curvature. The Fock
variable $\xi_{\mathrm d}=\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\clr$ does not contain this
mechanism. The gate must therefore carry both widths, and the sharp-edge limit
recovers the Fresnel transition $V(\clr)$, not a step. This is the precise sense in
which the old $\erf$ self-shadowing gate is the knife-edge limit of the Fock distal
gate: the lit core $\Philit$ survives with the Fresnel width, and the creeping tail
drops out.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Boundary value: $-3$ versus $-6$\,dB]\label{rem:boundary-db}
At $\clr=0$ the unsquared gate $V(0)=\half$ acts on power as $-3$\,dB. The rigorous
knife-edge \emph{field} is $\half$, hence $\tfrac14$ power, $-6$\,dB, recovered by
squaring the gate. The Fock gate pins $\abs{g(0)}^2$ to the exact oracle terminator
value $\approx0.488$ (\cref{sec:fock-g}), close to $-3$\,dB because the creeping
wave adds constructively above the knife-edge half value. AEGIS defaults to the
$-3$\,dB convention for continuity with the local gate and offers the squared,
$-6$\,dB form as a documented option. The difference is confined to the penumbra
and is sub-decibel after incoherent summation and 4\,cm$^2$ averaging.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The near-field wavefront factor}\label{sec:cmetric-nf}

The far-field gate assumes a plane incident wave. A source at finite range $d_1$
from the binding edge presents a spherical wavefront, which tightens the Fresnel
zone and sharpens the penumbra. Factor the knife-edge width of
\cref{eq:knife-gate} into its plane-wave value and a wavefront correction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wnf}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{ke}}
  = \underbrace{K\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{d_2}}}_{\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}}^{\,\infty}}
    \;\cdot\;
    \underbrace{\sqrt{\frac{d_1}{d_1+d_2}}}_{w_{\mathrm{nf}}},
  \qquad
  w_{\mathrm{nf}} = \sqrt{\frac{d_1}{d_1+d_2}}\ \in(0,1].
\end{equation}
This is the spherical-wave Fresnel zone read off \cref{eq:fresnel-nu}: the plane
wave ($d_1\to\infty$) gives $w_{\mathrm{nf}}\to1$ and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}}^{\,\infty}=K\sqrt{\lambda/d_2}$, while a finite $d_1$ replaces
the observation distance $d_2$ by the reduced distance $d_1d_2/(d_1+d_2)$ in the
Fresnel parameter. The factor is the square root of the ratio of the two,
$w_{\mathrm{nf}}=\sqrt{[d_1d_2/(d_1+d_2)]/d_2}$. As the source approaches the edge
the penumbra narrows: a point source pressed against the edge casts a sharp
shadow, smeared only by $\sqrt{\lambda d_1}\to0$ at the edge plane.

The same wavefront curvature enters the Fock detour, so $w_{\mathrm{nf}}$
multiplies the curvature width as well, $\sigma_{\mathrm{F}}\to w_{\mathrm{nf}}\,
\sigma_{\mathrm{F}}$, which is the division in \cref{eq:xi-distal}, since
$\xi_{\mathrm d}\propto\clr/\sigma_{\mathrm{F}}$. The same construction governs the
near-field local gate of \cref{sec:fock-local}, where the binding edge is the
body's own terminator, $d_2=\Rfock\abs{\theta}$ is the geodesic arc from terminator
to field point, and $d_1$ is the source-to-grazing-point distance
(\cref{sec:nearfar}).

\begin{remark}[Placement of the wavefront factor]\label{rem:wnf-placement}
The wavefront factor belongs on the width, not on the detour. Because
$w_{\mathrm{nf}}\le1$ and $\xi_{\mathrm d}\propto\clr/\sigma$, a near-field source
\emph{raises} $\xi_{\mathrm d}$, which sharpens the penumbra, matching the knife-edge
$\sigma_{\mathrm{ke}}\propto w_{\mathrm{nf}}$ derived independently above. Writing
the detour as $\xi_{\mathrm d}=\mfock_{\mathrm{occ}}\,\clr\,w_{\mathrm{nf}}$, with
$w_{\mathrm{nf}}$ as a multiplier, would invert the near-field trend and broaden the
penumbra as the source approaches, contradicting the Fresnel-zone result. The
division in \cref{eq:xi-distal} is the form consistent with both.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Multi-edge shadow and the deep-shadow amplitude}\label{sec:cmetric-validity}

\begin{remark}[Domain of validity of the distal gate]\label{rem:distal-validity}
The distal gate is built on a single binding edge and the three-pole creeping
composite. Three regimes bound its validity.

\emph{Single binding edge.} The C-metric stores the signed distance to the nearest
horizon point. In the lit penumbra a direction is shadowed if any edge blocks it,
so visibility is the product (AND) of per-edge gates, dominated by the nearest,
binding edge. The single-edge gate is then exact at leading order, and overlapping
penumbrae from a second edge are a second-order correction.

\emph{Deep-shadow additivity.} In deep shadow each edge leaks diffracted power and
the powers add. Two edges around a narrow gap leak more than one. The single
binding edge takes only the nearest, so it under-predicts deep-shadow leakage, the
same defect as the error-function tail standing in for the algebraic Fresnel tail
or the multi-pole creeping ladder.

\emph{Amplitude caveat.} The three-pole composite of \cref{def:fock-g}, with one
prefactor pinned to the terminator value, under-predicts the deep-shadow creeping
amplitude by a factor of $3$ to $4$ (\cref{rem:deep-shadow}). For the local gate
this is dose-negligible: the deep shadow carries about $10^{-6}$ of the lit value,
so the absolute error is order $4\times10^{-6}$ of lit. For the distal gate it is
not automatically negligible. A hand fully shadowing the cheek puts the
observation point deep inside one occluder's shadow, where the cross-body shadow
can carry real dose, and a factor of $3$ to $4$ there is a factor of $3$ to $4$ on
a dose that matters. The remedy is to treat the deep-shadow amplitude separately:
more creeping poles, a deep-shadow anchor that decouples the tail amplitude from
the $C^1$ terminator match, or the tabulated Pekeris-Logan Fock function. Until
then the distal gate is quantitative in the lit region and the penumbra, where the
C-metric and the single binding edge are exact to leading order, and a documented
lower bound on deep cross-body shadow dose.
\end{remark}
