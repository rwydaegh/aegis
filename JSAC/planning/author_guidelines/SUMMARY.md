# JSAC author guidelines — concrete cheatsheet

For the Digital Twins SI (submission 1 May 2026, ScholarOne).

## Page budget

| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| Initial submission (double-column, 10pt, 1" margins) | **13 pages max** (hard cap — "significantly exceeding" = desk reject) |
| Revised submission | **16 pages max** |
| Overlength charge kicks in at | **page 9** ($220/page, mandatory, non-negotiable) |
| "Maximum allowable page length" for accepted papers | set later by guest editors, depends on how many papers they accept. Separate from the $220 charge — you pay overlength even if you're under the max. |

Everything counts toward the 13 pages: title, author block, abstract, keywords, body, equations, figures, tables, appendices, references. Bios/photos only required after acceptance, not at submission.

Length estimation rule IEEE uses:
- 3 single-column manuscript pages ≈ 1 typeset page
- 6 figures/tables ≈ 1 typeset page

You must submit **both** formats:
- Single-column: 12pt, ~26 lines/page, 6.5" line, ≤30 double-spaced pages (includes figs/tables)
- Double-column IEEEtran 10pt (the one that determines actual typeset length)

IEEEtran style does **not** allow equations extending past the column width in the published version. If you have wide equations, relocate them yourself or they'll get moved to the top of the next page by staff.

## Required structure (in this order)

1. Title
2. Authors + affiliations + contact
3. Manuscript received/revised dates
4. Financial support acknowledgement (if any)
5. Abstract — **75–200 words** (100–200 for final). No equations, no unusual symbols.
6. Keywords — up to 5, from the IEEE keyword list (email `keywords@ieee.org` for current list)
7. Introduction (keep it short — "introductory discussion should be kept to a minimum")
8. Body with figures placed **in-line where they belong**, not at the end
9. Conclusion
10. Acknowledgment (optional)
11. References
12. Author bios + photos (only after acceptance; photo ≤ 9.5 cm across widest part of head)

Appendices strongly recommended for proofs, derivations, calculations, anything not essential to the narrative. Moving this material out of the main text is explicitly encouraged twice in the guidelines.

## Hard rules (get you rejected without review)

- Must be submitted to a specific Call-for-Papers. "At large" = auto-reject.
- **Max 3 submissions per author per special issue.** Over this limit = returned without review.
- No prior publication. Exceptions: limited-distribution prior work, abstract-only, conference/digest papers. Any conference paper overlap **must** be declared; substantial revision required.
- Double-submission or plagiarism → rejection + **6-month ban** from all ComSoc publications.
- English only. If not a native speaker, get a native-fluent colleague or use SPI Publisher Services before submitting.

## Acceptance rate

**~31–35%** (31% in 2023). Each paper gets ≥3 independent reviews from different organizations. Review window = 2 months.

## What reviewers are told to evaluate (tune your paper to these)

The guidelines explicitly name three selection criteria:

1. **Significance** — why the JSAC readership should care
2. **Novelty** — what's new vs. prior art
3. **Usefulness** — what someone can do with this

Reviewers are instructed to write detailed strength/weakness analysis with recommendations for improvement regardless of accept/reject. A "brief review with vague comments is of no value." This means reviewers are primed to look for specific, concrete contributions. Make yours visible and unambiguous.

"A small issue containing excellent papers is preferable to a large issue padded with mediocre papers" — guest editors are explicitly biased toward rejecting the borderline. Don't be borderline.

## What to optimize to maximize acceptance odds

Deducible from the guidelines, not fluff:

- **Write a sharp abstract.** It's the first filter. 75–200 words, scope + contributions, no equations. Will be converted to HTML.
- **Keep the intro short.** Explicitly called out. Reviewers hate long motivations.
- **Don't paraphrase prior work — cite it.** Repeated in the guidelines. Burning pages on background is a common rejection reason.
- **Clarity over sophistication.** "Excessively complex or poorly written presentation can only obscure the significance of the work."
- **Logical progression.** Reviewers are told to look at organization.
- **Push math into appendices** unless it's load-bearing for the narrative. Keeps main text readable.
- **Figure discipline.** "Used sparingly. Repetitive patterns or results omitted." Each figure costs ~1/6 of a typeset page.
- **Declare conference overlap explicitly and substantively revise.** Reviewers check. Undeclared overlap = misconduct.
- **Fit the CFP scope.** Off-scope = rejected by guest editors regardless of quality. For this SI: closed-loop DTN optimization and/or application-aware DT design — frame your contribution as one of their listed bullets.
- **Target ≤8 typeset pages** if you want to avoid the page charge entirely. Otherwise budget for $220 × (pages beyond 8).

## AI policy

IEEE policy in PSPB Operations Manual §8.2.1, subsection B article 10 (authors) and subsection C article 6 (reviewers). Disclose AI use; don't hide it.

## Things that are NOT in the guidelines (don't assume)

- No specified reference count limit.
- No specified figure count limit (just "sparingly").
- No explicit rule against self-citation beyond the misconduct policy.
- No double-blind — JSAC is single-blind (author identities visible to reviewers).
