\section{Tissue electromagnetics}\label{sec:tissue}

The material factor $T_0$ in the master law (\cref{prop:master-law}) is
determined entirely by the complex refractive index $\ntilde$ of skin at the
operating frequency. This section fixes $\ntilde$ from a dispersive
permittivity model and records the numbers used throughout the report.

\subsection{Dispersive permittivity}\label{sec:permittivity}

Biological tissue is a lossy dielectric whose response is frequency dependent.
AEGIS uses the four-pole Cole--Cole model of Gabriel~\textit{et al.}, as
tabulated in the IT'IS v5.0 database. The complex relative permittivity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cole-cole}
  \epstilde(\omega) \;=\; \varepsilon_\infty
  \;+\; \sum_{m=1}^{4}\frac{\Delta\varepsilon_m}
       {1+(\mathrm{i}\,\omega\,\tau_m)^{\,1-\alpha_m}}
  \;-\; \mathrm{i}\,\frac{\sigma_s}{\omega\,\varepsilon_0},
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_\infty$ the high-frequency permittivity, $(\Delta\varepsilon_m,
\tau_m,\alpha_m)$ the strength, relaxation time, and broadening of the $m$th
dispersion pole, and $\sigma_s$ the static ionic conductivity. The single-pole
Debye model is the special case of one pole with $\alpha=0$. The sign of the
final term follows the $e^{+\mathrm{i}\omega t}$ convention of
\cref{rem:convention}.

For dosimetry it is convenient to collapse \cref{eq:cole-cole} into an effective
permittivity and conductivity at the working frequency,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps-eff}
  \epstilde \;=\; \varepsilon_r \;-\; \mathrm{i}\,\frac{\sigma}{\omega\varepsilon_0},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_r = \RE(\epstilde), \quad
  \sigma = -\,\omega\varepsilon_0\,\IM(\epstilde),
\end{equation}
so that $\varepsilon_r$ absorbs the real dispersive part and $\sigma$ collects
both the ionic and relaxation losses.

\subsection{Refractive index and penetration depth}\label{sec:n-and-delta}

The complex refractive index follows from $\ntilde=\sqrt{\epstilde}$ with the
branch of \cref{rem:convention}. Writing $\ntilde=n-\mathrm{i}\kappa$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n-kappa}
  n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\,\abs{\epstilde}+\varepsilon_r\,},
  \qquad
  \kappa = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\,\abs{\epstilde}-\varepsilon_r\,}.
\end{equation}
The real part $n$ sets the phase velocity, the extinction $\kappa$ sets the
attenuation. A wave normally incident on the half-space decays in field
amplitude as $e^{-\kappa k_0 z}$ with $k_0=\omega/c$, so the power penetration
depth (where intensity falls to $1/e$) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penetration}
  \delta \;=\; \frac{1}{2\kappa k_0}
  \;=\; \frac{c}{2\kappa\,\omega}
  \;=\; \frac{\lambda_0}{4\pi\kappa},
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_0=2\pi/k_0$ the free-space wavelength. At millimetre-wave
frequencies $\delta$ is a fraction of a millimetre, which validates the surface
absorption picture of \cref{sec:power-balance}: all transmitted power is
deposited in a thin layer far smaller than any body curvature radius.

\subsection{Normal-incidence transmission}\label{sec:T0-from-n}

The material factor of the master law is the normal-incidence power
transmission. Setting $\mu=1$ and $\xi=\ntilde$ in the Fresnel coefficients of
\cref{sec:fresnel} gives

\begin{proposition}[Normal-incidence transmission]\label{prop:T0}
For a free-space to tissue interface with $\ntilde=n-\mathrm{i}\kappa$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T0}
  T_0 \;=\; 1 - \abs*{\frac{1-\ntilde}{1+\ntilde}}^2
  \;=\; \frac{4\,\RE(\ntilde)}{\abs{1+\ntilde}^2}
  \;=\; \frac{4n}{(1+n)^2+\kappa^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Skin at 28~GHz and across the band}\label{sec:skin-numbers}

The reference material throughout this report is human skin. At
\SI{28}{\giga\hertz} the IT'IS Cole--Cole model gives $\varepsilon_r=16.55$ and
$\sigma=\SI{25.8}{\siemens\per\metre}$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:skin-28}
  \ntilde \approx 4.49 - 1.79\,\mathrm{i},
  \qquad
  \eta \equiv \frac{1}{\ntilde} \approx 0.19 + 0.08\,\mathrm{i},
  \qquad
  T_0 \approx 0.536,
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ is the (normalised) wave impedance of the tissue relative to free
space. \Cref{tab:skin-band} lists the dielectric data and derived optical
quantities across the millimetre-wave band. As frequency rises the permittivity
falls and the loss tangent approaches unity, so $\abs{\ntilde}$ decreases and
$T_0$ increases monotonically.
