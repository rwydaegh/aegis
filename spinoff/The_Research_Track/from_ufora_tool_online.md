Research problem
It all started with a research problem that you tried to solve. Describe the academic, technological or societal problem or challenge you are trying to solve with your research in two or three sentences. Include the research domain as briefly as possible and in layman's terms and include the type of research you do (fundamental, applied or contract research).

Context
The context of your research describes the bigger picture in which your research may take part. It might be a part of a (European) project that is executed by a multidisciplinary team, or you might be the only one in your research group working on this or many other situations.

Your research results
Now we head over to the research itself and the work you did. Conclude your research in two to three sentences: what is the essence of your research findings? This is probably the biggest challenge of this exercise. However, remember what Leonardo Da Vinci said: "Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication!".

Also state the phase your research is in: did you just start or are you at the end of your research? Is it still theoretical or have you tested aspects in real-life as well? How close to market are you?

Output & ownership
List the outputs of your research. What are the tangible results that come out of it? There can be a wide variety in these outputs, depending of the type of research you are doing and there can be multiple outputs as well. These outputs will help you to identify the intellectual property and possible protection mechanisms that can be used.

Example outputs are: an algorithm, database, model, procedure, software, hardware, knowledge, expertise, literature, material, book, game, survey results, brand, design, ...

For each output you should mention who is the owner. The owner could be the project partners, you, Ghent University, ...If you don't know you can put a question mark next to it.

Differentiators
Complete this section after you have finished 'Your research results' and 'Alternatives & competition'.

Take a look at both sections and define what makes your research results and outputs different from what is already existing. What benefits can your solution offer compared to some (or all) of the alternatives & competition. Why is important or beneficial to offer these features or benefits?

Alternatives and competition
As a scientist you are well aware about other research in your domain. Research teams of other universities or countries may be trying to solve the same or a similar research problem using different approaches. Don't only compare your solution with these other technologies in development, but also look at the current solutions that are available on the market or the (sometimes crappy) alternatives that are in use today.

Keep an open eye for other solutions and technologies that solve the same problem but in a totally different way.

In this section you focus on alternatives and competition that is already on the market at this moment.

Upcoming alternatives and competition
In this section you focus on alternatives and competition that is not on the market yet, but could be soon. The unit of 'soon' might be different depending on your research domain. For some technologies we talk about months or one or two years, for other technologies this might be 5 or 10 years.

About you
Research driven innovation is teamwork so you will need to tell other people a bit more about yourself. This can be a step to new insights.

Where do you come from? What is your background, your education and what are your dreams and ambitions? What are your hobbies and interests? Do you want to become a top scientist in the academic world? Is it your ambition to become the CTO of a high tech company or do you plan to start your own company one day
