\section{Polarisation-aware absorption}\label{sec:pol}

The unpolarised transmission $\Tavg$ of \cref{sec:fresnel} discards the
orientation of the incident field. At oblique incidence the TE and TM
transmissions differ by $\Delta T = T_p-T_s$, and a fully polarised wave samples
this splitting according to how its field aligns with the local plane of
incidence. This section derives the exact polarisation-aware transmission
$\Teff$, its decomposition into baseline and correction, and the limits that
collapse it back to $\Tavg$. The TM-excess structure derived here also fixes the
soft and hard polarisations used by the diffraction gate of \cref{sec:fock-local}.

\subsection{Local TE/TM decomposition}\label{sec:te-tm}

A plane wave with propagation direction $\khat$ carries a transverse electric
field $\EE_0$. At a surface point $\rr$ the plane of incidence is spanned by
$\khat$ and $\nhat(\rr)$, and defines a local orthonormal TE/TM basis in the
plane perpendicular to $\khat$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:es-ep}
  \hat{e}_s(\rr) = \frac{\khat\times\nhat}{\norm{\khat\times\nhat}},
  \qquad
  \hat{e}_p(\rr) = \hat{e}_s\times\khat.
\end{equation}
The field decomposes as $\EE_0 = a_s\,\hat{e}_s + a_p\,\hat{e}_p$ with
$a_s,a_p\in\Complex$, and the local power fractions are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ws-wp}
  w_s(\rr) = \frac{\abs{a_s}^2}{\norm{\EE_0}^2}
  = \abs{\hat{e}_s\cdot\hat{\EE}_0}^2,
  \qquad
  w_p(\rr) = \frac{\abs{a_p}^2}{\norm{\EE_0}^2}
  = \abs{\hat{e}_p\cdot\hat{\EE}_0}^2,
  \qquad w_s+w_p=1,
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\EE}_0=\EE_0/\norm{\EE_0}$. The fractions $w_s$ and $w_p$ are the
energy weights of the wave in the local TE and TM channels.

\subsection{Effective transmission}\label{sec:Teff}

Each channel transmits with its own coefficient, so the effective transmission
is the weighted sum.

\begin{definition}[Effective transmission]\label{def:Teff}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Teff}
  \Teff(\rr) \;=\; w_s(\rr)\,T_s(\theta) \;+\; w_p(\rr)\,T_p(\theta).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\noindent
Substituting \cref{eq:Teff} into the master law gives the exact
polarisation-resolved absorbed power density of \cref{eq:master-law-exact},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sab-pol}
  \Sab(\rr) \;=\; \Sinc\;\Teff(\rr)\;\ReLU\!\bigl[\mu(\rr)\bigr]\;O(\rr,\khat),
\end{equation}
which holds for any polarisation, any frequency, and any geometry locally flat
on the scale of the wavelength.

\subsection{Baseline plus correction}\label{sec:decomp}

Writing the weights through the TM excess $q\equiv w_p-w_s\in[-1,1]$ separates
the unpolarised baseline from the polarisation correction.

\begin{proposition}[Polarisation decomposition]\label{prop:decomp}
With $\Tavg=\half(T_s+T_p)$, $\Delta T=T_p-T_s$, and $q=w_p-w_s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Teff-decomp}
  \Teff(\rr) \;=\; \Tavg(\theta) \;+\; \frac{q(\rr)}{2}\,\Delta T(\theta).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The first term is the polarisation-independent baseline of \cref{def:Tavg},
depending only on the incidence angle. The second is the polarisation
correction, the product of the wave-and-geometry factor $q$ and the
tissue-and-angle factor $\Delta T$. This is exactly the
$\Tavg+\tfrac{q}{2}\Delta T$ form the incoherent kernels use, with the code
computing $q=w_p-w_s$ from the field and surface geometry.

\subsection{TM excess near grazing}\label{sec:tm-excess}

The correction is largest where both factors are large. The geometric factor
admits the closed form (with ellipticity angle $\chi$, ellipse orientation
$\psi_0$, and local-basis rotation $\alpha(\rr)$)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-exact}
  q(\rr) \;=\; -\cos(2\chi)\,\cos\!\bigl(2\alpha(\rr)-2\psi_0\bigr),
\end{equation}
so $q$ reaches $\pm1$ for linear polarisation aligned with or perpendicular to
the plane of incidence. The tissue factor $\Delta T(\theta)$ grows steeply
toward grazing incidence, reaching $\approx0.83$ at $\theta=75^\circ$ for skin
at \SI{28}{\giga\hertz}, driven by the pseudo-Brewster rise of $T_p$ against the
monotone fall of $T_s$. Where a TM-dominant field ($q\to+1$) strikes the surface
near grazing, $\Teff$ exceeds $\Tavg$ by up to $\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta T\approx0.4$,
a polarisation enhancement of local heating that the unpolarised model misses.

\subsection{Unpolarised and circular limits}\label{sec:pol-limits}

The correction vanishes when any one of three conditions holds, which recovers
the scalar law of \cref{sec:fresnel}.

\begin{corollary}[Collapse to the baseline]\label{cor:collapse}
$\Teff=\Tavg$ pointwise when either the illumination is circularly polarised
($\cos2\chi=0$, hence $q=0$ everywhere) or the incidence is normal
($\Delta T(0)=0$). For genuinely unpolarised illumination, described by the
isotropic coherency matrix $\mathbf{J}=\half\Sinc\,\ident_2$, the ensemble
average gives $\langle q\rangle=0$ and $\langle\Teff\rangle=\Tavg$ at every
surface point.
\end{corollary}

The third condition is azimuthal averaging: if $\alpha(\rr)$ sweeps uniformly
over $2\pi$ across the illuminated surface, the cosine in \cref{eq:q-exact}
averages to zero and the body-integrated correction cancels even when it is
locally nonzero.

\subsection{Soft and hard polarisations}\label{sec:soft-hard}

The TE/TM split also labels the two boundary-condition classes that the
diffraction gate of \cref{sec:fock-local} treats differently. The TE (s)
component sees a soft (Dirichlet-like) boundary and the TM (p) component a hard
(Neumann-like) boundary, and the two diffract with different attenuation around
a curved shadow boundary. The decomposition of \cref{prop:decomp} therefore
feeds forward twice: the power weights $w_s,w_p$ scale the transmission here, and
the same TE/TM labelling selects the soft or hard Fock coefficient in the
diffraction sections.
