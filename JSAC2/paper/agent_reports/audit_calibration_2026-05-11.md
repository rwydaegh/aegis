## A. The figure cited as the calibration-recovery evidence is from a
   different problem

`SI S5 "Calibration recovery"` displays
`figures/residual_vs_snr.pdf` and writes:

> Recovery vs.\ SNR sweep on the plaza geometry. Fig. S? reports the
> post-calibration residual rho = ||hat_beta - gamma||/||gamma|| as a
> function of UL pilot SNR for path-set sizes N in {20, 40, 60} on the
> plaza geometry of the predecessor manuscript ... 200 Monte Carlo
> trials per (SNR, N) cell.

The PDF was generated by
`/home/user/aegis/JSAC/code/experiments/csi_calibration/run_calibration.py`
in the predecessor JSAC manuscript. Its README explicitly states
(lines 89-96):

> The paper writes the calibration as `beta_k <- argmin ||h^meas - beta_k^T
> hat_h_k||^2` ... In this brief there is no body translation Phi(t) or
> Jacobian J — calibration acts on alpha only ... the design matrix
> becomes `A . diag(alpha^insilico)`.

That is, the figure measures BS-side per-path amplitude calibration
(`beta` = `delta_beta` in the JSAC2 notation) and has no body component
at all. The N axis is the BS-side path-set size; the
"plain-LS-fails-at-N-> M_ant" story is about a steering matrix becoming
square. None of this corresponds to the body-side SVD-truncated
calibration of `gammab` that JSAC2 Sec V introduces.

The SI text however introduces the figure under the "Calibration
recovery" heading whose previous paragraphs ("Tier-D scalar
pre-conditioning" and "Tier-B operator-SVD basis") describe the body-side
modal fit. The reader is invited to interpret the figure as evidence for
the body-side claim. It is not.

Concretely:
- `||hat_beta - gamma||` mixes a per-path BS-side correction with a
  body-side modal coefficient. In the predecessor's setting both lived in
  C^N and the formula made sense; in JSAC2 they live in C^N and C^K_99
  respectively and the difference is undefined.
- The "no-cal baseline at -4.7 dB" is the per-path no-calibration error
  for an IID log-normal-magnitude / von-Mises-phase amplitude
  perturbation (sigma_mag = 3 dB, kappa = 4). It has no body model.
- "200 trials" matches the predecessor's `N_TRIALS = 200`, but the
  JSAC2 calibration_run.py uses `n_trials = 30`. The two sweeps were
  never reconciled.

The actual JSAC2 body-side calibration produces
`outputs/fig3_calibration.pdf` (Tier-D, Tier-C, Tier-B vs SNR for
far30m and close5m). This figure is **not cited anywhere** in the v3
paper or v3 SI. The cited figure is the predecessor's BS-side
beta-calibration plot.

## B. The Appendix C identifiability proposition is dimensionally
   ill-formed at face value, and false at the operating point even
   with the most charitable reading

The proposition (Sec V, Eq before line 727; Appendix C lines 1601-1608)
states:

> The fit is non-trivial when SNR_ul >= K * sigma_K^{-2}.

`sigma_K` is the K-th singular value of `Lambda_KH`. In the JSAC2
implementation (`calibration_run.py`), `Lambda_KH` is a per-element-per-
triangle Kirchhoff coefficient with absolute magnitudes ~1e-6 - 2e-5
(carrying the mmWave free-space pathloss). So `sigma_K^{-2}` is on the
order of 1e10 - 1e12. The LHS `SNR_ul` is dimensionless (signal-to-noise
ratio). The inequality "SNR (dimensionless) >= K * 1e11 (dim. of 1/Lambda^2)"
is dimensionally inconsistent. The proposition statement implicitly
assumes `Lambda_KH` is normalised but never says so, and the code never
normalises it.

The most charitable reading is to interpret `sigma_K` in normalised units
`sigma_K / sigma_1` or to use the per-mode spectral mass
`sigma_K^2 / ||Lambda||_F^2`. Under that reading, the K-th mode is
informative when its spectral mass exceeds `1 / SNR_ul`. For the close5m
geometry the per-mode spectral masses (verified against
`calibration_recovery_close5m.npz`'s `S` array) are:

| k  | mass (sigma_k^2 / sum) | min SNR for mode k informative |
|----|------------------------|--------------------------------|
| 1  | 0.8683                 |  0.6 dB                        |
| 2  | 0.1079                 |  9.7 dB                        |
| 3  | 0.0130                 | 18.9 dB                        |
| 4  | 0.0057                 | 22.5 dB                        |
| 5  | 0.0025                 | 26.0 dB                        |

The Appendix's stated operating point is 20 dB UL pilot SNR with K_99 = 4,
and it claims this "sits inside the identifiable region". The 4th mode
needs >=22.5 dB for `sigma_4^2 / sigma_p^2 > 1`. Under the K factor
(`SNR > K / mass(K_99)` reading), the threshold is `4 / 0.0057 = 28.5 dB`.
Either way 20 dB is below the threshold. The proposition's specific dB
claim is empirically violated for the close-BS regime by the same code
that generated the supporting npz.

Numerical confirmation in `calibration_recovery_close5m.npz`: at 20 dB,
the K_99 = 4 Tier-B fit gives 36.6% relative reconstruction error,
compared to 31.9% at K = 2 and 35.9% at K = 1. K_99 is not even the
optimal K choice at the stated operating point.

Also: Appendix C line 1606 asserts "sigma_K^{-2} <= 1" for all served-user
geometries. With `sigma_K` in absolute units this is six orders of
magnitude wrong. With `sigma_K / sigma_1` it gives `(sigma_4/sigma_1)^{-2}
= 152.9` for close5m, still not <= 1. The bound is unsupported under any
natural normalisation.

## C. The two-tier (Tier-D + Tier-B) story in the SI does not match the
   code

Main paper Sec V.B speaks only of a "K-mode SVD basis". The SI
introduces, without forward-reference from the main text:

- Tier-D scalar pre-conditioning: "fit a single complex scalar beta_0 on
  the LOS coefficient and a single real scalar on h_body to absorb gain
  miscalibration. The Tier-B SVD fit then operates on the resulting
  Tier-D residual r^(D)."

The actual code:

- `calibration.py::fit_tier_d` (lines 74-96) fits **one complex scalar
  gamma_0** on `h_body_pred`; it does not touch `h_los`. Inputs:
  `h_meas, h_los, h_body_pred`. Output: `gamma_0` such that
  `h_pred = h_los + gamma_0 * h_body_pred`. So Tier-D applies one complex
  DOF to the body channel, not (one complex DOF on LOS + one real on
  body) as the SI claims.
- `calibration_run.py` (lines 105-119) calls `fit_tier_d`,
  `fit_tier_c` (per-region R = 6), and `fit_tier_b` (K-mode SVD) as
  three **independent parallel fits**, each consuming the same residual
  `y = h_meas - h_los`. The Tier-B fit is **not** chained on the Tier-D
  residual.

Additionally, the Tier-C level (R = 6 anatomical regions) is computed,
saved into `calibration_recovery_*.npz`, never described in the main paper
or SI, and uses a 6-region partition `partition_thelonious_regions`
hardcoded to the **Thelonious upper-torso STL** and the bounding-box
heuristic — not the SMPL-X body the rest of the JSAC2 pipeline uses
(`pose_sweep.make_body_yaw_smplx`). It is dead code in the production
pipeline.

So we have a four-tier code (`A, B, C, D`), a two-tier SI (`B, D`)
describing a different algorithm than the code, and a one-tier main
paper (`B`-only). The discrepancy is not just notational; the SI's
"Tier-B operates on the Tier-D residual" describes a chained
pre-conditioner that does not exist in the implementation.

## D. The K_99 SVD basis is not measured on the JSAC2 Munich scenes

SI S8 caption ("Operator Lambda_KH singular-value spectrum on the JSAC2
Munich scene, normalised per regime"), and SI body line 524:

> reports the singular-value spectrum of Lambda_KH across the three BS
> regimes of the JSAC2 Munich scene.

The figure is `outputs/fig2_svd_spectrum.png/pdf`, generated by
`svd_spectrum.py`. Inspecting the script:

- `make_body(yaw_deg, body_world_centroid)` loads
  `data/thelonious.stl` (the original AEGIS upper-torso phantom);
- `BSArray` is a synthetic 8x8 panel placed in **free space** at
  `bs_distances = [(30,8), (10,5), (5,3), (3,2.5)]` metres;
- `los_path_dict` builds a single LOS path from BS to body centroid;
- No Munich GLB scene is loaded. No Sionna RT trace is imported.

The Munich-scene loaders (`latent_sweep_munich.load_munich_scene`) live
elsewhere and are used by the latent sweep, hero figures, and Q
construction. The SVD spectrum sweep is a synthetic free-space companion
that has nothing to do with Munich. The SI sentence "across the three BS
regimes of the JSAC2 Munich scene" misrepresents what the script
computed.

K_99 across the synthetic geometries (verified by loading
`outputs/svd_spectrum.npz`):

| BS distance | K_90 | K_99 | K_999 | Cond.       |
|-------------|------|------|-------|-------------|
| 30 m        | 1    | 1    | 2     | 2.32e+13    |
| 10 m        | 1    | 2    | 3     | 4.09e+13    |
| 5 m         | 2    | 3    | 6     | 6.12e+13    |
| 3 m         | 2    | 4    | 8     | 4.82e+11    |

So the actual sweep gives `K_99 in {1, 2, 3, 4}`. Both the SI body and
the figure caption claim `K_99 in {1, 2, 4}` — omitting the K_99 = 3
data point at d = 5 m. The omission is consistent only with a different
geometry (the calibration_run scripts use `phone_offset = 0.55 m` and get
`K_99 = 4` at 5 m, while svd_spectrum uses `phone_offset = 0.40 m` and
gets `K_99 = 3`). The two scripts are not commensurable: K_99 depends on
the phone position and they use different ones.

Either way, the paper does not say "K_99 measured on a synthetic
free-space rebuild of an upper-torso phantom at synthetic BS distances",
which is what the figure actually shows.

## E. The unknowns/equations balance is not honest

Main paper Eq. 5 splits `Delta in C^M (M = 64)` as

  Delta = Phi*J*delta_beta + Lambda_KH*delta_q + n_p

with `delta_beta in C^N` (N = number of BS path entries; in JSAC2 Munich,
`P_sionna ~ 100-500` per `pose_pipeline_jax.py:283-288`) and `delta_q in
C^{T_vis}` (T_vis ~ 10^4) projected to gamma in C^K. The paper writes:

> Both sides are identifiable from the same uplink stream when the column
> spaces of Phi*J and Lambda_KH are linearly independent.

Linear independence of column spaces is necessary but not sufficient for
identifiability when the **total number of unknowns exceeds the number
of equations**. With `N + K_99 ~ 350-500 + 4 >> 64 = M`, the system is
under-determined regardless of column-space orthogonality. The
"identifiable from the same uplink stream" claim handwaves this; the
paper would need to state a sparsity assumption, a per-snapshot fit on K
< M DoFs, or a slow-vs-fast separation (e.g., `delta_beta` calibrated on
a long-horizon background fit, `gamma` fit per slot) to make the
counting work.

The predecessor's csi_calibration figure happens to use `N in {20, 40,
60}` and explicitly notes the failure mode at `N -> M_ant`. The Munich
JSAC2 setting has `N` in the few hundreds. Importing the predecessor
figure as the calibration-recovery evidence quietly hides this scaling
problem.

## F. The "pose error and dielectric mismatch live in span(U_K)" claim
   is empirically reasonable but advertised stronger than it is

Sec V.D, lines 736-739:

> The body-side gamma absorbs pose error (residual IMU drift after
> attitude filtering) and dielectric mismatch in the same regression.
> To first order, both error sources produce residuals in the column
> space of Lambda_KH.

Mathematically, **any** body-mediated channel residual lies in
colspan(`Lambda_KH`) by construction (the body-mediated channel is
literally `Lambda_KH . q`). So the column-space claim is trivially true
and adds no information. The non-trivial claim is that the residuals lie
in `span(U_K)` for small K - which is what the calibration actually
projects onto.

I tested this empirically by perturbing the body in the close5m geometry:

- Yaw perturbation at sigma in {1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45} degrees:
  K = 4 captures 91-99.7% of `||r||`.
- Centroid translation in {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} m: K = 4 captures
  99.4-99.9% of `||r||`.
- Scalar dielectric perturbation (epsilon = 0.1): K = 4 captures 99.3%.

So the **operational** claim (K = 4 captures > 90% of typical pose /
dielectric residuals on close5m) is supported. The **stated** claim
("residuals in the column space of Lambda_KH") is trivially true and
uninformative. Wording would be more honest as "in the leading-K span
of Lambda_KH". The paper does not do the empirical study above (it just
asserts the property), so the support is hand-waved.

## G. The IMU "gamma fit" robustness story is not measured by any
   experiment

Sec V.D claims gamma absorbs pose error. SI Sec "IMU sensitivity overlay"
displays `imu_gamma_overlay.pdf`, which the user task lists as
"robustness to IMU drift via the gamma fit".

What's actually shown in the PDF on disk (mtime 2026-05-10 21:38) is the
output of `imu_sweep_munich.py`: closed-loop SINR loss vs.
sigma_joint, with **no gamma calibration applied at all**. The script
runs perturbed gradient ascent on a noisy IMU pose and measures the SINR
gap to the noiseless oracle. It does not run any Tier-B SVD fit. The
file `imu_gamma_overlay.npz` is stale (mtime 12:27) from the analytical
script `imu_gamma_overlay.py` and uses incompatible keys.

The analytical script `imu_gamma_overlay.py` constructs a "gamma-corrected
violation rate" curve as

  v_gc(sigma) = v_oracle + (v_cauchy - v_oracle) * w(sigma) * (1 - rho_B)

with `rho_B = 0.935` derived (per its own docstring) from a separate
30-trial Kirchhoff sim that is not saved to disk. The numbers are
hand-typed into the docstring (sigma=2: rho_B=0.923; sigma=4: 0.952;
sigma=8: 0.927; sigma=16: 0.950) and averaged. So the gamma-corrected
curve is

1. an exponential model fit to two anchor points (oracle, IMU 4 deg) from
   the predecessor paper's binding regime;
2. multiplied by a constant scaling 0.935 read from a docstring summary
   of an unsaved 30-trial simulation.

Neither the SI text nor any saved npz documents how the calibration
actually operates inside the IMU sweep. The closed-loop rate-loss
metric the SI cites (`<= 0.5 dB` across sigma in {0,2,4,8,16} deg from
`munich_imu_sweep.npz`) is achieved **without** any gamma correction. So
either:

- Gamma correction is unused and the closed loop is robust on its own
  (in which case the SI sentence "the body-side gamma absorbs pose
  error" is unsupported), or
- Gamma correction is applied but never measured and the rho_B = 0.935
  factor is purely modelled.

The figure does not contain the analytical gamma-corrected curve; the
PDF in the figures directory was overwritten by the Munich sweep at
21:38. So the "via the gamma fit" attribution in the user's task
description is not in the rendered figure either.

## H. Code quality and consistency

`calibration.py`:

- The Tier-A docstring says `gamma_t in C^T` but no implementation
  exists.
- `fit_tier_b` returns `K_eff = min(K, len(S))` and computes
  `spectral_mass`, but `calibration_run.py` does not use them; it caches
  its own SVD with no clipping at the K_99 condition required by
  Appendix C.
- Initial guess for the ridge in `fit_tier_b` is `alpha_init = S[:K_eff]`
  i.e. the "uncalibrated" point in alpha-space. With `rho = 1e-3` this
  pulls hard toward sigma_k for the leading mode (where sigma_k is
  large) and barely at all for the trailing modes (sigma_k tiny). The
  effect is a **mode-dependent** prior that is not described in the
  paper or in the docstring.

`calibration_run.py`:

- Uses `rho = 1e-12` (line 106, 111) for Tier-D and Tier-C, effectively
  disabling regularisation, while paper says `lambda > 0`.
- Uses `_fit_tier_b_cached` (line 88-95) which sets `rho = 0` (no ridge
  at all), inconsistent with `fit_tier_b` and with Appendix Eq.
  S(gamma-ridge).
- The "ground-truth" perturbation `gamma_true` is generated once
  (`_gen_random_unit_perturbation(R = 6, seed = 0)`) per scenario, then
  reused across every SNR cell. So the same complex 6-vector defines
  truth for every Monte Carlo trial; only the noise varies. The 30
  trials are therefore not 30 independent "scenes", they are 30 noise
  realisations of the same scene. This is a much weaker statement than
  "30 Monte Carlo trials" suggests and is not flagged in any output.

`outputs/calibration_recovery.npz`:

The first-generation file (mtime 10:48) is broken: every err_c entry
is identical (0.192) regardless of SNR, and Tier-B `err_b ~ 1.99` at
every K and every SNR. This was clearly a botched run that someone
re-ran 11 minutes later into the labelled `_close5m` and `_far30m`
files. The broken file is still in the repo; nothing reads it but it
clutters the audit trail.

`outputs/calibration_recovery_close5m.npz` and `_far30m.npz`:

- Schema: 8 SNRs x 6 K values x 30 trials = 1440 trials per geometry.
- These are NOT the (3 N, 7 SNR, 200 trial) shape that the SI claims
  for the calibration figure. The cited figure data lives in
  `JSAC/code/experiments/csi_calibration/residuals.npz` (different
  manuscript).
- `K_99` is stored as a single int per geometry (1 for far, 4 for
  close). No per-mode SNR threshold check is performed.

## Bottom line

The closed-loop calibration story has six layers of slippage:

1. The main paper claims a clean residual decomposition with two
   simultaneously identifiable corrections, but the unknowns (~500) far
   exceed the equations (M = 64) and the "linearly independent column
   spaces" condition does not save it. (Issue E.)
2. Appendix C states a dimensionally ill-formed proposition; the most
   charitable interpretation makes the 20 dB operating point miss the
   identifiability threshold for K_99 = 4 by ~3-9 dB. (Issue B.)
3. The SI describes a Tier-D + Tier-B chained pipeline that the code
   does not implement. (Issue C.)
4. The K_99 sweep in SI S8 is run on synthetic free-space upper-torso
   phantom geometries, not on the Munich scene the SI text claims; the
   reported K_99 in {1, 2, 4} omits the K_99 = 3 d=5m result. (Issue D.)
5. The recovery-vs-SNR figure used as evidence is from a different
   problem (predecessor BS-side per-path beta, not body-side gamma) and
   is conceptually incompatible with the SI text it accompanies; the
   actual JSAC2 calibration figure (`fig3_calibration.pdf`) is uncited.
   (Issue A.)
6. The "gamma fit" robustness to IMU drift is asserted but not measured;
   the IMU sweep in the SI does not apply gamma calibration at all, and
   the analytical gamma-corrected curve from `imu_gamma_overlay.py`
   never makes it to the rendered figure. (Issue G.)

The closest-to-real numerical study, `calibration_recovery_close5m.npz`,
shows that at the paper's stated operating point (20 dB UL pilot SNR,
K_99 = 4) the body-channel reconstruction error is 36.6%, with K = 2
beating K = 4. This is incompatible with the paper's narrative that
K_99 is the "right" basis size and the operating point is identifiable.
A serious revision would either:

(a) drop the proposition and replace it with a per-mode SNR criterion
plus an empirical "K* selected by minimising MSE on a held-out trial"
rule, then redraw the figure on Munich data with both far-BS and
close-BS regimes; or

(b) restrict the calibration-recovery story to the far-BS regime where
K_99 = 1 and the leading mode actually carries 99.3% of the spectral
mass, accepting that close-BS is a separate problem.

The current text reads as a clean theory + empirical-validation pair,
but the validation is from a different manuscript and the theory's
operating-point claim is empirically refuted by the JSAC2 own data.
