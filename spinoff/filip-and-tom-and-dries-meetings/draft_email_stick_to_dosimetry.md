# Draft email to Filip

**Subject:** AEGIS: recommendation to keep the IOF focused on dosimetry

Dear Filip,

Your challenge during the intake was useful. We needed to test whether AEGIS had a stronger application outside dosimetry, or whether the dosimetry case itself could support a credible valorisation project. I have now worked through both questions, including the broader EM applications, customer feedback, standards route, validation requirements, and bottom-up economics.

My conclusion is that we should keep the IOF and the initial IP strategy focused on dosimetry. More precisely, the product should be a fast screening and design-optimisation layer for absorbed power density. It would evaluate large sets of device configurations and pass the selected worst cases to established FDTD/FEM and measurement workflows for final verification.

I do not think the correct commercial comparison is that a new tool captures 1 to 5% of ZMT's present turnover. That assumes the same narrow seat-based product and the same customers. AEGIS can be sold at workflow level to device and chipset teams, test laboratories, and incumbent tool vendors. A specialist seven-figure business is credible if the validation succeeds. A larger EUR 5 to 7 million recurring-revenue outcome is possible through enterprise and embedded licences, but should be presented as conditional upside rather than the base forecast.

For a EUR 250,000, two-year maturation project, the immediate question is whether the project can remove the decisive technical and commercial risks. I think it can. The enclosed section proposes a bounded validation programme against qualified full-wave simulations and independent APD measurements, with predeclared acceptance criteria, uncertainty bounds, blind holdouts, and explicit stop or scope-reduction decisions.

Could we align on dosimetry as the core IOF thesis, with broader EM generalisation no longer a prerequisite? I would prefer to settle that internally before a wider meeting with Dries, Hendrik, or Keysight, so that such a meeting tests a defined hypothesis rather than reopening the product definition.

Best,

Robin
