\section{Fresnel transmission}\label{sec:fresnel}

The material factor of the master law is angle dependent. At oblique incidence
the normal-incidence scalar $T_0$ of \cref{prop:T0} splits into two
polarisation-specific coefficients $T_s$ and $T_p$. This section derives them
from the electromagnetic boundary conditions, records the unpolarised average
$\Tavg$ that the incoherent levels use, and notes the complex transmitted-angle
quantity that the coherent depth coupling of \cref{sec:coherent} reuses.

\subsection{Snell's law and the complex normal wave number}\label{sec:snell}

The body surface is a planar interface at $z=0$ between free space ($n_1=1$,
$z>0$) and a lossy half-space of index $\ntilde$ ($z<0$). Phase matching of the
incident, reflected, and transmitted waves along the interface forces a common
tangential wave number, which gives the law of reflection $\theta_r=\theta_i$
and Snell's law generalised to complex media,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:snell}
  \sin\theta_t \;=\; \frac{\sin\theta_i}{\ntilde}.
\end{equation}
Because $\ntilde$ is complex, $\theta_t$ is complex and is not a geometric
angle. The physically meaningful quantity is the normal component of the
transmitted wave vector, $k_{t,z}=k_0\ntilde\cos\theta_t \equiv k_0\,\xi$, with

\begin{definition}[Complex normal wave number]\label{def:xi}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-def}
  \xi(\mu,\ntilde) \;\equiv\; \ntilde\cos\theta_t
  \;=\; \sqrt{\ntilde^2 - \sin^2\theta_i}
  \;=\; \sqrt{\ntilde^2 - 1 + \mu^2},
  \qquad \RE(\xi)>0,
\end{equation}
where $\mu=\cos\theta_i$. The branch $\RE(\xi)>0$ enforces decay into the
medium. For a lossless dielectric $\xi$ is real; for tissue it is complex, and
its imaginary part sets the oblique penetration depth.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Reflection coefficients from boundary conditions}\label{sec:rs-rp}

Continuity of the tangential electric and magnetic fields across $z=0$ gives the
Fresnel amplitude reflection coefficients. For TE (s) polarisation the electric
field is perpendicular to the plane of incidence, and matching tangential $E$
and $H$ yields an impedance-mismatch ratio between the free-space factor $\mu$
and the medium factor $\xi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rs}
  r_s \;=\; \frac{\mu-\xi}{\mu+\xi}.
\end{equation}
For TM (p) polarisation the magnetic field is perpendicular to the plane of
incidence, and the constitutive relation $\mathbf{D}=\epstilde\,\varepsilon_0\,\EE$
introduces a factor $\ntilde^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rp}
  r_p \;=\; \frac{\ntilde^2\,\mu-\xi}{\ntilde^2\,\mu+\xi}.
\end{equation}
Energy conservation (all transmitted power is absorbed, \cref{sec:power-balance})
turns these into power-transmission coefficients.

\begin{definition}[Polarisation-resolved transmission]\label{def:Ts-Tp}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ts-Tp}
  T_s(\theta) = 1-\abs*{\frac{\mu-\xi}{\mu+\xi}}^2,
  \qquad
  T_p(\theta) = 1-\abs*{\frac{\ntilde^2\mu-\xi}{\ntilde^2\mu+\xi}}^2.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

At normal incidence $\mu=1$ and $\xi=\ntilde$, so $r_s$ and $r_p$ coincide and
both transmissions reduce to $T_0$ of \cref{prop:T0}. The complex amplitude
transmission coefficients used by the coherent channel of \cref{sec:coherent}
are $t_s = 2\mu/(\mu+\xi)$ and $t_p = 2\ntilde\mu/(\ntilde^2\mu+\xi)$.

\subsection{Unpolarised average and obliquity}\label{sec:Tavg}

The incoherent levels of \cref{sec:grid} that do not track polarisation use the
average of the two transmissions.

\begin{definition}[Unpolarised transmission]\label{def:Tavg}
For unpolarised or circularly polarised illumination the effective transmission
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tavg-def}
  T_0^{\,\mathrm{eff}}(\theta) \;=\; \Tavg(\theta)
  \;\equiv\; \half\bigl[T_s(\theta)+T_p(\theta)\bigr].
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\noindent
The associated polarisation splitting $\Delta T(\theta)\equiv T_p(\theta)-T_s(\theta)\ge0$
(for $\abs{\ntilde}>1$) is the quantity activated by the polarisation-aware law
of \cref{sec:pol}. In the absorbed-power-density product the transmission always
multiplies the projection factor $\mu=\cos\theta_i$, so the obliquity factor
$\cos\theta_i$ and the transmission $T(\theta)$ act together: at grazing
incidence $\cos\theta_i\to0$ suppresses absorption even where transmission
remains finite.

\subsection{Pseudo-Brewster behaviour}\label{sec:brewster}

For a lossless interface the TM reflection vanishes at the Brewster angle
$\theta_B=\arctan(n_2/n_1)$. For a lossy dielectric the TM reflection does not
reach zero but passes through a finite minimum at the pseudo-Brewster angle
$\theta_{pB}$, where $T_p$ peaks. The TE transmission $T_s$ decreases
monotonically with angle. The two effects partly cancel in the average, so
$\Tavg(\theta)$ stays close to its normal-incidence value $T_0$ over a wide
angular range. For skin at \SI{28}{\giga\hertz} the average remains within
\SI{5.6}{\percent} of $T_0$ up to steep angles, which is what justifies the
constant-$T_0$ approximation used by the lowest fidelity levels of
\cref{sec:grid}. The full angular structure of $\Delta T$ (it reaches
$\approx0.83$ near $75^\circ$ at \SI{28}{\giga\hertz}) is the lever the
polarisation correction of \cref{sec:pol} pulls on.

\subsection{Transmitted angle and depth coupling}\label{sec:xi-coherent}

The complex normal wave number $\xi(\mu,\ntilde)$ of \cref{def:xi} is not
merely an intermediate algebraic quantity. Its imaginary part is the oblique
attenuation constant, and the coherent formulation of \cref{sec:coherent} uses
it directly to phase and attenuate the transmitted field as it couples energy
into the lossy layer. Carrying $\xi$ (rather than only the real power
coefficients $T_s,T_p$) is therefore necessary for the coherent levels and is
forward-referenced there.
