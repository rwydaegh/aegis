Basic, crystal clear, no fluff. BUT clean. Sooo, that means generate --> png --> read --> critique extremely hard --> iterate, until satisfied. 3. Extremely precise on the use of ~. Adds it almost everywhere. Not just the obvious places, but nearly every number ever has one of these ~, or before a unit, and much much more. 
4. Wout is lawful neutral, he wants the paper to be normal and will circle any kind of weird sentence that isn't absolute deadpan straightforward science. Even words that are considered weird.
5. The abstract is quite prescriptive. It needs to be very dense. Needs to show key results. The quantities cited (numbers) require a remarkable amount of precision rather than 'just throwing out a number'. An error is not just 'an error', it's quite precise even in the abstract. 
6. Wout likes to sell towards the end of the introduction. The words 'For the first time' NEED to be included, and when the first author realizes, oh shit ive used it already 3 times, quickly searches for synonyms of this little subsentence (premier for example). Not just for the first time X, but restressing that things are done for the first time across 'first time' sentences. This is an artform as on the same time, it needs to remain precise and scientific. This is quite enumerative, to the point we can even consider a small bullet point list (maybe not, idk, depends).
7. **No internal-codebase brand names anywhere in the paper.** The codebase has a project name (e.g., "AEGIS"); the paper is science. Brand names belong in implementation, in repositories, on dashboards. In a journal paper, use neutral identifiers ("the closed-form prediction", "the proposed method", "the present approach") in legends, captions, and body prose alike. Wout will flag every occurrence.
8. **Standalone is non-negotiable; content overlap is fine.** No "as in companion paper A" prose. No `\cite{...}` placeholders for unpublished sister papers (unless the venue is an explicit short-letter format where parallel-submission citation is part of the contract, and even then only with a real arXiv DOI). Each paper derives what its readers need from first principles. Overlap of *content* between sister papers is fine; overlap of *citation graph* is not.
9. **No authors named in the abstract before the body introduces them.** "Smith's identity" / "Foschini's bound" / "the Azzam regime" are fine in the body after the result has been described. The abstract reader hasn't met these names. Reframe to plain prose: "a published identity for...", "a regime in which...", or just describe the result.
10. **No anthropomorphism of standards bodies, fields, literature, or algorithms.** They don't "want", "care about", "demand", "converge on", or "agree" on anything. ICNIRP *specifies*; the literature *reports*; the algorithm *computes*. "The regulator wants X" is the wrong angle; "ICNIRP C95.1 specifies X above 6 GHz" is the right one. "Five communities", "the literature has converged", "we chain five results into one closed form" — drop the grand-narrative framing. Cite the work and state the result.
11. **Quantitative abstract precision.** Numbers in the abstract should match the body to the third or fourth significant figure. State the regime: "up to 10^12 cells at 100 GHz on an adult phantom" beats "10^12 cells". Use "factor of N reduction", not "N reduction". An error is not "an error"; it has a numerical bound.
