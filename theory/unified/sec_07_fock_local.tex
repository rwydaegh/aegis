\section{Local diffraction: the Fock gate}\label{sec:fock-local}

The occlusion factor of \cref{sec:geom} is binary and discontinuous across the
shadow edge. Diffraction smooths it over a finite penumbra and leaks a small
field into the geometric shadow. On a smooth convex body the correct description
is Fock's boundary-layer theory of the shadow boundary, not knife-edge
diffraction. This section builds the local gate that replaces the heuristic GELU
smoothing of the monograph: a single complex transition function $g(\xi)$,
validated against the exact cylinder and sphere oracles
(\cref{sec:validation}), carrying the soft/hard polarisation split and the
lossy-skin impedance correction.

\subsection{The terminator and the Fock detour parameter}\label{sec:fock-xi}

The shadow boundary, or terminator, is the grazing locus $\mu=0$, with
$\mu\equiv\nhat\cdot(-\khat)$. On a convex body a surface point at signed angular
distance $\theta$ from the terminator has $\mu=\sin\theta$, so the map from the
obliquity to the signed terminator angle is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-mu}
  \theta = \arcsin\mu, \qquad
  \theta>0 \ \text{(lit)}, \quad \theta<0 \ \text{(shadow)}.
\end{equation}

Fock's analysis introduces the large parameter $\mfock=(k\Rfock/2)^{1/3}$, with
$\Rfock$ the in-incidence-plane radius of curvature
(\cref{prop:fock-radius}), and the dimensionless detour parameter
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fock-xi}
  \xi = \mfock\,\theta = \Bigl(\tfrac{k\Rfock}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\theta,
  \qquad \xi>0 \ \text{lit}, \quad \xi<0 \ \text{shadow}.
\end{equation}
The geometry $(\Rfock,\theta)$ is frequency-independent; $k$ enters only through
$\mfock$. The boundary layer is $O(1)$ in $\xi$, so its angular width scales as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fock-width}
  \Delta\theta \sim \mfock^{-1} = \Bigl(\tfrac{k\Rfock}{2}\Bigr)^{-1/3}
  \propto (k\Rfock)^{-1/3}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Why the GELU is the wrong form]\label{rem:gelu-wrong}
The monograph smooths the shadow edge with a GELU whose width is the Fresnel-zone
scale $\sigma=\sqrt{\lambda/2\pi R}\propto(kR)^{-1/2}$, the knife-edge law. The
exact cylinder oracle measures a shadow-decay exponent of $0.35$ to $0.39$ across
$kR$, close to the Fock value $1/3$ and far from the knife-edge $1/2$. The GELU
is therefore wrong in functional form, not merely in its constant: at 28\,GHz its
penumbra is too narrow by $(kR)^{1/6}$, a factor of $1.6$ to $2.7$ across body
parts. A limb diffracts like a Fock convex body, not a knife edge.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The uniform transition function}\label{sec:fock-g}

The gate is built as an additive uniform composite of a lit transition that
recovers geometric optics deep in the lit region and a creeping-wave residue
series that carries the field into the shadow.

\begin{definition}[Fock transition function]\label{def:fock-g}
For polarisation $\mathrm{pol}\in\{\text{soft},\text{hard}\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fock-g}
  g_{\mathrm{pol}}(\xi) = \Philit(\xi) + \Psishad(\xi),
\end{equation}
with the lit transition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-lit}
  \Philit(\xi) = \half\Bigl[1+\erf\!\bigl(\xi/\sqrt2\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
and the shadow creeping series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi-shadow}
  \Psishad(\xi) = \bigl[1-\Philit(\xi)\bigr]\sum_{p=1}^{P}
  A_p\,e^{\mathrm{i}\nu_p\,\min(\xi,0)},
  \qquad \nu_p = q_p\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi/3}.
\end{equation}
The $q_p$ are the creeping eigenvalues (\cref{sec:fock-eig}), $A_p$ the
corresponding residue amplitudes, and $P=3$ terms suffice.
\end{definition}

The exponent rotation $\nu_p=q_p\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi/3}$ is the crux of the sign
bookkeeping. It gives $\abs{e^{\mathrm{i}\nu_p\xi}} = e^{(\sqrt3/2)q_p\xi}$, which
decays for $\xi<0$, so in the shadow $\abs{\Psishad}^2\sim e^{-\sqrt3\,q_p\abs{\xi}}$,
the validated creeping decay law, while $\RE\nu_p$ advances the creeping phase
along the surface.

\begin{remark}[Windowing of the creeping series]\label{rem:windowing}
The bare residue sum $\sum_p A_p e^{\mathrm{i}\nu_p\xi}$ is a shadow-side
asymptotic: with $\nu_p=q_p e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi/3}$ it grows exponentially for
$\xi>0$ and is meaningless in the lit region. \cref{eq:psi-shadow} keeps it
bounded two ways. The phase is evaluated at the shadow-clamped argument
$\min(\xi,0)$ (smoothly, so $\Psishad$ stays $C^\infty$), and the whole series is
tapered by the shadow window $1-\Philit(\xi)$. Deep in the shadow both reduce to
the identity, recovering the exact creeping series and its $\sqrt3\,q_p$ decay
exactly; deep in the lit region the window kills it and $g\to\Philit\to 1$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Limits of the gate]\label{prop:fock-limits}
The transition function $g_{\mathrm{pol}}$ of \cref{def:fock-g} satisfies
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $g_{\mathrm{pol}}(\xi)\to 1$ as $\xi\to+\infty$ (geometric optics in the
        deep lit region);
  \item $g_{\mathrm{pol}}(\xi)\to A_1 e^{\mathrm{i}\nu_1\xi}\to 0$ as
        $\xi\to-\infty$, with $\abs{g_{\mathrm{pol}}}^2\sim
        e^{-\sqrt3\,q_1\abs{\xi}}$ (the dominant creeping mode);
  \item for fixed $\theta\ne 0$, $\xi\to\pm\infty$ as $k\to\infty$, so the gate
        converges pointwise to $\ReLU(\mu)/\mu$ as $\lambda\to 0$, recovering the
        sharp shadow edge (asymptotically, not bit-for-bit; the exact ReLU is the
        $\lambda\to 0$ limit, not an evaluation of $g$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The residue amplitudes $A_p$ are the analytic Fock residues, computed (not
fitted) from the Airy functions and anchored so the composite is $C^1$ at the
terminator and $\abs{g(0)}^2$ matches the exact cylinder terminator value. The
hard channel anchors the shared prefactor, because its oracle observable is the
surface field (angle-independent geometric optics), giving a clean
$kR$-independent terminator value $\abs{g(0)}^2\approx 0.488$. That value sits
\emph{above} the knife-edge half value, so the creeping wave adds constructively
and the residue magnitudes are the right sign. The soft channel shares the
prefactor and is anchored instead through its shadow decay, since its oracle
observable is the surface current, which diverges at the terminator.

\subsection{The creeping eigenvalues: soft, hard, and impedance}\label{sec:fock-eig}

The eigenvalues $q_p$ carry the polarisation split. The naming is a known trap.
The soft (Dirichlet) channel is the cylinder $\mathrm{TM}_z$ case, with $E$ along
the axis, which is Fresnel s-polarisation ($E$ perpendicular to the plane of
incidence). The hard (Neumann) channel is the cylinder $\mathrm{TE}_z$ case,
Fresnel p-polarisation.

\begin{definition}[PEC creeping eigenvalues]\label{def:pec-eig}
For a perfect conductor the creeping eigenvalues are the magnitudes of the Airy
zeros,
\begin{align}
  q_p^{\text{soft}} &= \abs{a_p}, & \Ai(-q_p^{\text{soft}}) &= 0,
    & (q_1,q_2,\dots) &= (2.338,\,4.088,\,\dots), \label{eq:q-soft}\\
  q_p^{\text{hard}} &= \abs{a_p'}, & \Aip(-q_p^{\text{hard}}) &= 0,
    & (q_1,q_2,\dots) &= (1.019,\,3.248,\,\dots). \label{eq:q-hard}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

The hard creeping wave decays more slowly than the soft one because
$q_1^{\text{hard}}<q_1^{\text{soft}}$. The decay-rate ratio approaches the
textbook PEC value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-pec}
  \frac{q_1^{\text{soft}}}{q_1^{\text{hard}}} = \frac{2.338}{1.019} = 2.295,
\end{equation}
confirmed on the cylinder oracle ($1.96\to 2.28$ as $kR$ grows $10\to 320$,
converging to $2.295$).

Skin is not a perfect conductor. For a lossy surface of impedance
$\eta=1/\ntilde$ the creeping eigenvalues shift to the roots of the Leontovich
impedance-Fock equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leontovich}
  \Aip(t) - \qF\,\Ai(t) = 0, \qquad q_p = -t,
\end{equation}
where the Fock impedance parameter $\qF$ carries the $\mfock=(k\Rfock/2)^{1/3}$
scaling (schematically $\qF\sim\mathrm{i}\,\mfock\,\eta$ for the hard branch and
$\sim\mfock/(\mathrm{i}\,\eta)$ for the soft branch). The PEC limits are
$\qF\to\infty$ (Dirichlet, soft) and $\qF=0$ (Neumann, hard).

\begin{remark}[The gain-sign convention]\label{rem:gain-sign}
AEGIS stores the refractive index as $\ntilde=n-\mathrm{i}\kappa$ (engineering
$e^{+\mathrm{i}\omega t}$), so $\eta=1/\ntilde$ has $\IM\eta>0$: a passive
inductive skin, $\eta\approx 0.19+0.08\mathrm{i}$ at 28\,GHz. Feeding the stored
$\IM\eta<0$ into an $e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}$ Hankel oracle models a \emph{gain}
medium and pumps the surface-bound hard creeping wave, producing a spurious
anti-Fock shadow that grows with $kR$. This was a real bug. The hard channel
is the one that hugs the lossy surface, so it is the one a sign error corrupts;
the soft channel has a near-node at the surface and is sign-robust. The solver
rejects $\IM\eta<0$.
\end{remark}

Because $\qF\propto(k\Rfock)^{1/3}$, the hard eigenvalue is not a fixed constant:
it drifts with $k\Rfock$. The effective hard eigenvalue migrates from the PEC
value $1.019$ upward toward the soft value $2.338$ as $kR$ grows
($q_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\text{hard}}\approx 1.20$ at $kR=40$, $1.84$ at $kR=2560$),
while the soft eigenvalue stays pinned near $2.3$. The surface looks
progressively softer to the hard creeping wave. The soft/hard decay ratio
therefore falls below the PEC $2.295$, to roughly $1.7$ to $1.95$ over
body-relevant $kR$ (a 1 to 10\,cm limb at 28\,GHz gives $kR\sim 6$ to $90$). The
exact Leontovich pole reproduces the exact dielectric-cylinder hard shadow field
to three digits (for example $5.654$ against $5.652$ at $kR=40$), so no separate
Zenneck or surface-wave term is needed: on a convex body the planar p-polarised
surface wave is leaky and radiation-damped far beyond the creeping pole. AEGIS
tabulates $q_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\text{hard}}(kR)$ per band on a log-$kR$ grid; the
soft channel uses the impedance-robust PEC value $2.338$.

\subsection{Incoherent and coherent use}\label{sec:fock-use}

The same complex $g(\xi)$ serves both regimes, which guarantees that the coherent
levels are never weaker than the incoherent ones.

The incoherent gate (levels 2 to 6) folds $\abs{g}^2$ with the geometric-optics
obliquity. With per-element TE/TM power weights $w_s,w_p$ (\cref{sec:pol}; the
unpolarised average $w_s=w_p=\half$ when no polarisation state is supplied), the
real, non-negative gate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fock-local}
  g_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu) = \max(\mu,0)\,\abs{\Philit(\xi)}^2
  + \bigl\lvert\, w_s\,\Psishad^{\text{soft}}(\xi)
                 + w_p\,\Psishad^{\text{hard}}(\xi)\,\bigr\rvert^2 .
\end{equation}
The first term is the obliquity $\mu$ modulated by the lit penumbra; the second
is the polarisation-combined creeping leakage, which is nonzero past the
terminator and independent of $\mu$. As $\lambda\to 0$ this converges to
$\ReLU(\mu)$ (\cref{prop:fock-limits}), so the diffraction model recovers the
bare surface law and the Mie regression canary is untouched.

The coherent levels (7 and 8) keep $g$ complex. The combined field gate
$w_s\,g_{\text{soft}}(\xi)+w_p\,g_{\text{hard}}(\xi)$ multiplies the body-surface
channel before the per-element accumulation (\cref{sec:coherent}). It tends to $1$
deep in the lit region, so the lit response is unchanged and coherent stays
exact where the dose lives, with the complex penumbra rolloff and creeping tail
near and into the shadow. Because $\abs{g_{\mathrm{complex}}}^2=\abs{g}^2$, the
complex gate loses no total dose against the magnitude gate, which keeps the
coherent dose at or above the incoherent dose in the lit and penumbra region.

\begin{remark}[Deep-shadow amplitude limitation]\label{rem:deep-shadow}
A three-pole additive composite with one prefactor cannot simultaneously match
the terminator value and the deep-shadow creeping amplitude; pinning the
terminator under-predicts the deep shadow by a factor of $3$ to $4$. For the
local gate this is dose-negligible: the deep-shadow leakage is about $10^{-6}$ of
the lit value, so the absolute error is order $4\times10^{-6}$ of lit. The
limitation matters for distal occlusion (\cref{sec:cmetric}), where a body part
can fully shadow another and the deeper-shadow magnitude carries real dose; that
case revisits the deep-shadow amplitude with more poles or a separate anchor.
\end{remark}
