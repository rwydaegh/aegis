% PREV: Realistic urban radiofrequency electromagnetic-field (RF-EMF) assessment must
% PREV: connect the field in a city to the power absorbed by the human body. This
% PREV: supports monitoring and epidemiological studies. The goal of
% PREV: this study is to compute direction-aware whole-body specific absorption rate
% PREV: (SAR) along pedestrian routes in ten cities. We combine 360-degree street
% PREV: images, photogrammetric geometry, and AI-derived object and material labels in
% PREV: a human-centric digital twin. Its agentic AI stage uses SAM~3 Agent for
% PREV: material and vegetation labeling. The calculation places possible transmitters
% PREV: along visible rooflines at 15~GHz, traces direct and single-reflection paths,
% PREV: and applies each arrival direction to an anatomical body model. Results are
% PREV: normalized per unit product of source density and effective isotropic radiated
% PREV: power. The ten routes contain 163 observation points, each evaluated with 64
% PREV: independent runs. The normalized route-median whole-body SAR ranges from
% PREV: 0.00902 to 0.129~m$^2$~kg$^{-1}$, a factor of 14.31. Direct paths give the
% PREV: largest component at 156 of 157 points with line of sight. The single-reflection
% PREV: specular component is largest at one point, while the single-reflection diffuse component is
% PREV: the only nonzero component at six shadowed points. Increasing the number of
% PREV: runs from 48 to 64 changes the total at any point by at most 0.32\%. Validation
% PREV: in a controlled scene gives maximum differences of 1.43\% against deterministic
% PREV: quadrature and 0.80\% against an independent Sionna RT forward calculation.
% PREV: The reported values apply to the selected routes under the stated source model.
% PREV: We do not estimate whole-city or deployed-network exposure.
% PREV: \end{abstract}
