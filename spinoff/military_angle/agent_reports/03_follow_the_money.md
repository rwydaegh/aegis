# Follow the money: tempo, HERO, or EMCON?

Agent report 03. Written 2026-07-09 against `AGENT_BRIEF.md`. Adversarial by mandate.

## 1. Verdict

All three reframings are mostly mirages for AEGIS's specific advantage: tempo has no distinct buyer and its binding constraint during flight ops is often ordnance not personnel, HERO's optimization structure transfers but AEGIS's moat (fast surface construction of Q) does not because ordnance coupling is a measured, per-munition, controlled quantity rather than a surface-absorption problem, and the EMCON opening rests on a physics claim (coherent multi-emitter addition) that is itself wrong, while the real funded EMCON program (RTSO) is an incumbent-held interoperability tool that never models a body. The one genuinely AEGIS-shaped pull I found is outside the Navy triad entirely: AFRL's directed-energy bioeffects community wants fast mission-scale dosimetry surrogates and there is a live certification gap for fielded non-lethal high-power microwave systems, both reachable through Air Force and Joint Directed Energy money rather than NAVSEA.

## 2. The three things that most changed my view

1. **The flight-deck radar restriction is not primarily a HERP problem, so Hypothesis 1 routes back through Hypothesis 2.** Navy flight-operations RF restrictions are driven jointly by HERO (ordnance on the aircraft and in loading zones), aircraft avionics EMI, and HERP, governed by NAVSEA OP 3565 Volumes 1 and 2 together, not by personnel limits alone ([NAVSEA OP 3565 Vol 2](https://www.dau.edu/sites/default/files/Migrated/CopDocuments/NAVSEA%20OP%203565%20Vol%202.pdf), [joint helicopter ops guidance NTTP 3-04M](https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/lemay_center/publication/afttp3-2.4/afttp3-2.4.pdf)). If you want to shrink the zone that forces a tracking radar into standby during flight ops, you frequently have to relax the ordnance constraint, which is exactly the receptor AEGIS cannot compute. The "give back air defense" pitch and the HERO problem are the same sale, and AEGIS solves the wrong half of it.

2. **The HERO coupling problem is a quadratic form in x, so the QCQP transfers, but AEGIS brings only the commodity part.** The Maximum No-Fire Stimulus is defined on the electrical parameters at the electrically-initiated device: current, di/dt, power, voltage, or energy in the firing circuit ([MIL-STD-464D](https://www.waru.edu/sites/default/files/Migrated/CopDocuments/MIL-STD-464D.pdf), [MIL-HDBK-240A](https://quicksearch.dla.mil/Transient/A6D67F5A06FF43A5AFCB8FD5BFCA8351.pdf)). Bridgewire power is quadratic in coupled current, current is linear in coupled voltage, voltage is linear in the incident field, and the field is linear in x, so coupled power is x^H G_ord^H G_ord x and the 16.5 dB margin is a quadratic constraint. Structurally it is the ECBF QCQP. But the channel G_ord is obtained by hardware-in-the-loop measurement of each specific munition's firing circuit in an anechoic chamber per MIL-HDBK-240, not computed from a surface mesh, and an EID buried in a missile is aperture-plus-transmission-line coupling, not Fresnel absorption in a lossy dielectric skin. AEGIS's differentiator, the millisecond surface construction of Q, produces nothing here. Only the optimizer transfers, and any QCQP solver is the optimizer.

3. **AESOP is not what the brief thinks it is, and its successor is a live, funded, incumbent-held program that does not model people.** AESOP is the Afloat Electromagnetic Spectrum Operations Program (not "Automated Electromagnetic System Onboard Ships"), built by SENTEL, and it is being replaced by RTSO, Real-Time Spectrum Operations, Project 3239 under program element 0605866N (Navy Space and Electronic Warfare Support), managed by PMW 120 in PEO C4I ([AESOP-to-RTSO](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/afloat-electromagnetic-spectrum-operations-aesop-rtso-paul), [RTSO budget project 3239](https://dtic.minsky.ai/index/3239_0605866N_6_1319_PB_2023), [PMW 120 tear sheet](https://www.peoc4i.navy.mil/Portals/98/Documents/Tear-Sheets/2023_PMW%20120_Tear%20Sheet.pdf)). Its charter is electromagnetic environmental effects between shipboard transmitters and receivers, spectrum planning, and EMCON for interoperability and counter-detection. It is inter-system EMI, not personnel dosimetry, and there is no human body anywhere in it. This is a real budget line, but AEGIS's exposure operator is orthogonal to what it does.

## 4. Leg by leg

### Leg 1, operational tempo: good story, no distinct buyer, wrong physics dependency

The reframe from safety to capability is rhetorically correct and worth using in a pitch. It is also, on inspection, mostly a mirage as a market.

- **No budget line is called "radar availability during flight ops."** The capability owner is PEO IWS (the combat system and SPY-6, [PEO IWS](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/PEO-IWS/About-PEO-IWS/)) and the ship's warfare commander, but neither holds a procurement line you can sell a keep-out-zone shrinker into. The people who own the artifact you would actually change (the RADHAZ certification and the EMCON schedule) are the safety authorities and the spectrum-management program, and those are the conservative HERP and interoperability worlds again. Reframing the pitch does not change whose signature you need.
- **The binding constraint during flight ops is frequently ordnance, not personnel.** See finding 2.1 above. When a tracking radar goes to standby for a launch or recovery cycle, HERO on the aircraft's munitions and EMI into avionics are usually the governing constraints, and those are tighter and better funded than HERP. Shrinking the zone requires solving the HERO receptor, which AEGIS cannot.
- **The one part that is real and AEGIS-shaped is narrow.** Above 6 GHz, the regulated quantity becomes absorbed power density on skin, exactly what AEGIS computes, and replacing an unperturbed-field keep-out with a body-aware APD keep-out genuinely shrinks the personnel zone. That is Play A from `military_rf_hazard.md`. It is real, but it is HERP's small budget wearing a tempo costume, and it only reclaims the personnel-driven portion of the zone, not the ordnance-driven portion.

Verdict on Leg 1: a true and useful narrative frame, not a distinct market. Do not price against capability and expect a capability buyer to appear. The buyer is still the safety authority.

### Leg 2, HERO with HERP's math: the structure transfers, the company's advantage does not

The orchestrator's prior is correct on the math and wrong on the consequence for AEGIS.

- **Correct:** "maximize radar performance subject to coupling at every ordnance location staying 16.5 dB below MNFS" is the same QCQP as the exposure-constrained beamformer. Verified: MNFS is a firing-circuit power/current/voltage quantity, so coupled stimulus is quadratic in x. If you had G_ord, ECBF re-points at HERO unchanged.
- **Fatal for AEGIS specifically:** you do not have G_ord and you cannot build it with AEGIS's engine. The HERO channel is measured per munition by hardware-in-the-loop chamber test (MIL-HDBK-240), the coupling is into concealed firing circuits (aperture and transmission-line coupling, not skin absorption), and the munition internals are controlled or classified. AEGIS's entire differentiator is the fast surface construction of Q from a mesh and a Fresnel law. That machinery is inert for an EID. What transfers is the QCQP solve, which is a commodity: CVX, a projected subgradient, or an SDR does it, and NSWC Dahlgren already owns the test data that would populate the matrix.
- **The money is real but structurally unreachable.** HERO commands the large budget precisely because it is verified by physical test ($1.2B to $1.6B services segment, versus $300M to $400M software, per `military_rf_hazard.md`), and the authority is NOSSA, the single most conservative actor in the system. A software envelope that shortcuts chamber testing of live ordnance is the last thing that authority will accept from an unproven foreign SME.

Verdict on Leg 2: the math transfers, the moat does not, and the money sits behind the physical-test wall and the most risk-averse safety board in the domain. Mirage for AEGIS.

### Leg 3, EMCON optimization: a real funded program you would subcontract into, and a physics claim that does not hold

- **The Gemini "linear sum is physically wrong" opening is itself mostly wrong.** The cumulative-exposure rule sum_i S_i / S_MPE_i <= 1 is a conservative thermal-additivity bound for incoherent emitters at different frequencies, and for uncorrelated, non-phase-locked sources that is the physically correct conservative treatment, not an error. Coherent addition only matters when emitters share a frequency and a phase reference, which distinct shipboard radars and comms do not. AEGIS's coherent operator Q is exactly the right tool for a single coherent array, and exactly the wrong tool for summing across independent emitters that are incoherent with each other. So the stated technical opening is based on a misdiagnosis, and the one place coherence genuinely matters (a single array) is already Leg 1/Finding A, not a multi-emitter EMCON story.
- **The combinatorial EMCON scheduling problem is real but not AEGIS's.** Which emitters may radiate, in which sectors, at what power, subject to HERP, HERO, HERF, and co-site EMI, is a genuine constrained-optimization problem. But it is dominated by co-site interference and interoperability, where AEGIS contributes nothing, and the exposure term is one small constraint among many. AEGIS would be a bolt-on constraint generator inside someone else's optimizer.
- **The incumbent is entrenched and funded.** RTSO is an active program of record under PMW 120 in PEO C4I, built by SENTEL with support from Vectrus, accredited for CANES and ISNS. You do not displace a program of record as a spin-off. At most you subcontract a module, which means partnering with SENTEL or a prime, not selling a product.

Verdict on Leg 3: real money, wrong owner for AEGIS, and the specific physics opening the brief cites is a misread of conservative incoherent summation. Mirage.

### The leg that was not on the list: directed-energy bioeffects and safety-of-effect certification

The strongest AEGIS-shaped pull I found is outside the Navy HERP/HERO/EMCON triad and inside the Air Force and Joint directed-energy world.

- **AFRL 711 HPW Directed Energy Bioeffects Division** owns HPM and mmWave dosimetry and high-fidelity anatomical body models, and (inferred, not yet tied to an open topic) struggles to run them at mission scale because full-physics FDTD is too slow. That is precisely AEGIS's proposition: a fast surrogate that reproduces the expensive oracle. They have the oracle and the phantoms; they lack the millisecond surrogate.
- **Safety-of-effect certification for fielded non-lethal HPM** is a live gap. Epirus already asserts software-defined safe zones and nulling, but the certification path is an engineering assertion tied to combat-management logic, not a standardized regulatory protocol (Gemini, consistent with the immaturity visible in the open Epirus material). This is squarely in the brief's allowed scope: proving a deployed non-lethal system does not permanently injure bystanders and friendly forces. Finding A (the certifiable λ_max / ‖G̃_t‖² envelope) plus Finding B (VOP regulatory precedent from 7T MRI) is the differentiated instrument for exactly this, and it is a bound rather than a runtime controller, which is what a safety board can accept.
- **The money channel fits a spin-off:** JDETO S&T, AFRL 711 HPW BAAs, and DoD SBIR/STTR (Phase I roughly $0.1M to $0.3M, Phase II roughly $1M to $1.7M) are the willing-to-pay signals here, plus EDF and EDA on the European side. These are small, non-dilutive, research-scale, and match the funding path in the brief. Note the caveat: much of this pull sits at sub-6 GHz (Leonidas L-band, THOR S-band), where AEGIS's planner transfers but its surface-speed trick does not, so the honest wedge is the mmWave slice (95 GHz Active Denial class) plus the certification-envelope instrument, which is band-agnostic because it only needs field linearity in x.

## 5. What would kill this

- **Kills the whole military thesis:** take one shipboard X-band or Ka-band scene, compute the personnel keep-out zone two ways, the industry unperturbed-field-versus-threshold way and the AEGIS body-aware APD way, and measure the area reduction. If no such funded topic exists after a direct search of dodsbirsttr.mil and SAM.gov, the AFRL pull is a hypothesis about a need, not a market, and should be demoted.
