\section*{Setup}

Part III of the monograph gives the coherent body-surface channel
\begin{equation}
  \Gt(\rr) \in \mathbb{C}^{3 \times M},
  \qquad
  \Sab(\rr;\x) = \norm{\Gt(\rr)\x}^2,
\end{equation}
and the whole-body exposure operator
\begin{equation}
  \Q
  = \int_{\Sigmaset} \Gt(\rr)^H \Gt(\rr)\,\diff A,
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{abs}}(\x) = \x^H \Q \x.
\end{equation}
For a user of mass $m$, the whole-body SAR is
\begin{equation}
  \SARwb(\x) = \frac{P_{\mathrm{abs}}(\x)}{m}.
\end{equation}

ICNIRP's mmWave basic restriction is not on $P_{\mathrm{abs}}$ alone. It is on
the absorbed power density averaged over any patch of prescribed area
$A_{\mathrm{avg}}$, here
$A_{\mathrm{avg}}=4\,\mathrm{cm}^2=4\times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}^2$.

\section*{1. Local exposure operators}

Let $\omega_{A}(\rr_0,\rr) \ge 0$ be a normalized patch kernel centered at
$\rr_0 \in \Sigmaset$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\Sigmaset} \omega_A(\rr_0,\rr)\,\diff A = 1,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{supp}\omega_A(\rr_0,\cdot)
  \subseteq \text{a surface patch of area } A.
\end{equation}
Then the local averaged absorbed power density is
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{ab},A}(\rr_0;\x)
  = \int_{\Sigmaset} \omega_A(\rr_0,\rr)\,
    \norm{\Gt(\rr)\x}^2\,\diff A
  = \x^H \Q_A(\rr_0)\x,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  \Q_A(\rr_0)
  = \int_{\Sigmaset} \omega_A(\rr_0,\rr)\,
    \Gt(\rr)^H \Gt(\rr)\,\diff A.
\end{equation}
Each $\Q_A(\rr_0)$ is Hermitian PSD. The whole-body operator is the special
case in which the kernel is uniform on the entire surface.

\paragraph{Discrete AEGIS form.}
On a mesh with triangle index $i=1,\dots,M_\Sigma$, the existing AEGIS
averaging matrix $G_A$ is row-stochastic. If row $m$ contains the weights of
the $A$-patch centered at triangle $m$, then
\begin{equation}
  \Q_{A,m}
  = \sum_{i=1}^{M_\Sigma} (G_A)_{mi}\,
    \Gt_i^H \Gt_i,
  \qquad
  S_{\mathrm{ab},A,m}(\x) = \x^H \Q_{A,m}\x.
\end{equation}
So the ``infinite family'' becomes a finite family of convex quadratic
constraints on the mesh. AEGIS already supplies the weights through its
\texttt{precompute\_averaging\_matrix()} path.

\section*{2. The actual beamforming problem}

With both whole-body SAR and local APD enforced, the natural downlink design is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \max_{\x} \quad & \abs{\hvec^T \x}^2 \\
    \text{subject to} \quad
    & \x^H \Q \x \le m\,\SARwb^{\max}, \\
    & \max_{\rr_0 \in \Sigmaset} \x^H \Q_A(\rr_0)\x \le S_{\mathrm{ab},A}^{\max}, \\
    & \norm{\x}^2 \le P.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:dual-problem}
\end{equation}
In the mesh-discretized AEGIS version this is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \max_{\x} \quad & \abs{\hvec^T \x}^2 \\
    \text{subject to} \quad
    & \x^H \Q \x \le m\,\SARwb^{\max}, \\
    & \x^H \Q_{A,m}\x \le S_{\mathrm{ab},A}^{\max},
      \qquad m=1,\dots,M_\Sigma, \\
    & \norm{\x}^2 \le P.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:dual-problem-discrete}
\end{equation}
It is still a QCQP, but now with one global quadratic constraint and a family
of local quadratic constraints.

\section*{3. When does local APD bind before whole-body SAR?}

Define the hotspot concentration factor
\begin{equation}
  \eta_A(\x)
  \equiv
  \frac{\max_{\rr_0 \in \Sigmaset} S_{\mathrm{ab},A}(\rr_0;\x)}
       {P_{\mathrm{abs}}(\x)/A_\Sigma}
  =
  \frac{A_\Sigma \max_{\rr_0} \x^H \Q_A(\rr_0)\x}
       {\x^H \Q \x},
\end{equation}
where $A_\Sigma$ is the total body surface area. This is the ratio between the
worst local $A$-patch average and the whole-body mean absorbed power density.

The APD constraint is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
  \x^H \Q \x \le \frac{S_{\mathrm{ab},A}^{\max} A_\Sigma}{\eta_A(\x)}.
\end{equation}
Comparing with the SAR constraint $\x^H \Q \x \le m\,\SARwb^{\max}$ gives an
exact switching condition.

\begin{proposition}[APD beats SAR when the hotspot is concentrated enough]
The local APD constraint is tighter than the whole-body SAR constraint for a
given precoder $\x$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
  \eta_A(\x)
  >
  \eta_A^{\mathrm{th}}
  \equiv
  \frac{S_{\mathrm{ab},A}^{\max} A_\Sigma}{m\,\SARwb^{\max}}.
  \label{eq:eta-th}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
APD is tighter iff
\begin{equation}
  \frac{S_{\mathrm{ab},A}^{\max} A_\Sigma}{\eta_A(\x)}
  < m\,\SARwb^{\max}.
\end{equation}
Rearranging gives \eqref{eq:eta-th}.
\end{proof}

For ICNIRP 2020 public exposure at mmWave,
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{ab},4\mathrm{cm}^2}^{\max} = 20~\mathrm{W/m^2},
  \qquad
  \SARwb^{\max} = 0.08~\mathrm{W/kg},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{4\mathrm{cm}^2}^{\mathrm{th}}
  = 250\,\frac{A_\Sigma}{m}.
\end{equation}
Using the monograph's representative body types gives
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Body type & $m$ (kg) & $A_\Sigma$ (m$^2$) & $\eta_{4\mathrm{cm}^2}^{\mathrm{th}}$ \\
\midrule
Adult  & 70 & 1.8 & 6.43 \\
Child  & 20 & 0.8 & 10.0 \\
Infant &  8 & 0.4 & 12.5 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

So an adult needs only about a $6.4\times$ local concentration over the
whole-body mean before the $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ constraint becomes the active
one. This is not a large factor.

\section*{4. Why coherent MIMO can cross that threshold}

The monograph's coherent-hotspot section gives the key scaling law. If $N$
dominant paths are co-phased and co-polarized at a surface point, then
\begin{equation}
  \Sab^{\mathrm{peak}} \propto N^2,
\end{equation}
whereas the incoherent floor and the total absorbed power scale only as
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{abs}} \propto N.
\end{equation}
Therefore, provided the $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ patch is not much larger than the
coherence area of the hotspot,
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{4\mathrm{cm}^2}(\x) = O(N).
\end{equation}
This immediately explains the regime change:
\begin{equation}
  N \gtrsim \eta_{4\mathrm{cm}^2}^{\mathrm{th}}
  \quad \Longrightarrow \quad
  \text{local APD can bind before whole-body SAR.}
\end{equation}
For adults the threshold is around $N \approx 7$ in this idealized equal-path
picture. For infants it is around $N \approx 13$.

\section*{5. AEGIS case study on a real phantom}

I ran one compact AEGIS experiment directly in this repository on the
\texttt{thelonious} phantom:
\begin{itemize}
  \item mesh: \texttt{data/thelonious.stl}, $23{,}826$ triangles;
  \item tissue: \texttt{SKIN\_28GHZ};
  \item propagation: one equal-power path per antenna element, directions placed
        on a $20^\circ$ cone around $-\hat{\mathbf{x}}$;
  \item local operator family: built from AEGIS's existing
        $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ averaging matrix;
  \item beamformer for each $N$: dominant eigenvector of the worst patch
        operator $\Q_{4\mathrm{cm}^2,m}$.
\end{itemize}

The table reports
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{4\mathrm{cm}^2}
  =
  \frac{\max_m \x^H \Q_{4\mathrm{cm}^2,m}\x}
       {(\x^H \Q \x)/A_\Sigma}.
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
Elements $N$ & peak $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ APD & total $P_{\mathrm{abs}}$ & $\eta_{4\mathrm{cm}^2}$ \\
& (W/m$^2$ per unit precoder power) & (W per unit precoder power) & \\
\midrule
1  & 0.539 & 0.105 & 4.05 \\
2  & 0.930 & 0.140 & 5.23 \\
4  & 0.930 & 0.140 & 5.23 \\
8  & 1.886 & 0.121 & 12.26 \\
16 & 3.756 & 0.126 & 23.51 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two facts matter.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The whole-body absorbed power stays in the narrow range
        $0.10$--$0.14$ W per unit precoder power. It does not blow up with $N$.
  \item The worst local $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ operator does blow up. By $N=8$ the
        concentration factor is already $12.3\times$, which is above the adult
        and child thresholds in \eqref{eq:eta-th}. By $N=16$ it is
        $23.5\times$, well above all three representative thresholds.
\end{enumerate}

So the answer to the original intuition is yes: coherent MIMO can create a
regime in which a local APD limit becomes the active constraint even though the
whole-body SAR budget still looks comfortable.

\section*{6. Relation to Zhou et al.}

Zhou et al. (arXiv:2601.19587, 2026) formulate a different but complementary
problem. Their abstract says they convert instantaneous exposure limits into a
long-term thermal budget using a Pennes-based impulse response and then solve an
online beamforming policy under that thermal state constraint. The present note
is upstream of that. Before any temporal filtering, the instantaneous spatial
constraint is already not a single operator $\Q$ but a family
$\{\Q_A(\rr_0)\}_{\rr_0 \in \Sigma}$.

\begin{remark}
If one wanted to combine the two viewpoints, the natural state variable would
not be driven by $P_{\mathrm{abs}}$ alone. It would be driven by the
worst-patch local APD, or by a small set of thermally relevant patch states.
That is a different control problem from the ECBF QCQP, but it starts from the
same local operator family defined above.
\end{remark}

\section*{Bottom line}

The right mathematical object for direction 4 is not one exposure matrix but a
mesh-indexed family of local exposure operators. Once that family is written
down, the binding-condition calculation is simple:
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{4\mathrm{cm}^2} > 250\,\frac{A_\Sigma}{m}
  \quad \Longrightarrow \quad
  \text{local APD binds before whole-body SAR.}
\end{equation}
Because coherent hotspots raise local APD like $N^2$ while whole-body
absorption stays $O(N)$, that crossover happens at modest array sizes.
