You asked for two things at the dose-model meeting. A way to correct a normalized
SAR value when the phone sits at a different distance. And an uncertainty estimate
for each value, like the \SI{2.62}{W\per kg\per W} brain figure for Duke in the
front-of-eyes scenario. Both are ready. This note has the short version. The
numbers and tables are in the attached workbook \texttt{nearfield\_dose\_results.xlsx},
and the figures here are also in the email.

The nice part: we no longer hand you one number per scenario. We hand you the
whole picture. How the value moves with distance, and how much it spreads.

\section*{A quick word on the method}

We switched the dosimetry to a new in-house method. I will keep this light, partly
because it is under patent and partly because the physics is not the point for you.
Two things matter.

It works on the body \emph{surface} rather than the full \SI{}{3D} tissue volume.
Think of it as a fast \SI{}{2D} skin model instead of a full-wave solve.

And it is fast. A full-wave (FDTD) run gives you one number for one phone position
and one orientation, and it takes hours. Ours runs thousands of positions and
orientations in minutes. So the distance curve and the orientation spread come
almost for free. We calibrate it against the full-wave reference, so the absolute
level stays anchored to what you already trust.

Figure~\ref{fig:geometry} shows the three phone placements on the Duke model.
Figure~\ref{fig:apd} shows what one run produces: the absorbed power painted over
the skin. The patch of face nearest the phone takes most of the dose.

\section*{1. Distance correction}

Near the body the dose follows a simple inverse-square law with a small offset:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:dist}
  \mathrm{SAR}(d) = \mathrm{SAR}(d_{\mathrm{ref}})\,
  \left(\frac{d_{\mathrm{ref}}+\delta}{d+\delta}\right)^{2}.
\end{equation}
Here $d$ is the phone-to-body distance, $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the distance the
value was computed at (\SI{200}{mm} for front of eyes), and $\delta$ is a small
near-field offset of about \SI{6}{mm}. Beyond a few centimetres this is just the
textbook inverse-square rule. Halve the distance and the dose roughly quadruples.
The fit to our runs is tight: $R^2 > 0.999$ across every band and placement.

\section*{2. Uncertainty}

For each value we now give the mean, standard deviation, min, max, and the
\SIrange{5}{95}{} percentiles. For a fixed body the main driver is how the phone is
held, its orientation. We sample that with a large set of random orientations.

For the Duke brain value the orientation spread is about \SI{27}{\percent} (one
standard deviation). So
\[
  \SI{2.62}{W\per kg\per W} \;\longrightarrow\; 2.6 \pm 0.7~\si{W\per kg\per W},
\]
and over all orientations the full range runs from roughly \SI{0.8}{} to
\SI{3.8}{W\per kg\per W}. Worth noting: the straight-at-the-head pose is not the
worst case, so the spread is not symmetric.

Body model is a second axis. Across the four models (Duke, Ella, Eartha,
Thelonious) the value shifts by a further \SIrange{30}{40}{\percent}. Smaller
bodies absorb more per kilogram, so a child model sits above the adult. The two
axes add in quadrature to about \SI{45}{\percent} total. Figure~\ref{fig:unc}
shows the orientation spread per placement. The per-scenario tables are in the
workbook (sheets \texttt{unc\_*}).

\section*{A few honest caveats}

The very closest distances (under a couple of centimetres) are an upper bound. The
phone is no longer a clean point at that range, so read those as conservative.

The brain number itself is still a volume quantity from your full-wave model. We do
not recompute the brain. What we do is take the distance scaling and the spread,
which are set by the geometry between phone and body, and carry them onto your
\SI{2.62}{} value. That transfer is the defensible part.

The \SI{700}{} and \SI{835}{MHz} antennas sit off-resonance in free space, so their
absolute level is unreliable. The \emph{shape} of the distance and orientation
behaviour is still sound, which is all the distance law and the uncertainty need.

Happy to walk through any of it. And if you want the same treatment for another
tissue, band, or scenario, we can turn it around fast now.
