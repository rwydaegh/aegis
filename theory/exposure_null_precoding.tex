\begin{abstract}
This note develops the downlink half of the AEGIS beamforming story,
specifically the case that matters when the regulator is not ICNIRP
but a city. We start from the empirical fact that Brussels has halted
5G once already, over a decade sat at 6\,V/m, and now caps outdoor
exposure at 14.57\,V/m cumulative across all operators. This is the
regime where a single macro cell's EIRP genuinely interacts with the
limit, where zero-forcing against interference is no longer enough,
and where body geometry is load-bearing for compliance rather than
decorative. We formulate the multi-body exposure-constrained precoding
problem, present a Lagrangian closed form that generalises
Hochwald--Ying to arbitrarily many bodies, show that the feasible set
is the intersection of ellipsoids whose joint null space is the
``quiet'' subspace of the twin, and give a pose-worst-case bound via
the Löwner semidefinite order. We close on the multi-operator
aggregation that Brussels actually enforces, and on why body-centric
bounding replaces traditional exclusion zones in a digital-twin
setting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Where this fits}
% ============================================================

The monograph's Part~III introduces the exposure operator
$\Q=\int_\Sigma\Gtil^H\Gtil\,\diff A$ and the closed-form ECBF
precoder $\xx^\star\propto(\lambda\Q+\nu\ident)^{-1}\hh^*$
(cf.~\cite{Ying2015}).  That solution treats one body, one user, one
constraint. This note extends in three directions that the Brussels
setting demands:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item Many bodies: users who also act as exposure targets, plus
      non-users who contribute nothing to the utility but everything
      to compliance.
\item Many operators: the regulator's constraint is cumulative over
      all sources in the same cell.
\item Many poses per body: the twin knows pose approximately;
      compliance has to survive the residual uncertainty.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting object is a multi-constraint QCQP whose feasibility
geometry, worst-case robustification, and solution structure are the
subject of this note.

% ============================================================
\section{The Brussels setting}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Numbers}

The Brussels-Capital Region imposes a cumulative public exposure
limit expressed as a reference field strength. Recent values, from
Bruxelles Environnement:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Regime                   & outdoor          & indoor          \\
\midrule
2007 ordinance (900\,MHz)& $3.0$\,V/m       & --              \\
2014 raise (4G)          & $6.0$\,V/m       & --              \\
2024 raise (5G)          & $14.57$\,V/m     & $9.19$\,V/m     \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Field strength scales with $\sqrt{f}$ in the ordinance's reference
formula, so at 3.6\,GHz the caps become roughly $29$\,V/m outdoor and
$18$\,V/m indoor; power density at 3.6\,GHz is about
$2.2$\,W/m$^{2}$ outdoor. At 26\,GHz (if ever deployed under the
same rule) the outdoor power density cap would sit around
$16$\,W/m$^{2}$, still far below ICNIRP's 100\,W/m$^{2}$ and
dramatically below mmWave free-space EIRP values for a colocated
8$\times$8 panel. The operational consequence is that a single
Ericsson-class 55\,dBm EIRP site at 26\,GHz has a non-trivial
ordinance-margin, and multiple such sites from different operators
compete for the same budget.

Key feature the ordinance encodes, and the one that makes it
mathematically interesting: the limit is a \emph{sum} across all
transmitting sources in the cell. If Proximus radiates $S_1$ and
Orange radiates $S_2$ at a publicly accessible point, Bruxelles
Environnement checks $S_1+S_2+\cdots \le S_{\mathrm{lim}}$.
Per-operator budgets are allocated administratively.

\subsection{Why this matters for a DT paper}

In most ECBF literature, compliance never binds. ICNIRP outdoor limits
at mmWave are $100$\,W/m$^{2}$ and whole-body SAR limits assume a
body touching the antenna or nearly so. A building-mounted BS
radiating at 55\,dBm EIRP, serving users tens of metres away, does
not threaten ICNIRP at any location a pedestrian can stand.
Consequently, prior ECBF work has to invent the scenario in which the
constraint is tight: a handset to the ear, a body a few centimetres
from an array, a UE clipped to a worn device. These are real
scenarios, but they are all uplink or proximate.

Brussels flips this. The ordinance is tight enough that downlink
compliance at the coverage edge of a macro cell becomes a binding
design variable. A regulator-imposed cap, denser than ICNIRP by
roughly an order of magnitude in field strength and shared among
operators, is exactly the hook that a network-scale digital twin has
to clear. The paper's pitch: show that a body-aware twin buys back
the capacity that a field-strength exclusion-zone approach concedes.

\subsection{Exclusion zones vs.\ body-centric compliance}

The current regulatory verification method in Brussels is
simulation-based: operators submit antenna patterns and site
parameters, software computes free-space power density on a grid of
publicly accessible points, permits are issued if and only if the
worst point over a 200\,m radius is below the cap. This is an
\emph{exclusion-zone} approach: compliance is a statement about
every free-space point, independent of whether anybody is actually
there.

The twin-based alternative is compliance on \emph{bodies that exist}.
A body DT (pose, position) produces an exposure operator
$\Q^{(u)}$; the precoder is constrained by
$\sum_k\ww_k^H\Q^{(u)}\ww_k\le L_u$ for each body $u$. Free-space
field strength at empty locations is irrelevant. The regulator-facing
statement is: for every body we know about, absorbed-power density
stays under a limit by construction. If the operator can establish
that ``every body we know about'' is a faithful surrogate for
``every body that could be there'', the body-centric approach is
simultaneously safer (directly addresses the absorbed-power quantity
the regulator cares about) and less conservative (does not waste
capacity on unoccupied free space). This surrogate question is what
\cref{sec:worstcase} addresses.

\section{Setup and notation}\label{sec:setup}
% ============================================================

Fix an array of $M$ BS antenna elements. Let $\Bodies=\Users\cup\NonUsers$
be the set of bodies in the coverage region, with $\Users$ the served
user set ($|\Users|=K$) and $\NonUsers$ the non-user set
($|\NonUsers|=B$). Each body $u\in\Bodies$ has surface $\Sigma^{(u)}$
and, at its current pose $\theta_u$, a Fresnel-filtered surface
channel $\Gtil^{(u)}(\rr;\theta_u)\in\Complex^{3\times M}$ exactly as
defined in the monograph (\cref{sec:coherent-absorption-law} there).

Two operators per body are relevant:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item The whole-body exposure operator
$\Q^{(u)}=\int_{\Sigma^{(u)}}\Gtil^{(u),H}\Gtil^{(u)}\,\diff A
 \in\Complex^{M\times M}$, Hermitian PSD, rank $\le\min(M,3M_\triangle^{(u)})$;
\item The local (4\,cm$^2$) family $\{\Q^{(u)}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\rr_0)\}_{\rr_0\in\Sigma^{(u)}}$
for APD compliance (cf.~direction 4, \cref{sec:local-Q} there).
\end{itemize}

Each user $k\in\Users$ has a narrowband channel
$\hh_k\in\Complex^M$ at its device; the BS transmits
$\xx(t)=\sum_k\ww_k\,s_k(t)$ with
$\mathbb{E}[|s_k|^2]=1$, $s_k$ uncorrelated across $k$. Total Tx
power is $\Ptx=\sum_k\|\ww_k\|^2$. The expected absorbed power on
body $u$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pabs-u}
  \Pabs^{(u)}
  \;=\;\sum_{k=1}^K \ww_k^H\,\Q^{(u)}\,\ww_k
  \;=\;\tr\!\bigl(\Q^{(u)}\W\bigr),
  \qquad \W:=\sum_k\ww_k\ww_k^H,
\end{equation}
with $\W\succeq\zero$, $\tr\W=\Ptx$, $\rank\W\le K$. The expression
$\tr(\Q^{(u)}\W)$ is linear in $\W$, which is the key algebraic fact
for everything that follows.

\paragraph{Limits.} Let $L^{(u)}$ denote the absorbed-power budget on
body $u$. For a user $u\in\Users$ we use the self-exposure limit
(ICNIRP or the tighter of ICNIRP and any Brussels-style operator
allocation). For a non-user $u\in\NonUsers$ we use the public limit.
In the single-operator case, $L^{(u)}$ is an absorbed-power reading
of the Brussels incident-PD cap, which we justify in
\cref{sec:exclusion} below.

% ============================================================
\section{The multi-body QCQP}\label{sec:qcqp}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Problem statement}

Sum-rate under per-body exposure and per-antenna / total power:
\begin{empheq}[box=\tcbhighmath]{equation}\label{eq:P-MB}
\begin{aligned}
(\text{P-MB})\quad
\max_{\{\ww_k\}}\;&
  \sum_{k=1}^K \log_2\!\Bigl(1+\frac{|\hh_k^H\ww_k|^2}
    {\sum_{k'\ne k}|\hh_k^H\ww_{k'}|^2+\sigma_k^2}\Bigr)\\
\text{s.t.}\;&
  \sum_{k=1}^K \ww_k^H\Q^{(u)}\ww_k\le L^{(u)},
  \quad u\in\Users\cup\NonUsers,\\
&\sum_{k=1}^K\|\ww_k\|^2\le P.
\end{aligned}
\end{empheq}

Relative to single-user ECBF (monograph Part~III, \cref{sec:ecbf}),
the only change is that the single exposure constraint is replaced by
a family of $K+B$ exposure constraints, one per body. The power
constraint remains a single scalar inequality.

\subsection{Lifting to $\W$}

Write the MU-MIMO SINR as
$|\hh_k^H\ww_k|^2 = \hh_k^H\ww_k\ww_k^H\hh_k$. The exposure
constraints are linear in
$\W=\sum_k\ww_k\ww_k^H\succeq\zero$ with $\rank\W\le K$. The
semidefinite relaxation of \eqref{eq:P-MB} replaces the sum-rate by
a concave surrogate (WMMSE, MSE-to-SINR duality~\cite{Christensen2008})
that turns the problem into a sequence of
linearly-constrained matrix programs. Each subproblem has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W-SDP}
  \min_{\W\succeq\zero}\;
  \tr(\mathbf{A}\W) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
  \tr(\Q^{(u)}\W)\le L^{(u)}\ \forall u,\quad \tr\W\le P,
\end{equation}
for some $\mathbf{A}\succeq\zero$ fixed per outer iteration.
\eqref{eq:W-SDP} is a standard SDP and its solution lies in the
low-dimensional face of the PSD cone defined by the active
constraints.

\subsection{Lagrangian and closed form}

The Lagrangian of \eqref{eq:P-MB} attaches a multiplier $\lambda_u\ge0$
to each exposure constraint and $\nu\ge0$ to the power constraint.
The KKT stationarity condition in $\ww_k$ reads, for a fixed outer
MMSE surrogate,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KKT-w}
  \Bigl(\sum_u \lambda_u\,\Q^{(u)} + \nu\,\ident\Bigr)\ww_k^\star
  \;=\;\mathbf{g}_k(\{\ww_{k'}^\star\}_{k'\ne k}),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{g}_k$ collects the intra-user signal and
inter-user interference terms from the WMMSE surrogate. Defining the
\emph{aggregate exposure operator}
\begin{empheq}[box=\tcbhighmath]{equation}\label{eq:Qtot}
  \Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\lambda) \;:=\; \sum_{u\in\Bodies}\lambda_u\,\Q^{(u)}
  \;\in\;\Complex^{M\times M},
\end{empheq}
the per-user precoder takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ww-form}
  \ww_k^\star \;=\; \bigl(\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\lambda)+\nu\ident\bigr)^{-1}
    \mathbf{g}_k,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda,\nu$ solving the complementary-slackness system over
all active exposure and power constraints.
\eqref{eq:ww-form} is the natural many-body generalisation of
Ying~et~al.'s single-body closed form
$\ww^\star\propto(\lambda\Q+\nu\ident)^{-1}\hh^*$:
all bodies enter through the weighted sum $\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}$, and
the body-specific Lagrange multipliers record which body is actually
binding.

\begin{remark}[S-procedure in the single-stream case]
For $K=1$ the MU-MIMO SINR reduces to a single Rayleigh quotient and
\eqref{eq:P-MB} is a QCQP with $1+B$ quadratic constraints. The
S-procedure gives a global optimum in closed form when $B\le 1$
(\cite{Boyd2004,Polik2007}). Beyond that, strong duality is not
guaranteed in general, but the SDP relaxation
\eqref{eq:W-SDP} is exact when the rank constraint on $\W$ is
inactive, which is the generic case for moderate $B$.
\end{remark}

\section{Exposure-null precoding: the ZF generalisation}\label{sec:null}
% ============================================================

Classical zero-forcing precoding for $K$ users with channel vectors
$\{\hh_k\}$ chooses $\ww_k$ to lie in the joint null space of
$\{\hh_j\}_{j\ne k}$, which enforces zero inter-user interference.
Algebraically it is the special case of \eqref{eq:P-MB} in which the
``exposure operator'' of user $j\ne k$ is $\hh_j\hh_j^H$ (rank one)
and the limit is zero.

Exposure-null precoding is the generalisation: for each body $u$ the
operator $\Q^{(u)}$ is PSD of rank $\le 3M_\triangle^{(u)}$, and the
limit $L^{(u)}$ is a positive budget rather than zero.

\subsection{Hard nulls: the quiet subspace}

\begin{definition}[Quiet subspace]\label{def:quiet}
The quiet subspace of the body set $\Bodies$ is
\[
  \mathcal{Q}(\Bodies)\;:=\;\bigcap_{u\in\Bodies}\ker\Q^{(u)}
  \;\subseteq\;\Complex^M.
\]
\end{definition}

Any precoder $\ww\in\mathcal{Q}(\Bodies)$ satisfies
$\ww^H\Q^{(u)}\ww=0$ for every body, i.e.\ every body in the twin
receives zero expected absorbed power. This is not zero
\emph{electric field} at the body; it is zero integrated
absorption, which is strictly weaker. In pseudo-Brewster regime
(cf.~\texttt{q\_complement.tex}), zero absorption also implies zero
scattered power, hence $\ww\in\mathcal{Q}(\Bodies)$ is in fact
``invisible'' to each body from both absorption and sensing
perspectives, to the approximation of that regime.

\begin{proposition}[Dimension bound]\label{prop:dim}
Let $r_u=\rank\Q^{(u)}\le 3M_\triangle^{(u)}$. Then
\[
  \dim\mathcal{Q}(\Bodies)\;\ge\;\max\!\Bigl(0,\;
     M-\textstyle\sum_u r_u\Bigr).
\]
In particular, if $M$ exceeds the total number of independent
body-incident polarised paths summed across bodies, the quiet
subspace is nontrivial.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\ker\Q^{(u)}\supseteq\ker\Gtil^{(u)}$, and
$\dim\ker\Gtil^{(u)}\ge M-3M_\triangle^{(u)}$ by rank-nullity.
The intersection of $B$ subspaces of dimensions $d_u=M-r_u$ has
dimension at least $M-\sum_u r_u$ when the sum is nonnegative, by
the subspace dimension formula applied inductively.
\end{proof}

The bound is loose: real $\Q^{(u)}$ operators are typically rank
$\ll 3M_\triangle$ because path-visibility and BS-array geometry
correlate heavily across antenna elements. A useful empirical
quantity is the $\varepsilon$-effective rank
$r_u^\varepsilon=|\{k:\lambda_k(\Q^{(u)})\ge\varepsilon\lambda_1(\Q^{(u)})\}|$,
which on typical phantoms at $M=64$, 28\,GHz and $5\,\mathrm{m}$ range
sits at roughly $8$--$16$, far below $3M_\triangle\sim 10^4$.

\subsection{Soft nulls: the $\varepsilon$-budget subspace}

In practice one wants absorbed power \emph{below a budget}, not zero.
Let
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Q}_\varepsilon(\Bodies) \;:=\;
  \bigl\{\ww\in\Complex^M:\;
    \ww^H\Q^{(u)}\ww\le\varepsilon\lambda_1(\Q^{(u)})\,\|\ww\|^2,
    \forall u\in\Bodies\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
This is not a subspace (it is a cone after $\ell_2$ normalisation),
but its projection onto the unit sphere is the feasible direction set
for any precoder satisfying the exposure constraints at a uniform
relative severity $\varepsilon$ across bodies.

\begin{remark}[Geometric picture]
Each $\{\ww:\ww^H\Q^{(u)}\ww\le L^{(u)}\}$ is an ellipsoid with axes
along the eigenvectors of $\Q^{(u)}$. The feasible set of
\eqref{eq:P-MB} is the intersection of $K+B$ such ellipsoids with
the unit ball $\{\ww:\|\ww\|^2\le P\}$. When bodies have
well-separated exposure modes (users spread over the plaza, bystanders
scattered), the intersection is large. When bodies cluster (a group
of pedestrians near the user), the intersection collapses rapidly
with $\varepsilon\to 0$. The paper's experimental axis is exactly
this clustering: Brussels plazas at rush hour versus off-peak.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Connection to classical ZF and MMSE}

Set $\Q^{(j)}=\hh_j\hh_j^H$ (one operator per ``body'', rank one,
aligned with that user's channel). Then
$\ww_k^H\Q^{(j)}\ww_k=|\hh_j^H\ww_k|^2$, and enforcing
$\ww_k^H\Q^{(j)}\ww_k=0$ for all $j\ne k$ recovers classical ZF.
The aggregate operator from \eqref{eq:Qtot} becomes
\[
  \Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\lambda)
  \;=\;\sum_{j\ne k}\lambda_j\hh_j\hh_j^H
  \;=\;\H_{-k}^H\,\mathrm{diag}(\lambda)\,\H_{-k},
\]
which at $\lambda_j\equiv\sigma^2/P$ is the classical MMSE
regularisation. Exposure-null precoding is therefore a strict
generalisation: classical multi-user precoders are the degenerate
limit where every ``body'' is a rank-one channel and every ``limit''
is zero or proportional to noise.

What changes physically when $\Q^{(u)}$ is not rank-one: the
``direction'' of the constraint is not a vector but a subspace of
$\Complex^M$, weighted by eigenvalues that encode how much of body
$u$ each precoder direction illuminates. Classical ZF nulls a line;
exposure-null precoding nulls (or budgets) a geometric object with
$O(\text{visible body surface})$ intrinsic directions.

% ============================================================
\section{Worst-case pose: Löwner envelope}\label{sec:worstcase}
% ============================================================

The operator $\Q^{(u)}$ depends on body $u$'s pose $\theta_u$.
Pose estimation from IMU or from any other channel is imperfect. A
compliance claim has to survive that imperfection. The cleanest way
is to bound absorbed power by a \emph{pose-robust envelope} that
dominates $\Q^{(u)}(\theta)$ for every pose the body could physically
be in.

\subsection{Pose dictionaries}

Let $\Poses_u\subset\Reals^{p}$ be a pose set for body $u$. Three
plausible choices, increasingly conservative:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Point estimate.} $\Poses_u=\{\hat\theta_u\}$, a single
estimate from the IMU pipeline. Not robust.
\item \textbf{Uncertainty ball.}
$\Poses_u=\{\theta:\|\theta-\hat\theta_u\|\le\delta_u\}$, a metric
ball around the estimate with radius $\delta_u$ from the sensor-fusion
covariance. The useful case for small residual error.
\item \textbf{Activity dictionary.}
$\Poses_u=\{\theta^{(a)}\}_{a\in A}$, a finite subset of AMASS or
AMASS-derived prototypical poses consistent with the activity class
(walking, standing, sitting). This is what you use when the pose is
unknown but the activity is known from context (``person on sidewalk
during business hours'').
\item \textbf{Anatomical envelope.} $\Poses_u=\Poses_{\mathrm{all}}$,
the full physically-admissible pose manifold of a human body. This is
what a regulator defending the ordinance would want if you give them
no information about pose at all.
\end{enumerate}

Any of these produces a family
$\{\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\}_{\theta\in\Poses_u}$ of Hermitian PSD matrices.
The question is how to reduce the family to a single operator that
controls the constraint.

\subsection{Scalar worst case}

Given a candidate precoder $\ww$, the scalar worst-case absorbed
power on body $u$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-wc}
  \overline{\Pabs}^{(u)}(\ww)
  \;:=\;\sup_{\theta\in\Poses_u}
  \ww^H\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\ww.
\end{equation}
This is well-defined for compact $\Poses_u$ and continuous
$\theta\mapsto\Q^{(u)}(\theta)$, both of which hold for SMPL pose
(continuous + compact manifold after box constraints on joint
angles). Constraining \eqref{eq:scalar-wc} by $L^{(u)}$ is the sharp
robust formulation:
\[
  \sup_{\theta\in\Poses_u}\ww^H\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\ww \le L^{(u)}.
\]
The trouble is that this is \emph{not} a convex quadratic constraint
in $\ww$: it is a pointwise supremum of convex constraints, so the
feasible set is an intersection of ellipsoids indexed by $\theta$,
convex, but with potentially infinitely many active $\theta$.

\subsection{Löwner envelope}

A tighter-in-structure, looser-in-value approach: replace the family
by a single operator that dominates every member in the Löwner
(PSD-semidefinite) order.

\begin{definition}[Löwner envelope]\label{def:loewner}
A Löwner envelope of $\{\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\}_{\theta\in\Poses_u}$ is
any Hermitian matrix $\Qbar^{(u)}$ satisfying
\[
  \Qbar^{(u)}\;\succeq\;\Q^{(u)}(\theta)
  \qquad\text{for all }\theta\in\Poses_u.
\]
The \emph{tightest} envelope, call it $\Qbar^{(u)}_\star$, is the
unique solution of
\[
  \min_{\mathbf{M}\succeq\zero}\;\tr\mathbf{M}
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
  \mathbf{M}\succeq\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\ \forall\theta\in\Poses_u.
\]
\end{definition}

For a finite dictionary $\Poses_u=\{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_T\}$, this
is a semidefinite program in the $M\times M$ Hermitian cone with $T$
LMI constraints, tractable at typical $M=64$ and $T\le 100$.

\begin{proposition}[Robust constraint via envelope]\label{prop:envelope}
If $\Qbar^{(u)}\succeq\Q^{(u)}(\theta)$ for all $\theta\in\Poses_u$,
then the robust constraint
$\sup_\theta\ww^H\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\ww\le L^{(u)}$ is implied by the
single convex quadratic constraint
\[
  \ww^H\Qbar^{(u)}\ww\le L^{(u)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\ww^H\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\ww\le\ww^H\Qbar^{(u)}\ww$ by the Löwner
ordering, for every $\theta$ and every $\ww$; hence the supremum
over $\theta$ is bounded by the right-hand side.
\end{proof}

The envelope replaces the infinite family of ellipsoids by a single
ellipsoid, at the cost of shrinking the feasible set. The tightness
of that shrinkage is exactly the Löwner slack
$\sup_\theta(\Qbar^{(u)}-\Q^{(u)}(\theta))$, which is PSD by
construction. The paper's empirical question is how small this slack
is for realistic pose dictionaries.

\subsection{Cheap envelopes and their Löwner slack}

Three constructions, in order of cheapness:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Trace envelope.}
$\Qbar^{(u)}_{\tr}=\bigl(\sup_\theta\tr\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\bigr)\ident/M$.
Isotropic, loose, trivially computable. Bounds every direction by the
worst scalar absorption.
\item \textbf{Pointwise max.}
$\Qbar^{(u)}_{\max}=\sum_{\theta\in\Poses_u}\mathbf{P}_\theta$,
where $\mathbf{P}_\theta$ projects onto the top eigenvectors of
$\Q^{(u)}(\theta)$. Not a Löwner envelope in general but a useful
heuristic.
\item \textbf{SDP envelope.} $\Qbar^{(u)}_\star$ from
\cref{def:loewner}. Tight.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{conjecture}[Structural low-rank Löwner envelope]\label{conj:lowrank}
For a body in a fixed location illuminated by a BS panel of modest
aperture, the SDP envelope $\Qbar^{(u)}_\star$ over an activity
dictionary has numerical rank close to the pointwise rank of a
typical $\Q^{(u)}(\theta)$, roughly 8--16 at $M=64$ and 28\,GHz. That
is: the envelope is not a dimension-inflated object; it is the same
kind of operator as its members. Reason to believe: the dominant
modes of $\Q^{(u)}(\theta)$ correspond to the body's silhouette from
the BS, which changes slowly across activity variations.
\end{conjecture}

If the conjecture holds, worst-case-pose robustification is
essentially free: the feasible set shrinks only modestly, yet the
compliance statement becomes pose-independent.

\subsection{Regulatory implication}

\begin{empheq}[box=\tcbhighmath]{align}
\text{Claim:}\quad
& \text{For every }u\in\Bodies,\ \theta\in\Poses_u,\notag\\
& \sum_k\ww_k^H\Q^{(u)}(\theta)\ww_k
    \le L^{(u)}.
\end{empheq}

This is a statement the BS can prove to the regulator without
knowing the actual pose, provided $\Poses_u$ covers plausible poses.
That is the thing an ordinance-level regulator wants to see and that
a free-space exclusion-zone method cannot produce: a guarantee of
absorbed-power compliance, on bodies, independent of pose, evaluated
against a known dictionary.

\section{Multi-operator aggregation}\label{sec:multiop}
% ============================================================

Brussels enforces the cumulative sum over all operators $o\in\Ops$.
In absorbed-power terms, the regulator's constraint per body is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coop}
  \sum_{o\in\Ops}\Pabs^{(u,o)}\le L^{(u)},
\end{equation}
with $\Pabs^{(u,o)}$ the absorbed power on body $u$ produced by
operator $o$'s precoder. Two regimes matter:

\paragraph{Non-cooperative.} Each operator $o$ has an allocated
budget $L^{(u,o)}=\alpha_o L^{(u)}$ with $\sum_o\alpha_o=1$, chosen
administratively (in Brussels historically $\alpha_o=1/4$ across four
operators). Operator $o$ optimises \eqref{eq:P-MB} with $L^{(u,o)}$
and stops there. This is what current Brussels-Environnement permits
effectively assume.

\paragraph{Cooperative.} All operators share twin information via a
neutral coordinator (e.g., the regulator or an MNO-neutral DT
service). The joint problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint}
  \max_{\{\ww_k^{(o)}\}}\;\sum_{o\in\Ops} U^{(o)}
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
  \sum_o \sum_k (\ww_k^{(o)})^H\Q^{(u,o)}\ww_k^{(o)}
  \le L^{(u)}\ \forall u,
\end{equation}
plus per-operator power budgets. Here $\Q^{(u,o)}$ is the exposure
operator of body $u$ as seen from operator $o$'s BS array, which is
generally different from $\Q^{(u,o')}$ because the arrays are at
different locations. Per-user utility $U^{(o)}$ is each operator's
own sum-rate; the overall objective is utilitarian.

\subsection{Cooperation gap}

\begin{proposition}[Cooperation is never worse]\label{prop:coop}
Let $U_{\mathrm{NC}}$ be the sum of non-cooperative operator
utilities (each solving its own \eqref{eq:P-MB} with administrative
budget $\alpha_o L^{(u)}$), and $U_{\mathrm{C}}$ the cooperative
optimum \eqref{eq:joint} over $\{\alpha_o\}$. Then
$U_{\mathrm{C}}\ge U_{\mathrm{NC}}$, with equality iff the
administrative split is Pareto-optimal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The non-cooperative feasible set with fixed $\alpha$ is a subset of
the joint feasible set, by construction. Taking the sup of the
objective on a superset is never worse. Strict inequality arises
whenever the administrative split is inefficient for the current
scene (e.g., one operator has a small serving set and spare exposure
budget that could fund another operator's bigger set).
\end{proof}

The paper's narrative angle: Brussels's current permit architecture
(per-operator quotas) is strictly suboptimal for aggregate sum-rate.
A DT-based neutral coordinator, which is exactly the object the DTN
SI imagines, recovers the cooperation gap. This gives the twin a
role that no link-level paper can reproduce.

\subsection{Aggregation as a quadratic form}

A clean observation. Stack per-operator precoders into
$\overline{\ww}_k = (\ww_k^{(1)T},\ldots,\ww_k^{(|\Ops|)T})^T
\in\Complex^{M|\Ops|}$, and define the block-diagonal aggregate
\[
  \overline{\Q}^{(u)}
  \;=\;\mathrm{blkdiag}\bigl(\Q^{(u,1)},\ldots,\Q^{(u,|\Ops|)}\bigr)
  \;\in\;\Complex^{M|\Ops|\times M|\Ops|}.
\]
Then
$\sum_o\sum_k(\ww_k^{(o)})^H\Q^{(u,o)}\ww_k^{(o)}
=\sum_k\overline{\ww}_k^H\overline{\Q}^{(u)}\overline{\ww}_k$.
The cooperative problem is structurally identical to \eqref{eq:P-MB}
in the enlarged antenna space of $M|\Ops|$ elements. Algorithms that
solve \eqref{eq:P-MB} solve \eqref{eq:joint} by lifting to
$\overline{\ww}_k$; per-operator power constraints become separate
block-diagonal constraints.

\section{Exclusion zones, surrogates, and the twin}\label{sec:exclusion}
% ============================================================

\subsection{From incident PD to absorbed power, and back}

Brussels measures incident power density $\Sinc(\rr)$, not absorbed
power. But at FR2 the two are equivalent at a fixed point up to a
Fresnel transmittance: $\Sab(\rr)=T_0\,\Sinc(\rr)\,[\mu(\rr)]_+$ for a
single wave, and the pseudo-Brewster compensation makes $T_0$ nearly
angle-independent (monograph Part~I). Hence
$\int_\Sigma\Sab\,\diff A \approx T_0 \int_\Sigma \Sinc\,[\mu]_+\,\diff A$
for a body surface, and the two quantities are linked by the same
geometric factor the monograph calls the absorption area $A_{\mathrm{ab}}$.

\begin{proposition}[Body-specific incident-PD bound]
For a body with surface $\Sigma^{(u)}$, exposure fraction
$\eta^{(u)}(\rr)$, and incident Poynting flux $\Sinc(\rr)$ arising
from all operators summed incoherently,
\[
  \Pabs^{(u)}
  \;\le\;T_0 \int_{\Sigma^{(u)}} \eta^{(u)}(\rr)\,\Sinc(\rr)\,\diff A.
\]
If the regulator enforces
$\Sinc(\rr)\le S_{\mathrm{lim}}$ pointwise in a region
$R\supset\Sigma^{(u)}$, then
$\Pabs^{(u)}\le T_0\,S_{\mathrm{lim}}\,|\Sigma^{(u)}|\,\bar\eta^{(u)}$
with $\bar\eta^{(u)}$ the mean exposure fraction.
\end{proposition}

Numerically, for an adult at 26\,GHz with $T_0\approx 0.37$ and
$\bar\eta\approx 0.5$, the implied absorbed-power budget under a
Brussels-style pointwise 14.57\,V/m cap is
$\Pabs^{(u)}\lesssim 0.37\times 0.565 \times 1.8 \times 0.5
\approx 0.19$\,W. That is several orders of magnitude below ICNIRP's
whole-body implied budget ($70\,\mathrm{kg}\times 0.08\,\mathrm{W/kg}=5.6$\,W),
so the Brussels ordinance remains dominated by ICNIRP for absorbed
power. The binding object is incident field at public space, not
absorbed power in any body. That is exactly where the
body-centric twin approach has leverage: one can meet ICNIRP
absorbed-power limits with ample margin while violating the
Brussels incident-PD cap at unoccupied free-space points, and vice
versa. The optimisation variable that reconciles them is the
\emph{shape} of the precoder, which the twin can choose once it
knows where bodies are.

\subsection{Twin-vs-exclusion, rhetorical frame}

The argument to a regulator is:

\begin{quote}
Your ordinance caps free-space field strength at every publicly
accessible point. We propose to cap absorbed power on every body
that is actually there. The latter is the physical quantity the
ordinance is proxying for. Our twin provides a pose-worst-case
guarantee of absorbed-power compliance on every body within radius
$R$ of the BS, drawn from a registered pose dictionary. We are
prepared to let you audit the twin.
\end{quote}

This reframes the regulator's question from ``is any point over the
cap'' to ``is any person over the cap''. Cities that banned 5G over
the cap (Brussels 2019--2023, Geneva 2019, several Italian
municipalities) did so because the cap was a blunt proxy. A twin
reads the proxy back into the quantity of interest and gives the
regulator a more precise guarantee, at materially higher capacity.
If the paper lands that rhetorical frame cleanly, it becomes a
policy-relevant paper, not only a signal-processing paper.

\section{What happens at 26\,GHz on an 8\texttimes 8 panel}\label{sec:numbers}
% ============================================================

Back-of-envelope for the Brussels Grand Place scenario (intended as
the paper's hero experiment):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item BS: 8$\times$8 panel on a building facade, 26\,GHz, EIRP 55\,dBm
  max broadcast. Aperture $\approx 4.1$\,cm across.
\item Users: 25 served UEs in the plaza, ranges 20--80\,m.
\item Non-users: 25 bystanders, ranges 5--80\,m.
\item Pose: AMASS-derived walk/stand dictionary, $T\approx 16$
  prototypes per activity class.
\item Limit: 14.57\,V/m outdoor Brussels cap, shared across a
  hypothetical 4-operator cell.
\end{itemize}

Back-of-envelope spatial selectivity: at 50\,m range, angular
resolution of the panel is $\sim\!\lambda/D\approx 16^\circ$,
translating to a linear resolution of $\sim 14$\,m. Two pedestrians
within 14\,m of each other share a beam at this range. That is not
``null one, dose the other''; it is ``shape the integrated
absorption on the cluster''. The spatial selectivity does matter
across groups, not within them.

Expected paper-level result (hypothesis): at current Brussels caps,
a four-operator aggregate 8$\times$8 deployment exceeds the
regulator's incident-PD cap on 10--30\% of random pedestrian
configurations under MRT precoding and $\sim 3$--$5$\% under classical
ZF, while an exposure-null precoder keeps the violation rate under
$\sim 1$\% across poses at a sum-rate cost of 1--2\,dB relative to ZF.
The cost is small because the quiet subspace is generically
nontrivial (\cref{prop:dim}).

\section{Open threads}
% ============================================================

A list of loose ends, not claims:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=3pt]

\item \textbf{The ``who is a non-user'' problem.} The paper assumes
the twin knows about non-users. Three pragmatic paths:
(i) they carry a smartphone on an operator the coordinator has
visibility into, so they contribute IMU just like users;
(ii) passive ISAC at mmWave picks them up as specular returns
(q\_complement pseudo-Brewster locking makes this cheap);
(iii) the regulator mandates a privacy-respecting public
``here is a body'' beacon. The paper should present (i) as baseline,
nod at (ii) and (iii), and be honest that full non-user enumeration
is a trust assumption.

\item \textbf{Fair allocation of the exposure budget.} Within-cell,
given a finite budget $L^{(u)}$ shared across operators, what is
the ``right'' $\alpha_o$? Shapley value on the cooperative game of
\cref{prop:coop}. Tractable for small $|\Ops|$. Could be a two-page
section that reaches beyond signal processing into mechanism design.

\item \textbf{Lower-bound capacity.} What is the minimum sum-rate
achievable under the Brussels constraint for a given BS and body set?
Minimax, dual of \eqref{eq:P-MB}, bounded by
$\min_u \log(1+P\lambda_1(\hh_k\hh_k^H)/(L^{(u)}\lambda_1(\Q^{(u)})+\nu))$
or similar. Worth doing closed-form at $K=1$ to ground intuition.

\item \textbf{Löwner slack empirically.} Run the SDP envelope for
a body on the thelonious phantom across a 30-pose dictionary. Plot
$\lambda_k(\Qbar_\star)-\lambda_k(\Q(\hat\theta))$. If
\cref{conj:lowrank} holds the slack decays with $k$; if not, the
worst-case approach is pessimistic and the paper has to argue for
point-estimate pose with sensor-fusion uncertainty balls.

\item \textbf{Link to direction 5.} Direction 5's translation phasor
identity
$\Q(\mathbf{t})_{ab}=\sum_{nn'}e^{-ik_0(\khat_n-\khat_{n'})\cdot\mathbf{t}}Q_{ab}^{(nn')}$
says translation is an analytic update of the exposure operator. For
walking pedestrians, this is the dominant motion; Löwner envelope
over pose-plus-translation decomposes into
$\Qbar=\Qbar_{\mathrm{rot}}\otimes e^{i\cdots}$ (conjecturally), with
the rotation envelope captured by the dictionary SDP and the
translation handled analytically. If clean, this is a key figure:
``the twin updates at pilot cadence without rebuilding.''

\item \textbf{Multi-cell.} Extending \cref{sec:multiop} from
multi-operator to multi-cell (same operator, multiple BSs in the
plaza) is algebraically identical but scenario-relevant for the DTN
pillar. Hot threat: inter-cell interference nulling plus
exposure-null precoding in the same framework, with both constraints
PSD. This is the clean ``twin-in-the-network'' extension.

\item \textbf{q\_complement revisited.} The pseudo-Brewster duality
there says $\Q_{\mathrm{re}}\approx (1-T_0)/T_0\,\Q$. So
exposure-null precoding is simultaneously
backscatter-null precoding. For an MNO that wants to avoid lighting
up bystanders on monostatic sensing (privacy), this is a bonus the
same precoder delivers for free. Worth a half-page.

\item \textbf{Regulator protocol.} The paper could include an
artefact: a proposed audit protocol for twin-based compliance.
``Given $(\Poses_u)_u$, the BS publishes $(\Qbar^{(u)})_u$; the
regulator verifies random
$(\ww,\theta)$ pairs via in silico AEGIS.'' Turns the paper into a
standardisation sketch. Bolder than a PHY paper but aligned with the
DTN SI's ``open interfaces and intent-based APIs'' bullet.

\item \textbf{Fear-angle vs engineering-angle.} The paper's
introduction has to thread a needle between ``regulators are
irrational, here's how to route around them'' (wrong tone) and
``ordinances reflect legitimate public concern, and here is how an
engineering twin can meet those concerns on their own terms''
(correct tone). Draft the intro from the second framing. Cite
specific Brussels sources (the 2019 Fremault halt, the 2023
Constitutional Court ruling, the 2024 ordinance) to anchor the
problem as a live regulatory reality, not a hypothetical.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary}
% ============================================================

One page of narrative, if one writes the abstract after the note.

Brussels caps cumulative outdoor EMF at 14.57\,V/m, shared across all
operators, at the point of exposure. At 26\,GHz on a single
Ericsson-class panel this cap actually binds. The monograph's
single-user ECBF has no bite in this regime because it does not know
about bystanders, does not know about other operators, and does not
know about pose uncertainty. Those are three upgrades:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item Many bodies: \eqref{eq:P-MB}, closed form via
$\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\lambda)=\sum_u\lambda_u\Q^{(u)}$, with ZF as the
degenerate rank-one case.
\item Many operators: cooperative version at aggregate budget,
provably never worse than the non-cooperative administrative split.
\item Many poses: Löwner envelope $\Qbar^{(u)}$ from an activity
dictionary yields pose-independent compliance by construction.
\end{itemize}

The resulting object is a multi-body, multi-operator,
pose-robust precoder whose feasible set is the intersection of
ellipsoids over PSD operators and whose Lagrangian has the same
closed form as Hochwald--Ying with a summed exposure operator in
place of the scalar one.

Written from this angle, the JSAC DTN paper is:

\begin{quote}
\textit{Twin the bodies in the plaza. Solve for the precoder that
respects every body's Löwner-enveloped exposure operator at the
cumulative operator-aggregated budget. Show the resulting sum-rate
recovers most of the capacity that a Brussels ordinance
conservatively removes.}
\end{quote}

The network is the substrate: the BSs, the propagation, the
multi-operator sharing. The twin is application-aware: it is a
population of body DTs, parameterised by SMPL pose from a mix of IMU
and opportunistic sensing. The loop is closed: every pose refresh
shrinks or grows the Löwner envelope, which shrinks or grows the
feasible precoder set, which shrinks or grows the achieved sum-rate.
That is a DTN in the SI's sense, done on an ordinance the public
cares about.
