The Simulation LayerTo demonstrate compliance with the FCC's power density limits for devices incorporating phased arrays (such as 5G mmWave antennas), physical measurements alone are mathematically and economically impractical. Phased arrays can form thousands of discrete beam patterns, making exhaustive physical scanning of every beam impossible. Consequently, the equipment authorization process relies on a hybridized simulation-measurement workflow.2.1 Commercial EM Simulation Tools in Public FilingsAn analysis of public "Part 0" and "RF exposure simulation report" documents filed with the FCC reveals a distinct hierarchy of electromagnetic simulation tools:Ansys HFSS (Ansys Electromagnetics Suite): The dominant tool appearing in public filings for cellular phased arrays. It utilizes the frequency-domain Finite Element Method (FEM) and adaptive meshing to resolve electrically small antenna elements within complex device chassis.Sim4Life / SEMCAD X Matterhorn (SPEAG / ZMT): Widely utilized in close-proximity biological modeling, absorbed power density (APD) assessments, and wireless power transfer (WPT) simulations. These tools rely on the Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) solver and are optimized for anatomical human phantom interactions.CST Studio Suite (Dassault Systèmes): Frequently utilized in Wi-Fi 6E/7 laptop integrations and automotive 60 GHz radar applications. It features a Finite Integration Technique (FIT) transient solver that excels in handling large, multi-material structures.In-House Codes, FEKO, and openEMS: These codes are rarely cited in portable consumer electronics filings due to the immense validation burden mandated by FCC KDB 447498 and equivalent standards.2.2 Concrete Filing Examples of Computational WorkflowsSamsung Galaxy Handsets (e.g., FCC ID: A3LSMS921U)Tool & Solver: Ansys Electromagnetics Suite (HFSS) frequency-domain FEM solver.Methodology: Full-wave 3D electromagnetic modeling of the device chassis, including the display, battery, and mmWave modules (ANT M and ANT N). The simulation utilizes adaptive meshing to refine the grid dynamically around the antenna structures.Convergence & Calculation: The adaptive convergence is governed by the $S$-parameter delta ($\Delta S$), which was strictly limited to $0.02$. The electric ($\vec{E}$) and magnetic ($\vec{H}$) fields are extracted at a grid resolution of 1 mm. The point-wise power density is calculated from the real part of the Poynting vector ($\vec{S}$):$$\langle\vec{S}\rangle = \frac{1}{2} \text{Re}\{\vec{E} \times \vec{H}^*\}$$The spatial-averaged power density ($PD_{avg}$) over a $4\text{ cm}^2$ area $A$ is then derived as:$$PD_{avg} = \frac{1}{A} \iint_{A} \langle\vec{S}\rangle \cdot d\vec{a}$$This computational workflow determines the maximum peak surface-averaged power density ($mpsPD$) to derive the input power limit (input.power.limit) for every supported beam.Motorola Wearable Devices (e.g., FCC ID: IHDT56AA6)Tool & Solver: Ansys HFSS (2021 R2).Methodology: Used to identify the worst-case near-field beams and beam pairs for the mmWave antenna modules across defined evaluation planes around the device.Validation: Rather than measuring all beams, the simulated near-field results are used to select a highly restricted subset of worst-case beams, which are then physically measured on a power density scanner to validate the simulation model.Apple iPhone Wireless Power Transfer (e.g., FCC ID: BCG-E3542A)Tool & Solver: Sim4Life / SEMCAD X FDTD electromagnetic modeling.Methodology: At 360 kHz, direct physical measurement of SAR is impossible due to the lack of calibrated low-frequency SAR probes. Apple used computational modeling to estimate the maximum SAR distribution inside an anatomical human body model during portable use scenarios.Acceptance: This simulation-only compliance strategy was formally accepted by the FCC and TCBs through a specialized KDB inquiry approval process.2.3 Acceptance of Simulation-Only EvidenceThe regulatory acceptance of simulation-only evidence is highly bifurcated based on the device's operating frequency and standard:5G mmWave Part 0 (Simulation-Only Accepted): The FCC fully accepts simulation-only evidence within the "Part 0" report to construct the device's antenna array codebook and calculate the theoretical beam limits.Part 1 and Part 2 (Hybrid Mandatory): A simulation-only package is not accepted for final device certification. The applicant must submit physical measurement data on a subset of worst-case beams to validate the simulation's accuracy.Low-Frequency and Specialized Exclusions: In rare cases where measurement tools are physically unavailable—such as sub-10 MHz nerve stimulation evaluations or specific close-proximity WPT configurations—computational modeling may be accepted as standalone proof of compliance, subject to strict KDB inquiry clearance.2.4 TCB Council Workshops and FCC Position on IEC/IEEE 63195-2At recent TCB Council Workshops (2022–2026), the computational evaluation of power density remains a major topic. During the October 2024 Workshop, Niels Kuster presented a critical update on computational modeling standards:Normal Component as a Poor Proxy: Under the draft IEC/IEEE 63195-2 standard, spatial power density definitions allow for the surface-normal real component ($sPDn+$), all real components ($sPDtot+$), or the modulus of the Poynting vector ($sPDmod+$). Kuster demonstrated that using the normal component ($sPDn+$) acts as a poor proxy for actual exposure.Dielectric Loading Effects: Calculating incident power density in free space ignores the dielectric loading of the human body on the device's antennas. The confinement of electromagnetic energy between the antenna structure and the lossy tissue can lead to substantial deviations between simulated free-space PD and the actual power absorbed by the skin. This physical reality has accelerated regulatory interest in transitioning from Incident Power Density (IPD) to Absorbed Power Density (APD) using phantoms with a lossy silicon target or validated skin models.Skin Model Standardization: In 2025, the IT'IS Foundation’s high-resolution skin model, built from measurements of 44 human volunteers, was formally adopted as the international reference skin model for both simulated and measured APD under the IEC/IEEE 63195-3/4 standards. The regulatory transition toward APD is actively supported by commercial software updates. For instance, SEMCAD X Matterhorn V19.2 and V20.2 feature direct implementations of the latest IEC/IEEE 63195-2 FDIS and the draft IEC PAS 63446 ED1 for APD simulations up to 300 GHz.3. The PAG process introduces a major regulatory hurdle: the TCB is legally prohibited from issuing the final Grant of Equipment Authorization until the FCC's Office of Engineering and Technology (OET) has explicitly reviewed the complete test report and simulation package.Standard PAG Delay: For standard 5G mmWave devices, the PAG review process adds a mandatory latency of 4 to 8 weeks to the certification timeline.Novel Device Delay: For devices utilizing novel form factors (such as foldable displays), complex multi-transmitter simultaneous transmission profiles, or sensor-driven dynamic power back-off mechanisms, the PAG review process routinely extends to 3 to 4 months. The delay stems from iterative rounds of "deficiency letters" issued by the FCC as they coordinate technical parameters with the NTIA or request additional verification data from the applicant.3.3 The Risk of First-Pass Failures and Module MisclassificationEntering formal certification without rigorous pre-compliance simulation is a highly high-risk strategy.First-Pass Failure Rate: Industry data from leading testing organizations indicates that approximately 50% of wireless consumer electronics fail formal electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) or RF exposure testing on their first pass.The Cost of Re-Testing: A single first-pass failure is catastrophic to a product launch. It typically adds 4 to 12 weeks of design, PCB layout, and firmware modification to the program, alongside $5,000 to $30,000 in direct lab re-testing fees.The Module Misclassification Trap: Many hardware teams integrate a pre-certified Wi-Fi 6E/7 or 60 GHz module into their design, assuming the modular grant covers the host device. However, if the host device allows the transmitter to operate within $20\text{ cm}$ of the user's body, the device category changes from "mobile" to "portable". This transitions the device to a strict, measurement-based APD or SAR compliance regime. Failure to identify this transition during the design phase results in TCB rejections, import blocks at the border, or costly post-market recalls.Geopolitical Capacity Contraction: The compliance bottleneck has been further exacerbated by the FCC's aggressive enforcement action against "bad labs". Between late 2024 and 2025, the FCC formally denied and stripped the accreditations of multiple high-volume testing laboratories apparently controlled by or linked to foreign entities. This sudden contraction of cheap offshore testing capacity has forced a massive volume of consumer wireless filings back into highly congested Western and Taiwanese laboratories, expanding queue times across the industry.4. Classification Edge Cases (60 GHz)Unlicensed operation in the 57–71 GHz band is highly popular for short-range radars, high-bandwidth data links, and spatial sensors. Because the reactive near-field boundary at 60 GHz is extremely small (the wavelength $\lambda$ is approximately 5 mm), the regulatory classification of a device as "portable" ($<20\text{ cm}$ separation) or "mobile" ($\ge20\text{ cm}$ separation) completely dictates the compliance pathway.5.1 Laptop Human-Presence Radars (Lenovo, Dell, HP)PC manufacturers routinely integrate low-power 60 GHz FMCW radar modules into the top bezel of laptop displays to enable automatic user lock/unlock and approach detection.Key Actors: Lenovo, Dell, and HP, utilizing transceivers from Infineon (XENSIV BGT60TR13C) or Texas Instruments (IWRL6432).Classification: Because the display bezel sits directly in front of the user's face and hands during close-proximity operation, the separation distance is frequently well under $20\text{ cm}$. Consequently, these integrations are classified as portable devices.Evaluation Path: Despite the portable classification, these sensors operate at extremely low transmit powers (typically under 6 dBm to 10 dBm EIRP) and utilize highly compressed duty cycles. Under the modernized RSS-102 and KDB 447498 rules, they typically qualify for SAR/PD test exclusion. If a high-power variant exceeds the exclusion threshold, a localized power density numerical simulation must be performed.5.2 Smart Glasses and XR HeadsetsExtended reality (XR), virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR) smart glasses utilize 60 GHz transceivers to transmit uncompressed high-resolution video streams.Key Actors: Meta, HTC (Maple Peak Wireless VR), and Apple.Classification: Because the antennas are physically embedded inside the temple arms, forehead pad, or display frame of the headset, they operate within millimeters of the user's head, eyes, and ears. They are strictly classified as portable devices.Evaluation Path: Simple far-field MPE calculations are entirely prohibited. These devices require comprehensive simulated and measured near-field power density (PD) and absorbed power density (APD) evaluations at a separation distance of 0 mm. The evaluation plane must be modeled directly against the outer housing of the headset.5.3 In-Cabin Automotive Child-Presence Detection (CPD)Automotive manufacturers deploy high-resolution 60 GHz 4D imaging radar arrays in the cabin ceiling to detect the breathing and movement of sleeping infants to prevent vehicular heatstroke.Key Actors: Valeo (CPD Module EC30693), Vayyar Imaging (CPD+SBR Module), Tesla (Model Y Cabin Radar), IEE Sensing, and Brose.The FCC Waiver Framework: Under the landmark FCC Order DA 21-407 (issued April 14, 2021), the Commission granted a waiver of Section 15.255 of its rules to allow Valeo, Vayyar, Tesla, IEE, Infineon, and Brose to operate in-cabin radars in the 57–64 GHz band at higher power levels (+13 dBm EIRP) than traditionally permitted for short-range interactive motion sensors.Classification: Because the radars are permanently integrated into the vehicle’s roof-liner or overhead console, they maintain a physical separation distance of greater than $20\text{ cm}$ from all occupants in the seats or footwells below. They are strictly classified as mobile devices.Evaluation Path: Due to the mobile classification, none of these automotive manufacturers were required to submit measured or simulated near-field power density on human tissue. They demonstrated compliance by filing simple far-field MPE calculations evaluated at a standard distance of $20\text{ cm}$ using the traditional formula:$$Pd = \frac{P_{out} \cdot G}{4\pi r^2}$$5.4 WiGig / 802.11ay AccessoriesThe classification of general high-speed 60 GHz data accessories depends on the physical use case:Portable Snap-on Displays: Devices like dual-display phone attachments (e.g., Keyssa 60 GHz operations under FCC ID: ZNFV500EM) are classified as portable. Europe and CanadaHomologating a high-frequency wireless device for international markets requires navigating distinct regional frameworks. While the biophysical interaction of electromagnetic fields with the human body remains identical, the administrative requirements, acceptable limits, and database structures are highly localized.6.1 European Union: Radio Equipment Directive (RED)In the European Economic Area, all wireless products must demonstrate compliance with the essential health and safety requirements of Article 3.1(a) of the Radio Equipment Directive 2014/53/EU.Harmonised Standards FrameworkCENELEC Technical Committee 106X (CLC/TC 106X) oversees electromagnetic field exposure standards from 0 Hz to 300 GHz. For devices operating above 6 GHz, the harmonised standard pathway is strictly governed by:EN IEC 62311:2020: The primary generic standard for demonstrating compliance of electronic and electrical equipment with basic human exposure restrictions (0 Hz to 300 GHz).EN 50665:2017: A generic standard used to assess low-power electronic equipment for which no dedicated product or product-family standard applies.EN IEC/IEEE 63195-1:2023: This standard has been formally adopted into the European system as a basic standard. It establishes the exact physical measurement procedures for evaluating human exposure to radiofrequency fields in close proximity to the head and body in the 6 GHz to 300 GHz range.CST/Simulations under RED: European compliance relies heavily on manufacturer self-declaration. If harmonised standards are fully applied and documented in a technical file, no third-party Notified Body is required. This allows manufacturers to include high-fidelity CST or SEMCAD simulation reports inside their internal technical file to declare CE compliance above 6 GHz without TCB-level review.Lack of a Centralized European DatabaseA major challenge in competitive analysis and market intelligence is the structural difference between the US and European regulatory models. There is no public European equivalent to the centralized FCC ID database.NANDO Database: The European Commission maintains the NANDO (New Approach Notified and Designated Organisations) portal. This database is merely a directory of accredited Notified Bodies and their technical scopes; it does not contain product-level test reports, schematics, or simulation data.How EU Data is Retained: Products placed on the EU market are supported by a manufacturer-issued Declaration of Conformity (DoC), which is often hosted on the brand's public website. The full technical test reports, including any computational EM exposure simulations, remain confidential within the manufacturer's internal technical file and are only surrendered to national market surveillance authorities (e.g., the Federal Network Agency/BNetzA in Germany or ANFR in France) during formal product audits or customs enforcement actions.6.2 ISED CanadaISED Canada operates a highly rigorous equipment authorization framework that aligns closely with the FCC on exposure limits but implements distinct technical standards and administrative procedures.Rescission of SPR-APD and SPR-002To streamline and modernize its regulatory framework, ISED executed a major consolidation of its RF exposure standards:SPR-APD Rescission: SPR-APD Issue 1, which set out the specific testing methods for portable devices operating in the 6 GHz band (5925–7125 MHz), was officially rescinded on August 15, 2025.SPR-002 Rescission: SPR-002 Issue 2, which governed low-frequency nerve stimulation and computational assessments from 3 kHz to 10 MHz, was simultaneously rescinded on August 15, 2025.The Modernized RSS-102 Issue 6 FrameworkFollowing these rescissions, ISED integrated all high-frequency and computational requirements into RSS-102 Issue 6 and a newly structured suite of measurement (.MEAS) and simulation (.SIM) companion documents.
