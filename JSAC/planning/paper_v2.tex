\begin{abstract}
\robinnote{Abstract lead is deployment-blocker + application-aware +
closed-loop, hitting three SI keywords on line one without
sounding like keyword-stuffing. The physical-quantity-versus-proxy
observation is the one sentence that makes the paper's motivation
obvious to a non-EMF reviewer.}
%
Downlink mmWave deployment in several European jurisdictions is
blocked not by propagation physics but by city-level
electromagnetic-field reference-level caps --- the Brussels 14.57~V/m
cumulative outdoor limit, Italian attention values, and equivalent
ordinances --- that are conservative free-space proxies for the
tissue-protection basic restrictions they are derived from. We
treat the human body as the \emph{application the network must
serve safely}, twin it inside the base-station control loop, and
re-cast exposure-constrained precoding as an application-aware,
closed-loop digital-twin network (DTN) problem. Each body in the
coverage cell carries a coherent exposure operator
$\Q^{(u)}\in\Complex^{M\times M}$; the precoder maximises sum-rate
under per-body absorbed-power budgets, yielding a closed-form
multi-user solution whose rank-one degenerate case recovers
classical zero-forcing. The twin supplies each $\Q^{(u)}$ without
body-side angle estimation: a precomputed scene-ray-tracing
path dictionary is indexed by body position (from MNO telemetry or
BS-side ISAC); a runtime calibration step scales the dictionary
entries against served-user uplink CSI. Pose, where available,
enters as a scalar absorption-cross-section that is a direct
evaluation of the AEGIS physics pipeline at pose-refresh cadence;
where unavailable, a pose-agnostic Cauchy bound
$D(\khat)\le 2$ closes the compliance loop without sensing pose.
We report a $k=3$--$4$ empirical dominant rank of $\Q^{(u)}$ in a
26~GHz $8\times 8$ plaza and a GPU $\Q$-refresh of 7.5~ms per
SMPL-scale body on commodity hardware, positioning the full
closed loop as real-time-in-silico on a single workstation. The
result is a DTN that closes the loop between the wireless
substrate and a tissue-protection semantic goal, verifiable by a
regulator through a standards-aligned audit pathway.
\end{abstract}

Digital twin network, closed-loop optimization, application-aware

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\robinnote{I pushed the deployment-blocker framing hard here, which

\subsection{Application-aware wireless meets a physical constraint}

The JSAC DTN special issue's thesis is that future wireless
networks are not the thing being twinned --- they are the
\emph{adaptive substrate} over which twins of the application
run a closed control loop. Most instantiations of this thesis
address a computational or sensing application: a robot, a
vehicle, an immersive headset. This paper addresses an
application that has hitherto been absent from the DTN literature
and which is, in quantitative terms, the primary deployment
blocker for mmWave in much of Europe: \emph{the human body in the
coverage cell}, served or bystander, whose tissue-protection
limit sets a ceiling on radiated power.

Brussels halted 5G in April 2019 under a 6~V/m
ordinance~\cite{BrusselsArrete2007}; the cap was raised to
14.57~V/m cumulative across operators in 2024 after a
Constitutional Court ruling specifically to enable 5G
deployment~\cite{BrusselsArrete2024}. Italy capped at 6~V/m
nationally from 2001 until a 2024 revision to 15~V/m with a
24-hour median averaging window; 600+ Italian municipalities
maintain anti-5G resolutions~\cite{ItalyCap}. France restricts
child handset exposure. The quantitative thread is a
reference-level (RL) cap one to two orders of magnitude tighter
than the ICNIRP~2020 reference levels~\cite{ICNIRP2020} it nominally
derives from. At 26~GHz a single Ericsson-class $8\times 8$ panel
at 55~dBm EIRP satisfies ICNIRP by a comfortable margin, yet can
violate the Brussels cumulative cap in parts of the coverage cell
once multiple operators share the same public space
(\S\ref{sec:binding}).

\robinnote{The ``conservative proxy'' framing below is the single
most important sentence in the intro. It tells the reviewer why
a body-centric method is scientifically better, not just
operationally convenient. Without it, the paper reads as
``cleverness to evade regulation.'' With it, the paper reads as
``compliance, done on the physical quantity the regulator cared
about all along.''}

Reference levels are conservative proxies for the tissue-protection
\emph{basic restrictions} (BR) they are derived from
\cite{ICNIRP2020}: a free-space field-strength bound that holds
pointwise in public space, derived under a worst-case body
orientation, pose, and occupancy assumption. This conservatism
is a capacity cost. ICNIRP~2020 explicitly permits compliance
demonstration via direct assessment of the basic restriction;
IEC/IEEE~62232:2022~\cite{IEC62232} and
ITU-T~K.122~\cite{K122} operationalize this path. What has been
missing is an auditable, real-time mechanism that assembles the
basic-restriction assessment from live network state rather than
from offline simulation campaigns. That mechanism is a digital
twin of the body, resident in the base-station's control loop.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item
A \textbf{coherent multi-body exposure-constrained precoder}

\section{Body Absorption Physics}\label{sec:physics}

\robinnote{Derivations here are condensed reproductions of the
monograph Parts I--II so the paper is self-contained on the
physics. Appendix~\ref{app:fresnel} holds the Fresnel details;
Appendix~\ref{app:cauchy} holds the $D\le 2$ proof.}

\subsection{Plane-wave absorbed power density}\label{sec:plane-wave}

Consider a plane wave of incident power density $\Sinc$ along
$\khat$ impinging on a locally flat body-surface element at
$\rr\in\Sigma$ with outward normal $\nhat(\rr)$. Let
$\theta_i(\rr)$ be the incidence angle,
$\mu(\rr)\equiv-\khat\cdot\nhat(\rr)=\cos\theta_i$. The inward
power flux through $\diff A$ is $\Sinc\mu\diff A$. Under the
assumption that the skin depth $\delta$ is much smaller than
body curvature radii (valid at mmWave,
$\delta\sim 0.3$--$0.5$~mm), all transmitted power is absorbed
within a surface layer, and the absorbed power density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sab-pt}
  \Sab(\rr)=\Sinc\,T(\theta_i)\,\mu(\rr)\,H(\mu(\rr)),
\end{equation}
where $T(\theta)=1-|r(\theta)|^2$ is the power transmittance of
the Fresnel interface and $H(\cdot)$ is the Heaviside indicator
for unoccluded surface. Under pseudo-Brewster compensation valid
for bare skin at mmWave, the TE/TM-averaged transmittance is
angle-insensitive to within a few percent and reduces to the
scalar $T_0=4n/((n+1)^2+\kappa^2)$ with $\tilde{n}=n-i\kappa$ the
complex refractive index of skin~\cite{WydaegheMonograph,Gabriel1996};
for skin at 26~GHz, $T_0\approx 0.54$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sab-T0}
  \Sab(\rr)\approx \Sinc\,T_0\,\ReLU(\mu(\rr)),
\end{equation}
where $\ReLU(\mu)=\mu H(\mu)$. Integration over the body
surface yields the total absorbed power under plane-wave
illumination:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pabs-plane}
  \Pabs(\khat)=\int_\Sigma \Sab\,\diff A
  =\Sinc\,T_0\,\Aperp(\khat),
\end{equation}
with $\Aperp(\khat)=\int_\Sigma\eta(\rr)\ReLU(\mu)\,\diff A$ the
projected absorption area and $\eta(\rr)\in[0,1]$ the exposure
fraction (fraction of hemisphere from which $\rr$ is unoccluded).

\subsection{Cauchy reduction and pose-agnostic bound}\label{sec:cauchy}

\begin{theorem}[Generalised Cauchy formula
\cite{Cauchy1841,WydaegheMonograph}]\label{thm:gen-cauchy}
For any body with surface $\Sigma$ and exposure fraction
$\eta(\rr)$, define the absorption area
$\Aab=\int_\Sigma\eta(\rr)\diff A$. The direction-averaged
absorbed power is
\begin{equation}
  \langle\Pabs\rangle=\Sinc\,T_0\,\frac{\Aab}{4}.
\end{equation}
For directional illumination from $\khat$,
$\Pabs(\khat)=\Sinc T_0(\Aab/4)D(\khat)$ with
\emph{absorption directivity}
$D(\khat)=4\Aperp(\khat)/\Aab$. For convex bodies,
$D(\khat)\le 2$; for non-convex bodies,
$D(\khat)\le 2A_{\mathrm{CH}}/\Aab$ with $A_{\mathrm{CH}}$ the
convex-hull surface area.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Fubini on $\int_{S^2}\Aperp(\khat)\diff\Omega$ and $\int_\Sigma
\langle \nhat\cdot(-\khat)\rangle_+\diff A$, with the inner
integral $=\pi$ by symmetry. Full proof in
\cref{app:cauchy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Posture insensitivity]\label{rem:posture}
Across a 51-frame backflip animation on an anatomical phantom,
$D_{\max}$ varies by only 6.5\%; at $L=6$ spherical-harmonic
expansion the RMS fit error on $D(\khat)$ is below 0.13\% per
frame \cite{WydaegheMonograph}. For Thelonious at 26~GHz,
$D_{\max}\approx 1.22$ (side-on) and
$A_{\mathrm{CH}}/\Aab\approx 1.38$. Directionally worst-case,
pose-agnostic absorbed power is therefore bounded with under 1~dB
of slack by the scalar
$\Sinc T_0 (\Aab/4)\,\min(2,2A_{\mathrm{CH}}/\Aab)$, requiring
neither pose estimation nor activity classification.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Pose-agnostic operator bound]\label{thm:cauchy-bound}
Let $\Q^{(u)}$ be the coherent exposure operator of body $u$
(\cref{def:Q}) and $\avec^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BS}}(\mathbf{t}_u)$ its
BS-side steering vector toward the body centroid.
For any precoder $\xx$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy-bound-op}
  \xx^H\Q^{(u)}\xx \le
  T_0\,\frac{\Aab^{(u)}}{4}\,D_{\max}^{(u)}\,
  |\avec^{(u),H}_{\mathrm{BS}}\xx|^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The right-hand side is a rank-one constraint on $\xx$; the bound
is the pose-agnostic safety net invoked for non-cooperating
bystanders. Proof and refinements in \cref{app:cauchy-bound}.

\section{Coherent Exposure Operator}\label{sec:operator}

\robinnote{This section is the Part-III compression, enough for
a reader to follow without reading the monograph. The path-space
factorisation is the one load-bearing factorisation that needs
to be derived, not just cited. I put it in the main text with
a full proof sketch; the exact $\mathbf{\Psi}$-tissue-weighting is
in~\cref{app:tissue}.}

\subsection{Setup and MIMO channel through the body}

The base station has $M$ antennas transmitting
$\xx=\sum_{k\in\Users}\ww_ks_k\in\Complex^M$ with
$\mathbb{E}[|s_k|^2]=1$ per symbol and total power
$\Ptx=\sum_k\|\ww_k\|^2$. Propagation from BS to a body surface
point $\rr\in\Sigma^{(u)}$ produces, through a scene-dependent ray
cascade, a finite set of $N$ paths indexed by $n$, each with
arrival direction $\khat_n(\rr)$, polarisation-amplitude vector
$\bm\psi_n\in\Complex^3$, phase reference
$e^{-ik_0\khat_n\cdot\rr}$, and element of origin $j(n)\in\{1,\dots,M\}$.

\begin{definition}[Dispatch, phase, tissue read-out]\label{def:JMphi}
The \emph{dispatch matrix} $\Jmat\in\{0,1\}^{N\times M}$ has
$J_{nj}=\delta_{j,j(n)}$, routing element weights onto path
amplitudes. The \emph{phase diagonal}
$\Phimat(\rr)=\mathrm{diag}(e^{-ik_0\khat_n\cdot\rr})$ applies
per-path geometric phase at $\rr$. The
\emph{tissue-weighted per-path read-out} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psitil}
  \tilde{\bm\psi}_n(\rr)=\sqrt{\tfrac{\sigma}{4\alpha_n}}\,
  \mathbf{F}_n(\rr)\,\bm\psi_n \in \Complex^3,
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{F}_n(\rr)$ the Fresnel transmission operator at
$\rr$ (\cref{app:fresnel}), $\alpha_n$ the intra-tissue
attenuation along the transmitted ray, and $\sigma$ the tissue
conductivity. Stack as
$\Psitil(\rr)=[\tilde{\bm\psi}_1(\rr),\dots,\tilde{\bm\psi}_N(\rr)]\in\Complex^{3\times N}$.
\end{definition}

Coherent superposition and depth integration of the transmitted
field~\cite{WydaegheMonograph} gives the \emph{coherent absorption
law}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sab-coherent}
  \Sab(\rr,\xx)=\bigl\|\Gtil(\rr)\xx\bigr\|_2^2,\quad
  \Gtil(\rr)=\Psitil(\rr)\Phimat(\rr)\Jmat.
\end{equation}
Integration over the body gives the exposure operator.

\begin{definition}[Coherent exposure operator]\label{def:Q}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-def}
  \Q^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)=
  \int_{\Sigma^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)}
  \Gtil(\rr)^H\Gtil(\rr)\,\diff A
  \in\Complex^{M\times M},
\end{equation}
Hermitian PSD; $\Pabs^{(u)}(\xx)=\xx^H\Q^{(u)}\xx$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Path-space factorisation}\label{sec:pathspace}

The surface integral factors cleanly onto the path set.

\begin{theorem}[Path-space factorisation \cite{SystemFormalism,WydaegheMonograph}]\label{thm:JMJ}
Define the \emph{path-exposure Gram}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-def}
  \Mmat^{(u)}=\int_{\Sigma^{(u)}}
  \Phimat(\rr)^H\Psitil(\rr)^H\Psitil(\rr)\Phimat(\rr)\diff A
  \in\Complex^{N\times N}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}\label{eq:Q-JMJ}
  \Q^{(u)}=\Jmat^{T}\Mmat^{(u)}\Jmat,\quad
  \Pabs^{(u)}(\xx)=(\Jmat\xx)^H\Mmat^{(u)}(\Jmat\xx).
\end{empheq}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute $\Gtil=\Psitil\Phimat\Jmat$ into \cref{eq:Q-def}; the
surface integral touches only $\Psitil\Phimat$ and factors out
$\Jmat$ on both sides.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:JMJ} isolates the three kinds of information in
$\Q^{(u)}$: $\Jmat$ is a \emph{combinatorial} BS-array property
(which element originates which path), independent of body and
scene; $\Mmat^{(u)}$ is a \emph{geometry-and-tissue} property of
the body relative to the path set; and position enters only
through $\Mmat^{(u)}$. The practical import is that $\Jmat$ is
precomputable and $\Mmat^{(u)}$ is refreshable at pose cadence,
not slot cadence --- a key ingredient of the real-time-in-silico
closed loop below.

\subsection{Translation phasor identity}\label{sec:translation}

Let $\rr=\rr_0+\mathbf{t}_u$ with $\rr_0$ in the body frame. Then
$\Phimat(\rr)=\Phimat(\mathbf{t}_u)\Phimat(\rr_0)$, and because
$\Psitil$ depends only on local normal and tissue,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mphase}
  \Mmat^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)
  =\Phimat(\mathbf{t}_u)^H\,\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm 0,\bm\theta_u)\,\Phimat(\mathbf{t}_u).
\end{equation}
Translation is a diagonal-phase conjugation of the body-frame
Gram. Pose refreshes $\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm 0,\bm\theta_u)$ at
pose cadence; translation refreshes reduce to $O(N)$ complex
exponentials.

\subsection{Empirical rank and the plaza regime}\label{sec:rank}

\robinnote{I tightened the rank claim from ``3--10'' to the
measured $3$--$4$ per agent 03. Putting the plot as the ``see
Fig.~X'' stub makes the paper land on an empirical fact, not a
speculation.}

For an $8\times 8$ panel at 26~GHz and Thelonious phantoms
across a plaza ring at 20--80~m range, $\pm 60^{\circ}$ azimuth,
both a deterministic plaza-specular path model (LOS + ground +
two facade reflections) and a stochastic 3GPP~38.901 UMa-LOS
model with 60 physical subpaths exhibit an $\epsilon$-effective
rank of the coherent exposure operator $\Q^{(u)}$ of
\emph{median 3}, 90-percentile 3, maximum 4 at $\epsilon=10^{-2}$
(\cref{fig:rank-cdf}, data in
\texttt{JSAC/experiments/rank\_check}). The spectrum is
array-aperture-limited, not multipath-limited: the 64-element
panel has $\sim 14^{\circ}$ angular resolution, so 60 physical
subpaths collapse into 3--4 distinguishable BS-side modes.

\begin{remark}[Why the coherent operator, not a rank-few
heuristic]\label{rem:coherent-wins}
The empirical rank-few observation is a computational
\emph{affordance}, not a theoretical replacement. Geometries
where multiple paths fall inside a single BS angular cell ---
small cells, same-facade double-bounces, near-field UEs ---
exhibit higher effective rank, and a rank-few substitution
would undercount exposure. The coherent $\Q^{(u)}$ handles
these cases without reparameterisation; the plaza regime shows
up as a computational simplification, not a model change.
\end{remark}

\section{Multi-Body Exposure-Constrained Precoder}\label{sec:precoder}

\subsection{Problem formulation}

Let $\Users$ be the set of served users and
$\AllBodies=\Users\cup\Bodies$ the full body set with bystanders
$\Bodies$. Sum-rate maximisation under per-body budgets $L^{(u)}$
and total power $\Ptx$:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P-MB}
\begin{align}
\max_{\{\ww_k\}_{k\in\Users}}&\;
  \sum_k\log_2(1+\mathrm{SINR}_k)\\
\text{s.t.}\;&
  \sum_k\ww_k^H\Q^{(u)}\ww_k\le L^{(u)}\quad
  \forall u\in\AllBodies,\\
&\sum_k\|\ww_k\|^2\le\Ptx,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with $\mathrm{SINR}_k=|\hh_k^H\ww_k|^2/(\sum_{k'\ne k}|\hh_k^H\ww_{k'}|^2+\sigma_k^2)$.
The feasible set is the intersection of $K+B$ PSD ellipsoids
(one per body) with the $\ell_2$ power ball.

\subsection{Closed-form precoder}

Attach $\lambda_u\ge 0$ to exposure constraints and $\nu\ge 0$ to
the power constraint. Define the \emph{aggregate exposure
operator}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qtot}
  \Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bm\lambda)=\sum_{u\in\AllBodies}\lambda_u\,\Q^{(u)}.
\end{equation}
Under a weighted-MMSE concave surrogate for the
objective~\cite{Christensen2008,Shi2011}, the KKT stationarity in
$\ww_k$ reads
$(\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}+\nu\ident)\ww_k^\star=\mathbf{g}_k$, hence
\begin{empheq}[box=\fbox]{equation}\label{eq:closedform}
  \ww_k^\star=\bigl(\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bm\lambda)+\nu\ident\bigr)^{-1}\mathbf{g}_k.
\end{empheq}
The outer bisection on $\bm\lambda,\nu$ enforces
complementary slackness on the active exposure and power
constraints.

\begin{proposition}[Classical ZF as rank-one limit]\label{prop:ZFlimit}
Let each body operator degenerate to
$\Q^{(j)}=\sigma_j\avec_j\avec_j^H$ (rank one) with $\avec_j$
the BS steering vector to body $j$, and let $L^{(j)}\to 0$.
Then $\lambda_j\to\infty$ on the binding constraints,
$\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}\to\mathbf{A}^H\mathbf{D}\mathbf{A}$
with $\mathbf{A}$ stacking steering vectors, and
\cref{eq:closedform} reduces to the regularised zero-forcing
precoder~\cite{PeelHochwald2005}.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{prop:ZFlimit} is not a curiosity: the proposed algorithm
\emph{is} a generalisation of ZF, and in the rank-one ZF
substrate the precoder's computational footprint is identical
to standard MU-MIMO regularisation. The practical upgrade is
replacing the steering-vector null set with a
body-operator PSD-budget set.

\subsection{Soft budgets, not hard nulls}\label{sec:softbudget}

Equation~\eqref{eq:P-MB} permits $L^{(u)}>0$; the formulation
is \emph{budgeted}, not hard-nulled. Hard nulls
($L^{(u)}=0~\forall u$) consume one degree of freedom per body
and saturate the $M$-dimensional precoder space at $K+B\approx M$.
Soft budgets degrade gracefully: bodies with binding
$\lambda_u>0$ absorb power at budget, bodies with $\lambda_u=0$
slack beneath budget, and at high body count the precoder
prioritises the tight budgets without losing feasibility.

\subsection{Budget from regulatory regime}\label{sec:budget}

Two compliance statements are certified simultaneously.

\textbf{Basic restriction.}
$L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BR}}$ is the ICNIRP~2020 whole-body SAR limit
times body mass; for a 70~kg public-exposure adult,
$L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BR}}\approx 0.08\cdot 70=5.6$~W. At mmWave, the
10~g peak-SAR / APD constraint may bind earlier; we take
$L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BR}}=\min$ across these.

\textbf{Reference level.} Under a Brussels-style cumulative
cap $\Sinc\le S_{\mathrm{lim}}$ at public points,
$L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{RL}}\approx T_0 S_{\mathrm{lim}}\Aab^{(u)}\bar\eta^{(u)}$.
For 14.57~V/m ($S_{\mathrm{lim}}\approx 0.56$~W/m$^2$),
$\bar\eta=0.5$, $\Aab=1.45$~m$^2$:
$L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{RL}}\approx 0.19$~W --- an order of magnitude
below BR.

The binding budget is
$L^{(u)}=\min(L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BR}},L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{RL}})$.
In the Brussels regime the RL always binds first; the
basic-restriction compliance is obtained for free and can be
reported independently.

\section{Scene as a Ray-Traced Path Dictionary}\label{sec:scene-dict}

\robinnote{This is the load-bearing DTN section. It is written to
directly address the ``how the hell does the BS know the
AoA'' question you caught me on. The key ingredient is
that the scene is the slow-varying part (buildings don't move);
ray tracing it exhaustively is an offline preprocessing step;
runtime gets a \emph{lookup} + CSI-calibration scale factor.
This turns the twin from a real-time simulator into a real-time
\emph{lookup-calibrate-use} pipeline, which is what closed-loop
DTN actually means in the SI's terminology.}

\subsection{Separating slow-varying from fast-varying}

The coherent operator $\Q^{(u)}$ depends on quantities that
evolve on wildly different timescales:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Static} (load once): BS position and array geometry;
  scene buildings and ground (OpenStreetMap + 3D Tiles).
\item \emph{Slow} ($\gtrsim 1$~s): body translation $\mathbf{t}_u$,
  body entry/exit from the cell.
\item \emph{Medium} ($\sim 100$~ms): body pose $\bm\theta_u$.
\item \emph{Fast} ($\sim 1$~ms): channel state $\hh_k$ per served
  user (3GPP pilots).
\end{itemize}

A realisable closed loop refreshes each quantity at its native
cadence and caches the rest. Because the scene is static, the
\emph{path set} from BS to any candidate body position is static
too. We construct it once.

\begin{definition}[Scene path dictionary]\label{def:dict}
For a discrete grid $\mathcal{G}\subset\Reals^3$ of body
positions in the public region of the cell, the
\emph{path dictionary} is
\begin{equation}
  \Dict=\bigl\{(\khat_n(\rr_0),\hat{\khat}_{T,n}(\rr_0),\bm\psi_n(\rr_0),\alpha_n(\rr_0),j(n))\bigr\}_{\rr_0\in\mathcal{G},n}.
\end{equation}
Each entry stores, per sample position and per path,
the body-side arrival direction, BS-side departure direction,
polarisation-amplitude vector, intra-tissue attenuation, and
element-of-origin index. $\Dict$ is precomputed once by running
the base station's ray tracer over $\mathcal{G}$.
\end{definition}

For a $100\times 100$~m cell at 1~m grid, $|\mathcal{G}|=10^4$,
typical $|\{n\}|\le 20$ paths per point: $\Dict$ fits in tens
of MB. At 5~m body diameter and typical walking
trajectories, three to five neighbouring grid points cover any
body; bilinear or nearest-neighbour interpolation is sufficient
between grid points. Pose-dependent operator refresh then
reduces to
\begin{enumerate}
\item look up paths for $\rr_0\approx\mathbf{t}_u$ in $\Dict$;
\item build $\Jmat$ from element-of-origin indices and the
      body-frame $\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm 0,\bm\theta_u)$ from the
      current pose mesh over the looked-up paths;
\item apply the translation phasor
      $\Phimat(\mathbf{t}_u)$ per \cref{eq:Mphase}.
\end{enumerate}
No new ray tracing at runtime.

\subsection{Closing the in-silico--deployed-channel gap via CSI calibration}

\robinnote{This is the crucial bridge from a simulation toy to
something the reviewer believes could deploy. The twin's RT is
\emph{physically plausible but not exact}. The CSI scaling is
the self-correction: whenever the twin can compare its predicted
CSI to a real UE's measured CSI, it updates its path
amplitudes. The technique is standard in differentiable
RT (Sionna~\cite{Sionna}, DiffeRT~\cite{DiffeRT});
we use it in a plug-and-play role here. No novel algorithm
required.}

Ray-traced path amplitudes carry residual error relative to a
deployed channel: uncatalogued scatterers, foliage, building
material mismatches, weather. The deployed twin closes this gap
via uplink-CSI-based calibration of dictionary amplitudes.

Formally, for each served user $k$ at position
$\mathbf{t}_k$, the twin predicts the UE channel
$\hat\hh_k=\bm\alpha_k^T\Phimat(\mathbf{t}_k)\Jmat$ from its
looked-up path set, with $\bm\alpha_k$ the UE-antenna-projected
per-path amplitudes. The deployed UE reports a measured
$\hh_k^{\mathrm{meas}}$. The twin fits a per-path complex scale
factor $\bm\beta_k\in\Complex^N$ minimising
$\|\hh_k^{\mathrm{meas}}-\bm\beta_k^T\cdot\hat\hh_k\|^2$,
yielding a scene-specific calibration that propagates to every
$\Q^{(u)}$ derived from the same dictionary entries. This is a
least-squares refinement of existing differentiable-RT
calibration~\cite{Sionna,DiffeRT,Hoydis2024} specialised to the
body-exposure consumer of the path set; the algorithm is not
a contribution of the present paper.

The practical effect: the body-side arrival
\emph{directions} $\{\khat_n\}$ (which determine the rank
structure of $\Q^{(u)}$) come from the scene geometry
untouched; the path \emph{amplitudes} (which determine the
magnitude of exposure) are scaled to reflect deployed-channel
reality. This is the cross-layer feedback asked for in the SI
CfP, implemented as a parameter update between the twin's RT
kernel and the RAN channel-estimation stack.

\subsection{Closed-loop operation summary}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Every 1~ms: BS measures $\hh_k$, looks up cached
    $\{\Q^{(u)}\}$, solves \eqref{eq:closedform}, transmits.
\item Every 1~s: BS updates $\mathbf{t}_u$ from IMU+GPS (tier
    A/B) or ISAC RCS (tier C), applies translation phasor
    \eqref{eq:Mphase} to cached
    $\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm 0,\bm\theta_u)$, accepts or promotes
    newly detected bodies. Updates calibration $\bm\beta_k$ from
    UE CSI.
\item Every 100~ms: BS ingests tier-A/B pose, re-evaluates
    $\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm 0,\bm\theta_u)$ via AEGIS direct pipeline.
\item Static: $\Dict$ refreshes on scene change (building
    construction, not pedestrian motion).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{When the cap actually binds}\label{sec:binding}

\robinnote{I committed to answering the ``is it moot'' question
inside the paper instead of hoping no reviewer flags it. The
back-of-envelope table ringfences the binding regime; the
AEGIS measurement will confirm or restrict. If AEGIS shows the
cap doesn't bind at 55~dBm / 25-user MRT, we lean on the
chronic-dose argument (\S\ref{sec:chronic}) and keep the

A back-of-envelope for a $K$-user MRT precoder at 55~dBm total
EIRP, bystander at range $r$ in the served-user beam, yields
per-operator free-space power density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
  S(r,K)\approx \frac{\Ptx}{4\pi r^2 K}
  = \frac{25~\mathrm{mW/m^2}}{K}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{10~\mathrm{m}}{r}\Bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
Summed across 4 cumulative-cap operators, the binding distance
$r^\star$ at which $\sum_o S_o=S_{\mathrm{lim}}=0.56$~W/m$^2$ is
\begin{equation}
  r^\star(K)\approx \frac{6.7~\mathrm{m}}{\sqrt{K/4}}\cdot\sqrt{4\cdot 1}.
\end{equation}
At $K=1$: $r^\star\approx 13$~m. At $K=5$: $r^\star\approx 6$~m. At
$K=25$: $r^\star\approx 2.7$~m. The binding regime is
\emph{close-range bystanders} and \emph{low-user-count slots},
not typical macro 25-user operation. \cref{tab:bind} shows the
envelope.

\subsection{Chronic-dose reduction}\label{sec:chronic}

Even where instantaneous RL compliance is easy, long-term
downlink dose $\int\Pabs\,\diff t$ accumulates at a continuous
duty cycle, in contrast to the intermittent uplink.
EU-funded GOLIAT and related programmes~\cite{GOLIAT} identify
this chronic-dose term as the primary
public-health concern for continuous network operation. The
proposed precoder reduces bystander chronic dose at near-zero
sum-rate cost independent of any instantaneous cap, which is a
deployable value proposition distinct from the binding-regime
story above.

\section{Regulatory Framing}\label{sec:regulatory}

\robinnote{This is the walked-back, two-pronged version. I am
\emph{not} claiming the BR-path is a slam dunk that Brussels
must accept. I am claiming (i) our method is an instance of
ICNIRP-permitted BR-based audit, (ii) our method reduces RL at
occupied points where exposure actually happens, and (iii) a
statutory extension would let jurisdictions accept the BR-audit
path. Three honest claims, none of them over-reaching.}

\subsection{ICNIRP basic-restriction audit pathway}

ICNIRP~2020 permits compliance demonstration through either the
reference level or direct assessment of the basic restriction.
IEC/IEEE~62232:2022 and ITU-T~K.122 operationalize the BR path
for fixed-site installations via an
``actual-exposure-scenario'' procedure that admits
body-phantom-based dosimetry. Our method is an instance of that
procedure: the per-body $\Q^{(u)}$ is a first-principles
computation of absorbed power on a physiologically faithful
anatomical model, pose-conditioned when pose is known and
Cauchy-worst-case-bounded otherwise, enforced at every slot by
construction of the precoder.

\subsection{Jurisdictional reference-level enforcement}

Jurisdictions that enforce RL-only --- Brussels under the current
arr\^et\'e, France for child handset exposure, Italy's 2024
revision --- are not bound by the ICNIRP framework they derive
their limits from. The Brussels cap (14.57~V/m cumulative
outdoor, \TODO{6-min averaging window per agent run; cross-check
in committed version}) operates at the RL layer, at every
publicly accessible point. Our method does not claim a statutory
exemption. Rather, the proposed precoder's RL footprint is
\emph{strictly reduced at occupied public points} --- a subset of
all public points, but the subset where exposure actually occurs
--- which is the dosimetrically relevant contribution to the
jurisdictional compliance story.

\subsection{Regulator-auditable twin}

The twin exposes two hooks a regulator can audit: the registered
pose dictionary $\Theta$ for the Cauchy worst-case bound, and
the in-silico replay of $\xx^H\Q^{(u)}\xx$ against the submitted
compliance certificate. An accompanying open-source AEGIS
toolkit~\cite{AEGIS} enables the regulator to reproduce and
spot-check operator compliance on challenge bodies and poses.
This is the \emph{open interface and intent-based API}
\cite{JSAC_CFP} the SI asks for, applied to
safety-critical compliance rather than throughput KPIs.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have placed the human body in the base-station precoder's
control loop, closing a twin-in-the-loop architecture over the
application-aware goal of tissue-protection compliance. The
per-body coherent exposure operator
$\Q^{(u)}=\Jmat^T\Mmat^{(u)}\Jmat$ enters a multi-body
exposure-constrained precoder whose closed form reduces to
classical zero-forcing in the rank-one limit; a scene-ray-traced
path dictionary indexed by body position supplies $\Jmat$ and
$\Mmat^{(u)}$ at runtime; a CSI-calibration layer bridges the
in-silico dictionary and the deployed channel; a Cauchy
pose-agnostic bound closes the compliance statement for
bystanders without telemetry. The loop closes every 1~ms over a
twin assembled from commercial 3GPP primitives, IMU telemetry,
and one ray tracer.

Deferred to follow-on work: (i) pose-differentiable
$\Q(\bm\theta)$ for near-field UE-side compliance; (ii) a
Shapley-based cooperative multi-operator exposure-budget
allocation; (iii) RIS-assisted compliance
buyback~\cite{RIS-EMF2022} at the coverage edge; (iv)
a monostatic sensing extension via the pseudo-Brewster
exposure-scattering duality~\cite{QComplement}; (v) a
standardised twin-audit protocol aligned with
IEC/IEEE~62232:2022.

\section{Fresnel transmission operator $\mathbf{F}_n$}\label{app:fresnel}

For incidence direction $\khat$ onto a half-space of complex
refractive index $\tilde n=n-i\kappa$, Snell's law extends to
complex angles: $\sin\theta_t=\sin\theta_i/\tilde n$. Define
$\xi=\sqrt{\tilde n^2-1+\mu^2}$ with $\Re(\xi)>0$. The Fresnel
amplitude coefficients are
\begin{equation}
  r_s=\frac{\mu-\xi}{\mu+\xi},\qquad
  r_p=\frac{\tilde n^2\mu-\xi}{\tilde n^2\mu+\xi}.
\end{equation}
Power transmittance $T_\sigma=1-|r_\sigma|^2$ for $\sigma\in\{s,p\}$.
The transmission operator $\mathbf{F}_n(\rr)\in\Complex^{3\times 3}$
projects incident polarisation onto the transmitted field at
depth zero, with amplitude $t_\sigma=\sqrt{T_\sigma}$ per
polarisation; its matrix form in the local TE/TM basis is in
\cite[App.~B]{WydaegheMonograph}. Under the
pseudo-Brewster compensation mechanism for skin at mmWave,
$T_s(\theta)\approx T_p(\theta)\approx T_0$ to within a few
percent for $\theta<70^{\circ}$, reducing the operator to a
direction-insensitive scalar in the paper's regime.

\section{Cauchy bound $D(\khat)\le 2$}\label{app:cauchy}

For a convex body, $\Aperp(\khat)\le A/2$ since the shadow
boundary is a closed curve partitioning $\Sigma$ into two pieces
of unequal area but the visible piece along $\khat$ is the
smaller: the visible-hemisphere integral of $\mu_+$ sums with the
invisible-hemisphere integral over the same body to $A/2$ by
reflection symmetry of $\mu\to-\mu$. Hence
$D(\khat)=4\Aperp/\Aab\le 4(A/2)/A=2$. Non-convex: replace $A$
with $A_{\mathrm{CH}}$ of the convex hull, $D\le 2A_{\mathrm{CH}}/\Aab$.
Full proof and refinements in~\cite[\S5.3]{WydaegheMonograph}.

\section{Pose-agnostic operator bound}\label{app:cauchy-bound}

\emph{Proof of \cref{thm:cauchy-bound}}: Start from the
angular-spectrum form
$\Pabs^{(u)}(\xx)=\Sinc^{(u)}T_0\Aab^{(u)}/4\cdot
\int\rho(\khat;\xx)D(\khat;\bm\theta_u)\diff\Omega/4\pi$
where $\rho(\khat;\xx)$ is the precoded beam's angular spectrum
at body $u$'s position (normalised by $\int\rho=4\pi$ for
isotropic comparison). Bound
$D(\khat;\bm\theta_u)\le D_{\max}^{(u)}\le 2A_{\mathrm{CH}}^{(u)}/\Aab^{(u)}$
uniformly. Within the BS's beam angular spread, $\rho$
concentrates on the steering direction
$\avec^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BS}}$, and
$\int\rho\diff\Omega/4\pi\le|\avec_{\mathrm{BS}}^H\xx|^2/\Ptx$.
Multiply through to obtain \cref{eq:cauchy-bound-op}. See
\cite{WydaegheMonograph} \S5.4 for the angular-spread correction
that removes the inequality in the monograph's
hero-experiment-level refinement.
