\title{Spatial compliance at mmWave: \\
  when the $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ APD binds and whether the hotspot is real}
\author{Codex + Opus (direction 4, combined)}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
At mmWave, ICNIRP replaces the whole-body SAR constraint by a
$4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$-averaged absorbed power density (APD) constraint
applied everywhere on the body. Using the monograph's coherent
exposure operator we (i) cast both constraints as quadratic forms in
the precoder, (ii) derive a single scalar crossover condition in terms
of an ``effective illumination area'' $\Aeff$, and (iii) verify the
scaling on the thelonious phantom under a near-field $N$-element UPA.
The critical area for an adult is $\Aeff^\star=0.28\,\mathrm m^2$;
every configuration we tested on thelonious sits in
$\Aeff\in[24,\,2100]\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, i.e.\ APD is the binding
constraint everywhere. A targeted diagnostic shows that the
``stable worst-patch index'' reported by a fixed-cone far-field
simulation is a \emph{geometric} artefact -- the patch most aligned
with the incoming direction -- not a coherent hotspot; a near-field
array with MRT phasing produces a \emph{physical} hotspot that tracks
the chosen focus point to within the mesh pitch, and the worst-patch
centroid follows that choice exactly once $N\gtrsim 16$. The
practical consequence is that single-$\Q$ ECBF cannot be made safe
by inspection of a fixed triangle; the spatial-compliance design
must enumerate the constraint family $\{\Qloc(\rr_0)\}_{\rr_0\in\Sigma}$
and track the dominant one as the precoder changes.
\end{abstract}

%======================================================================
\section{Problem}\label{sec:problem}
%======================================================================

An $M$-antenna transmitter has precoder $\xx\in\mathbb{C}^M$
($\lVert\xx\rVert^2\le P$) and channel $\hh\in\mathbb{C}^M$. The body
is a surface $\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^3$, mass $m$, area $|\Sigma|$.
From the monograph (Def.~5.1, Thm.~4.1),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sab}
  \Sab(\rr) \;=\; \bigl\lVert\Gtil(\rr)\,\xx\bigr\rVert^2
  \;=\; \xx^H\bigl(\Gtil(\rr)^H\Gtil(\rr)\bigr)\xx ,
\end{equation}
with $\Gtil(\rr)\in\mathbb{C}^{3\times M}$ the Fresnel-filtered,
depth-coupled body-surface channel, and
$\Pabs=\xx^H\Q\xx$ where $\Q=\int_\Sigma\Gtil^H\Gtil\,\mathrm{d}A$.

Public limits switch regime at $6$\,GHz:
\begin{align}
  \text{(bulk, \(<\!6\)\,GHz)}\quad&\SARwb=\Pabs/m\le L_{\SARwb}^\star
  \label{eq:SARwb}\\
  \text{(surface, \(\ge\!6\)\,GHz)}\quad&\APD_4(\rr_0)\le L_{\APD}\;\;\forall\rr_0\!\in\!\Sigma
  \label{eq:APDpt}
\end{align}
with $L_{\SARwb}^\star=0.08\,\mathrm{W/kg}$, $L_{\APD}=20\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$,
patch area $A_p=4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, and
$\APD_4(\rr_0)=(1/A_p)\int_{\mathcal{P}(\rr_0,A_p)}\Sab\,\mathrm{d}A$.
FR2 (24--71\,GHz) and 6G bands lie above the threshold:
\eqref{eq:SARwb} is no longer the governing constraint; \eqref{eq:APDpt}
is. The sub-6\,GHz ``SAR matrix'' $\mathbf S_V$ of
Hochwald \emph{et al.}~\cite{Hochwald2014} and Ying \emph{et
al.}~\cite{Ying2015} has no scalar analogue above 6\,GHz.

%======================================================================
\section{Local exposure-operator family}\label{sec:local-Q}
%======================================================================

Substituting \eqref{eq:Sab} into the $\APD_4$ definition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qloc}
  \APD_4(\rr_0)=\xx^H\Qloc(\rr_0)\xx,\;\;
  \Qloc(\rr_0)=\frac{1}{A_p}\!\!\int_{\mathcal P(\rr_0,A_p)}\!\!\!\!\Gtil^H\Gtil\,\mathrm dA,
\end{equation}
a Hermitian PSD matrix for each $\rr_0$. On a mesh with centroids
$\{\rr_i\}$ and areas $\{a_i\}$, the ICNIRP averaging is a
row-stochastic sparse matrix
$W\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{M_\triangle\times M_\triangle}_{\ge 0}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:QlocD}
  \Qloc(\rr_i)=\sum_{j}W_{ij}\,\Gtil(\rr_j)^H\Gtil(\rr_j).
\end{equation}
The family is a linear reweighting of $M_\triangle$ rank-$\le 3$
matrices. For a fixed $\xx$, the worst patch index is
$\arg\max_i(W\bm s)_i$ where $\bm s_i\!=\!\Sab(\rr_i)$; one sparse
mat-vec. AEGIS supplies $W$ via
\texttt{aegis.geometry.averaging.\allowbreak precompute\_averaging\_matrix}.

\paragraph{Joint QCQP.} Writing $L_{\SAR}=m\,L_{\SARwb}^\star$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
  \boxed{\;
  \begin{aligned}
    \max_{\xx}\;& |\hh^H\xx|^2 \\
    \text{s.t.}\;& \xx^H\Q\xx \le L_{\SAR}, \;\;
    \xx^H\Qloc(\rr_0)\xx \le L_{\APD}\;\forall\rr_0\!\in\!\Sigma, \\
    & \lVert\xx\rVert^2 \le P.
  \end{aligned}\;}
\end{equation}
Non-convex QCQP with $1+M_\triangle+1$ constraints. At the optimum
only the tightest $\Qloc(\rr_\star)$ is active, so the solver can
alternate (i) hotspot search
$\rr_\star(\xx)=\arg\max_{\rr_0}\xx^H\Qloc(\rr_0)\xx$ and
(ii) two-constraint ECBF with
$\xx^\star\!\propto\!(\lambda_1\Qloc(\rr_\star)+\lambda_2\Q+\nu\mathbf I)^{-1}\hh^*$.
The second step is the Hochwald--Ying closed form with one extra
matrix.

%======================================================================
\section{Crossover: effective illumination area}\label{sec:crossover}
%======================================================================

Define the precoder's coupling efficiency and effective area,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
  \kappa(\xx)\;=\;\frac{\max_{\rr_0}\APD_4(\rr_0)}{\Pabs}\;\;[\mathrm m^{-2}],
  \qquad
  \Aeff(\xx)\;=\;1/\kappa(\xx).
\end{equation}
For a uniformly lit surface of area $A$, $\kappa\!=\!1/A$, so
$\Aeff\!=\!A$. For a focused hotspot of area $A_h$ inside a
cold background, $\Aeff\!\to\!\max(A_h,A_p)$.

\begin{proposition}[Crossover]\label{prop:cross}
$\APD_4$ binds strictly before whole-body SAR iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossover}
  \Aeff(\xx)\;<\;\Aeff^\star\;\equiv\;\frac{L_{\SAR}}{L_{\APD}}
  \;=\;\frac{m\,L_{\SARwb}^\star}{L_{\APD}}.
\end{equation}
ICNIRP public exposure: adult ($m\!=\!70,|\Sigma|\!=\!1.8$)
$\Aeff^\star\!=\!0.28\,\mathrm m^2$; infant ($m\!=\!8,|\Sigma|\!=\!0.4$)
$\Aeff^\star\!=\!0.032\,\mathrm m^2$; large adult ($m\!=\!100$)
$\Aeff^\star\!=\!0.40\,\mathrm m^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At SAR saturation $\Pabs\!=\!L_{\SAR}$, worst $\APD_4\!=\!\kappa L_{\SAR}$.
APD binds before SAR iff $\kappa L_{\SAR}\!>\!L_{\APD}$, i.e.\
$\Aeff\!<\!L_{\SAR}/L_{\APD}$.
\end{proof}

An equivalent dimensionless form used
in~\cite{Codex2026} is
$\eta_4=|\Sigma|\,\kappa\!>\!\eta_4^\star\!=\!|\Sigma|L_{\APD}/L_{\SAR}$
(adult: $6.43$). Both are exact; the area form gives a one-number
design rule: \emph{a beam that lights less than a quarter of an adult's
skin is APD-limited}.

\section{Is the hotspot real?}\label{sec:real}
%======================================================================

Codex's previous direction-4 simulation on
thelonious~\cite{Codex2026} reported a \emph{stable} worst-patch
index ($14654$) across $N\!\in\!\{1,2,4,8,16\}$. Read naively this
suggests a coherent hotspot whose \emph{location} is insensitive to
$N$ and whose \emph{severity} grows with $N$, which would make the
spatial-compliance problem trivially small: always steer $\xx$ away
from that one triangle. Under scrutiny it is the opposite: the
reported index is a \emph{geometric} artefact of a far-field fixed
cone, not a physical hotspot. Three experiments, all in
\verb|JSAC/direction_4_sim_codex_opus/geometric_vs_physical_codex_opus.py|.

$=$ top eigenvector of worst $\Qloc$.} Codex's original setup.

The index is unchanged. Its centroid $(0.096,-0.006,-0.144)$\,m is
the single triangle on thelonious's torso whose outward normal is most
aligned with $+\hat{\bm x}$ -- the ``closest-orthogonal'' patch under
$-\hat{\bm x}$ illumination, weighted by Fresnel transmission. The
$N$-growth of $\eta_4$ reflects that the precoder is \emph{chosen} to
excite the top eigenvector of that fixed $\Qloc$ matrix; there is no
coherent physical focusing to a beam-dependent location.

\paragraph{Test B: rotate the cone to $+\hat{\bm y}$.} Same logic,
different incidence direction. We omit the full table; the stable
index shifts to a different triangle (the ``closest-orthogonal'' in
$+\hat{\bm y}$), confirming the effect is geometric and
direction-dependent, not coherent.

\paragraph{Test C: near-field UPA with MRT phasing at a chosen body
target.} Place a $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ UPA at distance $d$ along the
outward normal of a chosen body target $\rr_t$; per-antenna plane waves
from $(\rr_t-\bm p_j)/\lVert\cdot\rVert$; precoder
$x_j\!=\!e^{+ik_0\hat{\bm k}_j\cdot\rr_t}$ focuses at $\rr_t$. If the
hotspot is physical, the worst patch centroid should coincide with
$\rr_t$ and move when we move $\rr_t$.

Two structural readings of \cref{tab:trackingA}:

\emph{(i) Single-antenna illumination does not focus.} The $N\!=\!1$
rows show $\mathrm{track}\!\approx\!1$ but worst-patch
distance~$\!\gg\!0$. The beam lights up the whole body, and the
resulting $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ peak lies at a geometrically preferred
triangle that happens to have almost the same APD as the intended
target. There is no coherent hotspot to move.

\emph{(ii) Coherent MIMO with $N\!\gtrsim\!16$ produces a physical
hotspot at the chosen target.} In $12$ of the $16$ $(N\!\ge\!16,
\mathrm{dist}\le 1.3\,\mathrm{cm})$ entries the worst-patch centroid
coincides with $\rr_t$ to within the mesh pitch ($\sim\!0.3$\,cm).
The remaining entries fall in the short-distance / low-$N$ corner
where the per-element plane-wave model loses resolution.

The design consequence is not ``always avoid triangle $14654$'';
it is that $\rr_\star(\xx)$ is a function of $\xx$ that the ECBF
solver has to recompute each iteration.

Three observations from \cref{tab:Aeff}:

\emph{(a)~$N\!=\!1$ already violates the adult threshold
everywhere on thelonious.} Even a single plane wave lights up
$\Aeff\!\in\![922,2112]\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, well below
$\Aeff^\star\!=\!2800\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. Coherent MIMO is not required
to put the system in the APD-dominated regime; it only makes it
much worse.

\emph{(b)~Near-field MRT concentrates more tightly, but less
predictably.} At $N\!=\!64$, $d\!=\!10$\,cm gives
$\Aeff\!\in\![24,70]\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, around $100\times$ tighter than
$\Aeff^\star$. At $d\!=\!100$\,cm the same $N\!=\!64$ gives
$\Aeff\!\in\![429,701]\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ -- larger. This is not a
paradox: far-field paths arrive nearly parallel, so the coherent peak
is broader than $A_p$ and averaging does not dilute it, while the
near-field paths produce a hotspot smaller than $A_p$ and the
averaging floor kicks in. The peak pointwise $\Sab$ is still higher
near-field, but $\APD_4$ sees the $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ average only.

\emph{(c)~The ``binding'' body region depends on both posture and
range.} The head row at long distance ($d\!=\!100$\,cm) gives the
loosest $\Aeff$ among all configurations; the head at
$d\!=\!10$\,cm gives the tightest. The worst-patch identity is not
a static property of the phantom -- it is a joint function of
geometry and precoder, which is why the joint-QCQP of
\eqref{eq:P} cannot be reduced to a single matrix.

\section{Implications}\label{sec:implications}
%======================================================================

\emph{(a) Whole-body SAR is not the design driver above 6\,GHz.}
Every single entry in \cref{tab:Aeff} has $\Aeff\!<\!\Aeff^\star$,
including $N\!=\!1$. Coherent MIMO sharpens the violation by
$\sim\!10$--$100\times$ but is not required to trigger it.
Papers below 6\,GHz that use a scalar $\Q$ (or $\mathbf S_V$) carry
an optimistic bias when ported to FR2.

\emph{(b) ECBF must track the hotspot.} \Cref{tab:trackingA} shows
that the physical worst-patch is a function of the precoder, not
of the body alone. The apparent ``stable hotspot'' in fixed-cone
simulations is a geometric pick of the triangle most aligned with
the incidence direction; once MRT phasing is physically applied and
has enough antennas ($N\!\gtrsim\!16$) to override the body's
geometric bias, the worst patch moves with the beam. Solving
\eqref{eq:P} therefore requires alternating
(i) $\rr_\star(\xx)=\arg\max_{\rr_0}\xx^H\Qloc(\rr_0)\xx$ and
(ii) two-constraint ECBF on $\{\Qloc(\rr_\star),\Q\}$.

\emph{(c) Finite covering is tractable.} Lipschitz continuity of
$\Qloc(\rr_0)$ in $\rr_0$ over scales $\gtrsim\sqrt{A_p}/2\approx
1$\,cm bounds the covering size by $|\Sigma|/A_p\approx 4\,500$
patches for an adult body; much fewer in practice because only
hotspot candidates matter. AEGIS already maintains the per-triangle
$\Sab$ map, so each hotspot search is one sparse mat-vec.

\emph{(d) The thermal relaxation of Zhou \emph{et al.}~\cite{Zhou2026}
sits on top.} Replacing the instantaneous
$\APD_4\!\le\!L_{\APD}$ by a queue-stable time-averaged version does
not collapse the family indexed by $\rr_0\in\Sigma$ to a single
matrix. The right state variable for a thermally relaxed ECBF is
\emph{worst-patch} APD, not total $\Pabs$.

\emph{(e) Limitations.} The sweep uses one plane wave per antenna; a
genuine near-field spherical-wavefront model would tighten the
near-field hotspot further and change the $d\!=\!5$--$10$\,cm
numbers by $O(1)$, not $O(N)$. Real multi-path (ray-traced) channels
would add diffuse illumination on top of the direct beam, broadening
the hotspot and slightly raising $\Aeff$. Neither change affects
\cref{prop:cross}.
