1. Keep. It. Stupid. Simple. KISS
- Always the simplest 'academically valid' synonym of various words, especially for verbs (is, have, ... are gold).
- Sentence structure with subject-verb(prefer present tense)-therest. 
2. Never ;, no em dash, avoid interjectional subsectences
3. Staccato, simple, short sentences. Super clear. Impossible to critique. Stands like a bus.
3a. Long sentences are tolerable when the structure is **subject — verb — long list**. The reader handles the cognitive load every time. "The closed form matches Mie theory on lossy spheres, full polarization-aware Fresnel on the Thelonious phantom, Sim4Life FDTD at 5.8 GHz, and the dosimetry literature across 168 volunteers and 5 FDTD phantoms" is fine.
3b. Use **`resp.`** wherever it suits. The reader handles it. The full word **`respectively`** is equally fine. Neither counts toward the cognitive-load budget: parallel constructions of the form "X and Y are A and B, respectively" are clearer than splitting into two sentences and should be used freely.
4. Avoid we or passive forms, circumventing the entire discussion of which to pick. Unless it's really kinda needed and it would be make the alternative awkward.
5. I love connectors, especially the simplest kind.
- Therefore, ...
- However, ...
- First, ... . Second, ... . Third, ... .
I love it when a text just flows. Something that really builds, as if the prose itself is a dumb mathematical derivation.
