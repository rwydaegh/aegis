Subject: Distance and uncertainty for the normalized SAR values

Dear Hamed,

Thanks for the two clear questions at the dose-model meeting. Both are ready: a
way to correct a normalized SAR value for distance, and an uncertainty estimate for
each value. The short version is below. The detail is in the attached report and
Excel file.

I moved the dosimetry to a new method. It is fast, so this time I did not hold
distance and orientation fixed. The sweep was 4 body models, 8 bands, 3 phone
positions, distances from 5 to 400 mm, and tens of orientations at each: 69,216
configurations. Each one would be a separate full-wave run. It finished in a few
minutes. So for every value you now get a distance curve and a spread, not a single
point.

1. Distance correction

Near the body the dose follows an inverse-square law with a small offset:

    SAR(d) = SAR(d_ref) * ( (d_ref + delta) / (d + delta) )^2

Here d is the phone-to-body distance, d_ref is the distance the value was computed
at (200 mm in front of the eyes), and delta is about 6 mm. Beyond a few centimetres
this is the plain inverse-square rule: halve the distance, and the dose is about
four times higher. The fit is tight, with R^2 = 0.9999.

For your brain value, 2.62 W/kg per W for Duke at 200 mm, this gives:

    100 mm  ->  9.9   (about 3.8 times higher)
    150 mm  ->  4.6
    200 mm  ->  2.62  (reference)
    300 mm  ->  1.2
    400 mm  ->  0.68

The plot fig_distance_factor shows the curve with the reference marked. The full
table is in the Excel sheet brain_2.62_adjusted.

2. Uncertainty

For each value I give the mean, standard deviation, min, max, and the 5th and 95th
percentiles. For a fixed body the main source is how the phone is held. For the
Duke brain value that is about 27% (one standard deviation), so

    2.62 W/kg per W  ->  2.6 +/- 0.7,

with the full range over orientations from about 0.8 to 3.8. Body model adds
another 30 to 40%: smaller bodies absorb more per kilogram, so a child is higher
than the adult. The per-scenario tables are in the sheets named unc_.

One honest note. The method does not replace full-wave. Your absolute numbers stay
the full-wave ones. The distance correction and the spread are ratios around them,
so the parts that are hardest to model mostly cancel.
