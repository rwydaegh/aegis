\section{Validation against exact references}\label{sec:validation}

\providecommand{\SAR}{\mathrm{SAR}}
\providecommand{\rhom}{\rho_{\mathrm{m}}}
\providecommand{\Tzero}{T_{0}}
\providecommand{\Qabs}{Q_{\mathrm{abs}}}

The framework is validated against problems whose answers are known exactly. The
absorbed power density $\Sab(\rr) = \Sinc\,\Teff\,\ReLU(\mu)$, the Fock shadow
gate of \cref{sec:fock-local}, and the differentiable JAX implementation are
each measured against a closed-form oracle rather than against another
approximation. Two analytic oracles carry the load: the Mie sphere, which is the
continuous-integration canary, and the Bessel-Hankel cylinder, which resolves
the diffraction physics at a shadow edge. This section defines both, tabulates
the validated numbers, and states the error budget.

\subsection{The two exact oracles}
\label{sec:val-oracles}

Both oracles use the same physical observable as the framework: the absorbed
power density is the time-averaged Poynting flux flowing inward through the body
surface, evaluated from the exact exterior total field. No interior field model
enters the observable, so the comparison is apples-to-apples with $\Sab$.

\begin{definition}[Mie sphere oracle]\label{def:mie-oracle}
For a lossy dielectric sphere of size parameter $x = k_0 a$ and complex
refractive index $\ntilde = n - \mathrm{i}\kappa$ illuminated by a plane wave,
Mie theory gives the exact spherical-harmonic series for the absorption
efficiency $\Qabs$, the ratio of absorption cross section to geometric cross
section $\pi a^2$. The surface observable is the inward radial Poynting vector
just outside $r = a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mie-poynting}
  \Sab^{\mathrm{exact}}(\theta,\phi)
  = -\half\,\RE\!\left(E_\theta H_\phi^{*} - E_\phi H_\theta^{*}\right),
\end{equation}
with the spherical field components from the validated near-field routine. The
surface integral of \cref{eq:mie-poynting} recovers the Mie $\Qabs$ to
$0.7\%$, which certifies the observable. The sphere has double curvature and no
two-dimensional line resonance, so it isolates curvature effects cleanly.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Bessel-Hankel cylinder oracle]\label{def:cyl-oracle}
For a homogeneous circular cylinder (the canonical limb), two-dimensional
plane-wave scattering has a closed-form Bessel-Hankel series for both lossy
dielectric and PEC. The exterior field is expanded in $H_n^{(1)}$ (time factor
$e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}$), and the interior enters only through the numerically
stable log-derivative $D_n = J_n'(k_1 a)/J_n(k_1 a)$ by downward recurrence, so
there is no large-argument Bessel overflow for the lossy interior. The
observable is the inward radial Poynting flux from the exterior total field.
Cylinder TM$_z$ (E along the axis) is the Fresnel s-polarisation and the soft
(Dirichlet) case; TE$_z$ (H along the axis) is Fresnel p-polarisation and the
hard (Neumann) case.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The sign convention trap]\label{rem:sign-trap}
AEGIS stores $\ntilde = n - \mathrm{i}\kappa$ for the $e^{+\mathrm{i}\omega t}$
convention, whereas the cylinder oracle integrates outgoing $H_n^{(1)}$ under
$e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}$, which requires $\IM(\ntilde) > 0$ for a passive lossy
medium. Feeding the stored $\IM(\ntilde) < 0$ models a gain cylinder and
corrupts the surface-bound hard creeping wave specifically. The oracle
conjugates to $\IM(\ntilde) > 0$ before solving; a convention-free check
confirms the fix, since $Q_{\mathrm{sca}}$ exceeds its lossless value (negative
$\Qabs$, unphysical gain) only for the stored sign. The Mie oracle uses
\texttt{miepython}, whose $n - \mathrm{i}\kappa$ convention makes the stored
constant correct there, which is why the sphere was clean from the start.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Lit-region and phantom accuracy}
\label{sec:val-lit}

On the sphere at size parameter $x = 240$, flat-interface Fresnel reproduces the
exact $\Sab$ to about $1\%$ out to $66^\circ$ incidence for both polarisations,
departing only within $\sim 15^\circ$ of grazing where the penumbra rises ($1.46$
for s-pol and $1.35$ for p-pol at $82^\circ$, nearly polarisation-symmetric).
There is no large p-polarisation lit-region curvature coupling on a doubly
curved body, so flat Fresnel plus the per-triangle polarisation weighting is
correct in the lit region. The earlier $1.4\times$ to $5\times$ p-polarisation
enhancement seen on the cylinder was the gain-sign artifact of
\cref{rem:sign-trap} stacked with a closed-cylinder surface-wave resonance; with
the physical index, lit-region exact-to-GO converges to $-1.00$ for both
polarisations at every tested incidence by $x = 320$.

On the Thelonious phantom (23\,826 triangles) the geometric law
$\Sab = \Sinc\Tzero\ReLU(\mu)$ matches the full angle-dependent Fresnel
integration to $0.35\%$ in total absorbed power, $0.5\%$ in mean $\Sab$, and
$0.0\%$ in peak $\Sab$ (skin, 28\,GHz). The local error over the 4\,907
triangles with $\theta<75^\circ$ is mean $-2.6\%$, RMS $3.2\%$, conservative
because $\Tavg(\theta)>\Tzero$ in this band. Against exact Mie absorption the
framework underestimates by $3$ to $14\%$ for torso and head in the 28 to
60\,GHz band, dominated by diffraction into the geometric shadow that the
geometric law misses, with the deficit shrinking as the size parameter grows
(Fock scaling $x^{-2/3}$).

\subsection{Diffraction at the shadow edge}
\label{sec:val-diffraction}

The cylinder oracle answers the question the surface law cannot answer about
itself: a limb's shadow edge is Fock diffraction by a smooth convex body, not a
knife edge. The deep-shadow field decays exponentially at a rate scaling as
$(k\Rfock)^{1/3}$ (Fock), not $(k\Rfock)^{1/2}$ (knife edge). A similarity
collapse confirms this: the exact curves for many $k\Rfock$ collapse onto one
universal curve in the Fock variable $\xi = (k\Rfock/2)^{1/3}\theta$ but spread
in the knife variable $\theta\sqrt{k\Rfock}$.

\begin{proposition}[Fock scaling and the soft/hard split]\label{prop:fock}
The convex-body penumbra width scales as $(k\Rfock)^{-1/3}$, and the PEC
soft-to-hard shadow decay-rate ratio converges to
$q_{1,\mathrm{soft}}/q_{1,\mathrm{hard}} = 2.338/1.019 = 2.295$, the ratio of
the first zeros of $\Ai$ and $\Aip$. The heuristic GeLU gate, whose width
scales as $(k\Rfock)^{-1/2}$, has the wrong functional form: it is too narrow by
$(k\Rfock)^{1/6}$, a factor $1.6$ to $2.7$ across body parts at 28\,GHz.
\end{proposition}

The validated decay exponents sit near the Fock $1/3$ and far from the knife
$1/2$: PEC $0.35$ to $0.39$, skin soft $0.40$, skin hard $0.46$. On a lossy
surface the impedance damps the hard creeping wave, so the skin soft/hard decay
ratio drops to $1.6$ to $1.9$ (below the PEC $2.295$ and decreasing with
$k\Rfock$), pulling the hard eigenvalue from the PEC value $q_1 = 1.019$ up
toward $\sim 1.4$. This is captured by the impedance-Fock (Leontovich) creeping
pole $\Aip(t) - \qF\,\Ai(t) = 0$ with $\qF$ from the surface impedance
$\eta = 1/\ntilde$.

\begin{proposition}[Impedance pole matches the exact dielectric pole]\label{prop:pole}
At the physical sign ($\IM(\ntilde) > 0$), the Leontovich creeping pole
reproduces the exact dielectric p-polarisation shadow field to three digits. The
dominant pole slope $2\,\IM(\nu)$ matches the exact field at $k\Rfock = 40$
($5.654$ vs $5.652$), $k\Rfock = 80$ ($7.503$ vs $7.499$), and
$k\Rfock = 160$ ($10.099$ vs $10.085$). A three-route cross-check (analytic
zero, matrix-pencil extraction from the exact field, and measured slope) agrees,
and a 280-seed pole scan finds no slower physical pole, so no separate
surface-wave term is needed.
\end{proposition}

As the wavelength vanishes the Fock gate recovers the geometric $\ReLU(\mu)$
asymptotically (the penumbra width $(k\Rfock)^{-1/3}\to 0$), so the surface law
is the $\lambda\to 0$ limit of the diffraction model. Bit-exact ReLU remains
available by selecting the no-diffraction model.

\subsection{Dose impact and differentiability}
\label{sec:val-dose}

The diffraction upgrade is a pointwise correction near shadow edges, not a
whole-body mover. On the cylinder the integrated whole-limb change versus
$\ReLU$ is $2$ to $4\%$. On a real one-side-lit phantom (Duke, 56\,024
triangles, far-field plane wave) the whole-body SAR change versus $\ReLU$ is
$6$ to $7\%$, larger than the cylinder because a real phantom has far more
grazing and shadowed area under a single plane wave, while the pointwise change
peaks at $\sim 40\%$ in the penumbra near terminators. Deep-shadow creeping
leakage is real but carries only $\sim 10^{-6}$ of the lit dose, so it matters
only for dedicated deep-shadow or distal-occlusion studies, never for whole-body
or typical pointwise maps.

The gate is differentiable. Under the JAX backend, the gradient of the Fock gate
with respect to frequency matches a central finite difference to within $2\%$
(achieved relative error $4\times 10^{-9}$), and the gradient with respect to
the detour parameter $\xi$ is finite and flows cleanly. Differentiability is
preserved because the creeping eigenvalues are constants fed into the gate
rather than functions traced through the autodiff graph.

\subsection{Error budget}
\label{sec:val-budget}

\Cref{tab:val-budget} collects the framework error against exact electromagnetics
at mmWave. The Fresnel approximation contributes $\sim 5\%$ locally but only
$\sim 1\%$ integrated; diffraction into the shadow contributes $\sim 10\%$ for a
torso at 28\,GHz in the sphere worst case; curvature and near-field corrections
are sub-percent. These are dominated by the $\sim 20\%$ spread in the IT'IS
tissue database. The total framework error against exact EM is $10$ to $15\%$
(sphere worst case), rising to $15$ to $20\%$ once tissue uncertainty is
included. The sphere is a conservative bound: its uniform curvature maximises
both the Fresnel and diffraction errors, and a real body with large flat torso
regions has smaller net errors. The peak $\Sab$, which drives local exposure
limits, occurs at normal incidence where the Fresnel approximation is exact, so
the framework reproduces the peak faithfully.

The Mie comparison is the continuous-integration canary: a single regression
test asserts that the framework reproduces exact Mie absorption within tolerance
at body-relevant size parameters. It does not call the dosimetry kernel, so it
is unaffected by the Fock default, and it remains the fastest signal that the
physics is correct. If the Mie canary passes, the surface law, the Fresnel
coefficients, and the tissue model are all consistent with exact wave theory.
