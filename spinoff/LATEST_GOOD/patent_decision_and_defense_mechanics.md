## TL;DR

1. **File the priority application.** Cost to Robin: ~€0. UGent pays. Decision has to happen before the public defense because of EPO absolute novelty. Every step after priority has an off-ramp.
2. **"1-5 years" is not the question.** That's the time to *grant*. The decision gates are at month 0 (priority), month 12 (PCT), and month 30 (national entry). Everything between is free optionality. The only one that can't slip is month 0.

5. **Use the NDA jury + Biblio embargo as free insurance** against timing slip, not because they are strictly required.

## The patent staircase, high level

The confusion comes from mixing three timelines. Separate them.

### Stage 0: IDF (done, April 2026)
Internal UGent document. Not a patent. Does not establish legal priority. Triggers the TechTransfer IP team's decision on whether to file on UGent's behalf.

### Stage 1: Priority application — the defense deadline step
- A real patent application, filed at the Belgian national office or directly at EPO.
- Cost: ~€1,500 government fee + ~€2-5K patent attorney. **UGent pays entirely.**
- Timeline from IDF approval to filing: 5-12 weeks if TechTransfer moves. Realistic planning: **2-4 months end-to-end.**
- What it buys: the priority date. Every filing anywhere in the world after this date is later than Robin's.
- **This is the only step that has to happen before the PhD defense.** EPO absolute novelty means a public defense = disclosure = permanent priority loss if priority is filed after it.

### Stage 2: The 12-month optionality window
- Around month 6, the patent office returns a search report + preliminary opinion. First real signal on whether the claims survive prior art (Kodera 2024, Li 2019, Bamba).
- Months 0-12: Robin can drop (free), amend and refile (€1-2K), or proceed to PCT.
- This is the first "information revealed, re-evaluate" checkpoint.

### Stage 3: PCT (Patent Cooperation Treaty) at month 12 — optional placeholder
- **PCT is not a world patent.** It's a placeholder that preserves the priority date in 153+ member countries for an additional 18 months.
- Cost: ~€5-10K total (filing + attorney).
- UGent typically pays through PCT.
- After PCT filing, Robin has until month 30 (from priority date) to decide which specific countries to enter. This is the mechanism that lets you defer the expensive decision.

### Stage 4: National phase entry at month 30 — the real-money gate
- Must enter national phase in each country where actual rights are wanted.
- Each country: €3-15K filing + attorney + translations.
- Examination, office actions, amendments over 3-5 years: another €5-20K per country.
- Typical "US + EP + CN + JP" package: **€40-100K over 3-5 years.**
- UGent Technologietransfer page explicitly says: "given high costs, continuation is only considered if there is already a commitment from a commercial partner or a concrete valorisation strategy has been defined."
- By month 30, Robin will know whether any of: ZMT conversation advancing, JSAC published, LOI from test lab, VLAIO mandate granted. If yes to at least one, continue selectively. If no, UGent drops and so does Robin — at no meaningful sunk cost.

### Stage 5: Grant — year 4-6 from priority date
- The national patent offices examine, grant or reject. Annual maintenance fees for up to 20 years from priority.
- **This is the "1-5 years" timeline.** It is far after all the decision gates that matter for the spin-off strategy.

### The only clock that matters right now
Stage 1. Everything else has a structural off-ramp built in.

## UGent defense mechanics and the "public defense vs embargo" question

The OER doctoreren 2025-2026 (Faculty of Engineering) offers **three distinct mechanisms**. They are not one switch and they do different things.

### Article 27 §4 — Jury NDA during examination
If a patent application is being prepared when the examination committee is composed, the Valorisatiecel writes to the dean and:
- Internal UGent jurors: written notice that specific thesis parts are confidential.
- External jurors: NDA must be signed **before the thesis is sent to them**.
- The Valorisatiecel must strive to secure legal IP protection **before** the public defense.

### Article 30 §2 — Emergency procedure between committee appointment and defense
If patentable results emerge or the patent is still in preparation during this window, "noodprocedures voor voorwaardelijke publiekmaking" (emergency procedures for conditional publication) activate, explicitly to prevent loss of patent rights. The regulation's preferred outcome is unambiguous: **file the patent first, then keep the thesis unconditionally public.**

### Article 30 §8 — Biblio repository embargo
Default: open access under "zo open als mogelijk, zo gesloten als nodig". Access to the thesis in the Biblio repository can be temporarily or permanently restricted for legitimate reasons, explicitly including:
- IP / valorisation protection
- Contractual confidentiality
- **Plans to publish (parts of) the thesis in journals later** (the most common reason)
- Personal data, copyright

Side effect (Art. 30 §9): if you impose confidentiality, the public defense cannot be livestreamed. In-person only.

### The "paradox" resolved

The public defense does not require you to "tell everyone all the theory in ways that kill novelty" **if the priority patent is already filed**. Once priority is secured, the theory is legally protected — you can present it openly at the defense, at conferences, in papers. That is the entire point of filing first.

The Biblio embargo is about the **repository deposit** of the thesis text. It is not about the defense talk. A public defense with a Biblio-embargoed thesis is completely normal: you stand up, give your 45-minute talk, answer questions; the printed thesis copies go to the examination board and library, but the full text is not openly downloadable from Biblio for a year or two. Students who plan journal publications do this routinely for self-plagiarism reasons, with no IP implication at all.

### Where the real risk is
If priority is **not** filed before the public defense, then the public defense itself becomes a disclosure. In that case you have two bad options: delay the defense, or strip the novel substance from the public talk (which defeats the purpose of a public defense). Neither is pleasant. This is why Art. 30 §2 exists — to force the Valorisatiecel into crisis mode — but it is a safety net, not a plan.

**Bottom line: file the priority first, then the public defense is a normal public defense.**

## Contract-end vs defense timing

No hard regulatory deadline in the OER for how long after PhD contract end the defense must happen. You stay **enrolled** as a doctoral student until defense (annual re-enrollment), but that is administrative, not funded. The EPO novelty clock is triggered by the **public defense + Biblio deposit**, not the contract end. Benching for months between contract end and defense is common and IP-neutral provided you do not publicly circulate the thesis during the gap.

Note: VLAIO Innovation Mandate (September 2026 call) requires PhD diploma at the appropriate stage. That's a separate clock from the patent clock.
