% PREV: \begin{abstract}
% PREV: Street-level radiofrequency exposure at 15 GHz depends on the route geometry,
% PREV: surface materials, transmitter locations, and body orientation. This study maps
% PREV: 360-degree street images onto a photogrammetric city mesh and assigns surface materials
% PREV: around five selected pedestrian routes. A common source model sets the expected
% PREV: number of transmitters from their areal density and distributes them along the
% PREV: visible roofline in proportion to its physical length. All exposure values are
% PREV: normalized per unit areal density times effective isotropic radiated power.
% PREV: The calculation treats direct paths and one specular reflection exactly. It
% PREV: estimates one diffuse reflection and then stops the path.
% PREV: The directional fields are coupled to a 56,024-element Duke body surface. The
% PREV: five routes contain 73 route points. Each point uses 16 independent
% PREV: replicas, 200,000 primary rays per replica, and 4,096 fixed first-diffuse angular cells. In
% PREV: a controlled open-square case, the maximum bounced-term difference between the
% PREV: adjoint estimator and deterministic quadrature is 0.0616 dB, and the maximum
% PREV: total-transport difference between the adjoint and forward tracers is 0.0344
% PREV: dB. The route medians of normalized whole-body specific
% PREV: absorption rate differ by a factor of 13.34. Direct transport is the largest
% PREV: term at all 67 nonshadowed points. First-diffuse transport gives the only
% PREV: nonzero modeled contribution at six shadowed points. The maximum 12-to-16-replica change
% PREV: in total transfer is 0.0436 dB across the five sites, although lower-tail
% PREV: estimates in Mexico City and Tokyo are less stable. The result applies to five
% PREV: selected routes under the fixed model and does not estimate city-wide or
% PREV: deployed-network exposure.
% PREV: \end{abstract}

Body dosimetry, image-informed propagation, ray tracing, street imagery, urban
