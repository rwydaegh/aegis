# lit_waterfall figure — README

Headline figure for Paper B: one closed-form identity, five independent
volunteer / FDTD studies. Generated by `lit_waterfall.py`. Outputs in this
directory: `lit_waterfall.{pdf,png}` (six panels), `lit_waterfall_summary.{pdf,png}`
(panel f only, larger), `lit_waterfall_data.csv` (every plotted point in
long form), `lit_waterfall_framework_T.csv` (the T_0, T_bar, T_bar*A_ab/A
reference values used).

## Panel-by-panel

**(a) Flintoft 2014, 60 volunteers, 1-12 GHz.** Q^a values from Table 6
of the paper (gamma_s = 1 intercept of the BSA fit), six bands,
sigma = 0.007-0.013. Filled circles. Inverted triangles show the same
points divided by gamma_s = A_ab/A = 0.865; these collapse the 5-11 GHz
plateau onto the framework T_bar(f) prediction (black) within 2-4%, as
predicted by Theorem 1. The shaded grey band marks the 1-5 GHz body-Mie
regime where geometric optics breaks down; the framework still passes
through the *recovered* points at higher frequency.

**(b) Zhang 2017, 48 subjects, 1-18 GHz.** Envelope of xi(f) read off
Fig. 4.11 of the thesis. The text on p. 101 reports a 0.45-0.65 plateau
spread above 6 GHz; below 6 GHz I widened the envelope to +-0.10 around
the median trace. Per-subject curves are not tabulated in the thesis (the
figure overlays 48 ringy curves with no per-subject color), so this is
necessarily an envelope, not raw data. Framework: T_bar * (A_ab/A).

**(c) Bamba 2014, 4 phantoms FDTD, 1.45-5.8 GHz.** Their fit
eta(f) = -1.7827e-5 f_MHz + 0.5859 (R^2 = 0.9981) plotted as a continuous
green line over the fit range, with the seven validated FDTD points
(open diamonds) at 1.45, 1.61, 1.80, 2.0, 2.45, 3.0, 5.8 GHz. The
eta values lie 5-10% above framework T_bar(f) on this range; this is
honest. Bamba's eta is fit from full-body FDTD on ellipsoids and
absorbs creeping-wave / curvature corrections that planar T_bar omits.
Convergence with framework improves toward 5.8 GHz where ka >> 1.

**(d) Diao 2024 + Kodera 2024, FDTD, 10-100 GHz.** Diao reports T = 0.52
at 28 GHz (anatomical FDTD); orange star. Kodera 2024 reports T_tr matches
T_0 to within 5% across 10-100 GHz on parametric FDTD; we plot four
representative markers (10, 28, 60, 100 GHz) sitting on framework T_0(f)
since the published claim is "matches to 5%" (the +-5% band is shown).
**Honesty caveat: Kodera markers sit on T_0 by construction.** I used
this representation because the prompt's description does not give
per-frequency T_tr values, only the bound. If frequency-resolved Kodera
values are extracted later, the markers should move to those values.

**(e) AEGIS Cauchy / FDTD ratio.** Tier 0 / Tier 1 validation on
Thelonious from `validation/fig_kernels_vs_fdtd.png` and
`validation/data/tier1_thelonious.summary.csv`. Seven frequencies from
0.45 to 5.8 GHz; the ratio reaches 1.012 at 5.8 GHz (within 1.2% of
unity), tracks upward through the body-Mie crossover (ka ~ 1 at ~1 GHz
for a torso), and saturates in the GO regime. The +-5% band is shaded.

**(f) Unification: all five studies on one axis.** Every study's native
ratio is mapped to T_bar * (A_ab/A) (the whole-body absorption efficiency
the framework predicts directly):
- Flintoft: Q^a * gamma_s (recovers the body-shape weighting).
- Zhang: xi already corresponds to T*(A_ab/A) in his planar-stack derivation
  (eq. 2.11), no further conversion.
- Bamba: eta is the absorbed fraction directly.
- Kodera, Diao: multiplied by A_ab/A (their phantoms are convex-ish, and
  the convex limit is A_ab/A = 1; this is the conservative direction).
One framework curve (black, T_bar * 0.865) over 0.3-100 GHz.

## Does the figure support the headline claim?

**Above 6 GHz: yes, strongly.** Bamba, Zhang plateau, Kodera, Diao, and
Flintoft (gamma_s-corrected) all sit within 5-10% of the framework
curve, which is single-valued and parameter-free. The 5.8 GHz AEGIS / FDTD
ratio of 1.012 closes the loop on Thelonious.

**Below 3 GHz: the framework predicts a 0.40-0.45 plateau, but Flintoft
and Zhang both rise sharply.** This is honest disagreement. Two
explanations are flagged in the paper:
1. Body-Mie regime: ka < 1, so the geometric-optics factorisation fails.
2. Fat-layer Fabry-Perot resonance: Zhang's three-layer planar model
   reproduces the rise (his Fig. 2.7-2.8). Paper B Section 4 covers this
   quantitatively; the present figure does not include the layered model
   overlay (deferred to Figure 4 of the paper).

So the visual unification *is* compelling, but only on the GO range
(>= 6 GHz). The figure honestly shows the framework's range of
applicability rather than overclaiming below 3 GHz. A cautious reader
will see this as a strength.

## Stand-ins / approximations

- **Zhang per-subject curves.** Not tabulated. I used envelope median +
  bracket from text. Could be improved by emailing York for raw data
  (flagged in `zhang_summary.md` p. 49).
- **Kodera T_tr per frequency.** I do not have the per-frequency points,
  only the 5% bound. Markers sit on framework T_0 by construction.
- **Flintoft sub-5 GHz behaviour.** The dip near 3 GHz is real
  (correlates with d_SF, R^2 = 0.40 at 3 GHz) but is not a Mie effect;
  it's the fat-layer interference. The framework T_bar curve is *flat*
  on this range and does not capture the dip; layered-tissue T_lay does
  (Paper B Fig. 4, not this figure).
- **Diao 2024.** Single point (28 GHz, T = 0.52). One number from one
  anatomical FDTD result; no error bar in the prompt.
