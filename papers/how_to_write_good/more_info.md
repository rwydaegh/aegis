1. Keywords should be not too many, something around 7'ish words (if very short they can be a bit more, less if longer). It's important all the keywords are really short and punchy and fairly broad. They totally be acronym's. 5G or 6G is very often one. Exposure metrics like APD/SAR/... too. Raaarely compounded words or too technical words. You have to picture a little bit you're putting this in the grand IEEE paper catalogue, although that can be somewhat niche, you cant exxagerate and have half an abstract as keyword list with highly technical terms... The TAP paper is great in this: APD, dosimetry, FDTD, Fresnel transmission, ICNIRP, mmWave, SAR.
2. Actually count the number of words in the abstract. It CANNOT exceed 250-300, and preferably you target 200-250. Just because there is a ban on gls does not mean you shouldnt spell out acronyms. Eg if you use FR2 first time the abstract, you simple say Frequency Range 2 (FR2) and then you may reuse FR2 in that abstract. if in the intro you need FR2, you go back to respelling it out Frequency Range 2 (FR2) and then reusing it after that, as if the abstract never existed (btw check this again if you really did this, for FR2).
3. Since we manually write citations, we also have to make sure we read the whole text and ensure that citations appear in order in the pdf. So we have to keep the order right.
