\section{Coherent dosimetry: the exposure operator}\label{sec:coherent}

The incoherent levels of \cref{sec:grid} add path powers and
lose all phase. A phased array does not: the field at a body point is
the coherent superposition of every element's contribution, and the
absorbed power depends on how those contributions interfere. This
section carries the complex field through the surface law, factors the
whole-body absorbed power into a single Hermitian operator $\Qop$, and
shows that worst-case exposure, exposure-constrained beamforming, and
multi-user MIMO all reduce to spectral problems on $\Qop$. It closes
with the complex Fock gate and the precise sense in which the coherent
model dominates the incoherent one.

\subsection{The field channel}

A base station with $M_{\mathrm{ant}}$ elements drives the array with a
precoder $\xx\in\Complex^{M_{\mathrm{ant}}}$. Each propagation path $n$
originates at one element $j(n)$ and arrives at a body point with
direction $\khat_n$, polarisation-amplitude vector $\psivec_n$, and
phase $e^{-ik_0\khat_n\cdot\rr}$. Grouping the paths by originating
element gives the field channel matrix
$\mathbf{G}(\rr)\in\Complex^{3\times M_{\mathrm{ant}}}$ with columns
$\mathbf{g}_j(\rr)=\sum_{n:j(n)=j}\psivec_n\,e^{-ik_0\khat_n\cdot\rr}$,
and the total incident field is $\EE(\rr)=\mathbf{G}(\rr)\,\xx$, exact
in the locally plane-wave regime of \cref{sec:nearfar}.

The surface filters that field. The Fresnel transmission operator of
\cref{sec:fresnel} acts per path as the rank-two projector
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fn}
  \mathbf{F}_n(\rr)=\bigl(t_{s,n}\,\hat e_{s,n}\hat e_{s,n}^{\!\top}
                        +t_{p,n}\,\hat e_{p,n}\hat e_{p,n}^{\!\top}\bigr)
                    H(\mu_n),
\end{equation}
splitting $\psivec_n$ into its TE and TM parts, scaling each by its
transmission coefficient, and gating front-facing incidence through
$H(\mu_n)$. Collecting the depth-decay prefactor $\sqrt{\sigma/4\alpha_n}$
(with $\alpha_n=k_0\IM\xi_n$ the field attenuation in tissue) defines the
\emph{body-surface channel}.

\begin{definition}[Body-surface channel]\label{def:Gtil}
The exposure channel vector for element $j$ and the body-surface channel
matrix are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gtil}
  \tilde{\mathbf g}_j(\rr)=\sum_{n:j(n)=j}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{4\alpha_n}}\,
    \mathbf{F}_n(\rr)\,\psivec_n\,e^{-ik_0\khat_n\cdot\rr}\in\Complex^3,
  \qquad
  \Gtil(\rr)=\bigl[\tilde{\mathbf g}_1(\rr),\ldots,
                   \tilde{\mathbf g}_{M_{\mathrm{ant}}}(\rr)\bigr]
            \in\Complex^{3\times M_{\mathrm{ant}}}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Unlike the user-equipment channel, which collapses each path to a scalar
through the receive antenna response and discards the vector structure,
$\Gtil$ retains the full 3D field. That structural difference is the
geometric origin of the decoupling between signal quality and body
absorption.

\subsection{The coherent absorption law}

The absorbed power density is the ohmic loss of the transmitted field
integrated over depth. Expanding the squared norm over path pairs
produces a depth coupling factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda}
  \Lambda_{nn'}=\int_0^\infty
    e^{-(\alpha_n+\alpha_{n'})z}\,e^{i(\beta_n-\beta_{n'})z}\,\diff z
   =\frac{1}{\alpha_n+\alpha_{n'}-i(\beta_{n'}-\beta_n)},
\end{equation}
a Hermitian positive-semidefinite matrix with $\Lambda_{nn}=1/2\alpha_n$.
The single approximation that closes the algebra is the universal depth
coupling
$\Gamma_{nn'}=\Lambda_{nn'}/\sqrt{\Lambda_{nn}\Lambda_{n'n'}}\approx 1$,
which is exact on the diagonal and holds to $\abs{1-\Gamma_{nn'}}\le 0.44\%$
across all incidence-angle pairs for skin at \SI{28}{\giga\hertz},
because the high refractive index $\abs{\ntilde}\approx 4.8$ compresses
every refraction angle to within $\sim\!10^\circ$ of normal. With
$\Lambda_{nn'}=\sqrt{\Lambda_{nn}\Lambda_{n'n'}}$ the double sum
collapses into the expansion of a single squared norm.

\begin{theorem}[Coherent absorption law]\label{thm:coherent-law}
Under the TM-direction approximation of \cref{sec:fresnel} and universal
depth coupling, the absorbed power density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coherent-law}
  \Sab(\rr)=\norm{\Gtil(\rr)\,\xx}^2
           =\xx\herm\,\Gtil(\rr)\herm\Gtil(\rr)\,\xx,
\end{equation}
for any precoder $\xx$.
\end{theorem}

For a single path the squared norm collapses to one term, and the
depth-integral identity $\frac{\sigma}{4\alpha_n}\abs{t_{s,n}}^2
=T_{s,n}\cos\theta_n/Z_0$ recovers exactly the polarisation-aware law of
\cref{sec:pol}, $\Sab=\Sinc\,\Teff\,\ReLU(\mu)$. The coherent law is
thus the phased-array lift of the surface law: the incoherent levels are
its diagonal, the cross-terms are the interference the array controls.

\subsection{The exposure operator}

Integrating the coherent law over the body surface yields a quadratic
form in the precoder.

\begin{definition}[Exposure operator]\label{def:Q}
The exposure operator is the Gram integral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-def}
  \Qop=\int_\Sigma \Gtil(\rr)\herm\Gtil(\rr)\,\diff A
     \;\approx\;\sum_m a_m\,\Gtil_m\herm\Gtil_m
     \;\in\;\Complex^{M_{\mathrm{ant}}\times M_{\mathrm{ant}}},
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{abs}}=\xx\herm\Qop\,\xx,
\end{equation}
where $\Gtil_m=\Gtil(\rr_m)$ and $a_m$ is the area of triangle $m$.
\end{definition}

$\Qop$ is Hermitian positive-semidefinite by construction, with rank at
most $\min(M_{\mathrm{ant}},3M_\triangle)$. Its eigendecomposition
$\Qop=\sum_k\lambda_k\mathbf v_k\mathbf v_k\herm$
($\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge 0$) is the spectral description of the body's
response to the array: $\mathbf v_1$ is the precoder direction that
maximises whole-body absorption,
$\lambda_1=\max_{\norm{\xx}=1}P_{\mathrm{abs}}$ is the worst-case absorbed
power per unit transmit power, and $\operatorname{tr}\Qop=\sum_k\lambda_k$
is the absorbed power under isotropic transmission. The operator plays
the same role in the coherent regime that the absorption directivity
plays in \cref{sec:geom}: it reduces the infinite-dimensional space
of precoders to a finite spectrum. The quadratic form
$\xx\herm\Qop\xx$ shares the algebraic structure of the SAR matrix of
Hochwald \emph{et al.}, but $\Qop$ integrates over the whole body and
its entries follow in closed form from the path geometry and Fresnel
coefficients, with no FDTD calibration.

A path-space factorisation makes this practical. Writing the channel as
$\Gtil(\rr)=\tilde{\boldsymbol\Psi}(\rr)\,\boldsymbol\Phi(\rr)\,\mathbf J$
with $\mathbf J$ the element-to-path dispatch matrix, the operator
factors as $\Qop=\mathbf J^{\!\top}\mathbf M\,\mathbf J$ through the
$N\times N$ path-exposure Gram $\mathbf M$. Worst-case exposure and the
optimal precoder then live on the $N$ paths rather than the
$M_{\mathrm{ant}}$ elements, which is the smaller space whenever the
array is large.

\subsection{MRT, worst case, and exposure-constrained beamforming}

Maximum-ratio transmission $\xx_{\mathrm{MRT}}=\sqrt P\,\mathbf h^*/\norm{\mathbf h}$
maximises received signal power at the user. Its exposure cost is one
number, the exposure-signal alignment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
  \varrho=\frac{\mathbf h^{\!\top}\Qop\,\mathbf h^*}
              {\norm{\mathbf h}^2\,\lambda_{\max}(\Qop)}\in[0,1],
  \qquad
  P_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\mathrm{MRT}}=P\,\varrho\,\lambda_{\max}(\Qop).
\end{equation}
A large $\varrho$ means the dominant signal paths also illuminate the
body at moderate incidence; a small $\varrho$ means they arrive at
grazing incidence or from self-shadowed directions, so the body is
geometrically protected and strong signal coexists with low absorption.

The worst case is the other extreme. By Rayleigh--Ritz,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worstcase}
  \max_{\norm{\xx}=1}\xx\herm\Qop\,\xx=\lambda_{\max}(\Qop),
\end{equation}
attained at the top eigenvector $\mathbf v_1$: the energy-constrained
beamformer that an adversary, or a conservative compliance bound, would
choose. Between the two lies the design objective, maximise signal
subject to an absorption cap,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:QCQP}
  \max_{\xx}\ \abs{\mathbf h^{\!\top}\xx}^2
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
  \xx\herm\Qop\,\xx\le P_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\max},\ \ \norm{\xx}^2\le P.
\end{equation}
This QCQP has the closed-form solution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ecbf}
  \xx^\star=\sqrt P\,
    \frac{(\lambda\Qop+\nu\mathbf I)^{-1}\mathbf h^*}
         {\norm{(\lambda\Qop+\nu\mathbf I)^{-1}\mathbf h^*}},
\end{equation}
with multipliers $\lambda,\nu\ge 0$ fixed by complementary slackness.
The inverse $(\lambda\Qop+\nu\mathbf I)^{-1}$ regularises MRT in the
$\Qop$-eigenbasis: it suppresses the precoder along high-absorption
modes and leaves the absorption null-space unpenalised. As
$\lambda\to 0$ it reduces to MRT; as $\lambda\to\infty$ it steers
strictly through the null-space. A Woodbury push-through lifts
\cref{eq:ecbf} to the path space, replacing the
$M_{\mathrm{ant}}\times M_{\mathrm{ant}}$ inverse by the nonzero
eigenpairs of $\mathbf M\mathbf P$, of which there are at most
$M_{\mathrm{ant}}$.

\subsection{Multi-user and multi-body}

The single-user law extends without new approximations. With $K$ data
streams the total precoder is $\xx_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_k\mathbf w_k s_k$,
and since the symbols are uncorrelated the expected absorbed power
density on body $u$ is the incoherent sum of per-stream coherent
contributions,
$\mathbb E[\Sab^{(u)}(\rr)]=\sum_k\norm{\Gtil^{(u)}(\rr)\mathbf w_k}^2$.
Integrating gives a per-body exposure operator $\Qop^{(u)}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MU}
  P_{\mathrm{abs}}^{(u)}=\sum_{k=1}^K\mathbf w_k\herm\Qop^{(u)}\mathbf w_k,
\end{equation}
so each stream contributes to every body it illuminates, intended
recipient or bystander. Sum-rate maximisation subject to per-body caps
$P_{\mathrm{abs}}^{(u)}\le P_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\max}$ keeps the convex
quadratic structure and drops into WMMSE or alternating optimisation.
The limiting case where every cap is zero is exposure-null precoding:
the precoder is confined to the quiet subspace
$\mathcal Q=\bigcap_u\ker\Qop^{(u)}$, the joint absorption null-space,
of dimension at least $M_{\mathrm{ant}}-\sum_u\operatorname{rank}\Qop^{(u)}$.
This generalises classical zero-forcing, which nulls rank-one user
channels, to nulling the rank-$3M_\triangle$ absorption operators of
whole bodies.

\subsection{The coherent Fock gate}

Diffraction enters the coherent channel as the single complex Fock gate
$g(\xi)=\Philit+\Psishad$ of \cref{sec:fock-local}, evaluated at the
detour $\xi$ (positive in the lit region, negative in the shadow). It is
carried per polarisation, the soft constant on the TE field and the hard
constant on the TM field, and folded directly into the Fresnel
transmission of \cref{eq:Fn} by replacing the coefficients,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gated-fresnel}
  t_{s,n}\ \longmapsto\ g_{\mathrm{soft}}(\xi_n)\,t_{s,n},
  \qquad
  t_{p,n}\ \longmapsto\ g_{\mathrm{hard}}(\xi_n)\,t_{p,n}.
\end{equation}
Deep in the lit region $g\to 1$ and the channel is the exact
geometric-optics field, unchanged. Across the penumbra the gate applies
the complex transition: amplitude rolloff and phase shift on the
field, not on the power. Because $\abs{g}^2$ on the incoherent power and
$g$ on the coherent field come from the same function, the incoherent
and coherent diffraction models are consistent by construction.

\subsection{Coherent dominates incoherent, and where}

The complex gate cannot lower the total dose relative to the incoherent
magnitude gate, and matched-filter steering can raise it.

\begin{proposition}[Coherent dominance in the dose-relevant region]\label{prop:coh-ge-incoh}
In the lit region and the penumbra,
$\operatorname{tr}\Qop_{\mathrm{coh}}=\operatorname{tr}\Qop_{\mathrm{inc}}$,
because $\abs{g}^2=\abs{g}^2$ pointwise so the complex gate loses no
total dose against the magnitude gate. The worst-case coherent dose
dominates the isotropic incoherent dose,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coh-ge-incoh}
  M_{\mathrm{ant}}\,\lambda_{\max}(\Qop)\ \ge\
  \operatorname{tr}\Qop\ \ge\ P_{\mathrm{abs}}^{\mathrm{iso}},
\end{equation}
the constructive-interference headroom of matched-filter steering over
incoherent superposition.
\end{proposition}

The domain of \cref{prop:coh-ge-incoh} is the lit region plus penumbra,
which is where the dose lives. It does not extend into the deep
geometric shadow. The Fresnel operator hard-zeros the transmission
($t_s=t_p=0$ for $\mu\le 0$), so the coherent body channel carries no
field past the terminator: the coherent-local model injects no creeping
field into the deep shadow, whereas the incoherent model adds a residual
$\abs{g}^2\sim 10^{-6}$ of the lit value there. The coherent model is
therefore pointwise below the incoherent one by that same negligible
factor in the deep shadow, a documented limitation rather than a
violation of the construction. Carrying the complex creeping field into
the coherent deep shadow requires the diffracted-geodesic field vector,
which the Fresnel operator does not supply, and is the same follow-up
family as the coherent-distal phase: gated on a two-body oracle and
deferred (cf.\ the diffraction decisions log, L9).
