# EU funding and buyers: the shortest verified path into the defense RF-exposure domain

Agent report, written 2026-07-09. Answers the "get someone to pay for the research instead of selling a navy a product" question in the shared brief, section 5. Everything below is checked against a primary source where the source column says "yes". Where I could not verify an identifier I wrote UNVERIFIED and did not invent a substitute.

## 1. Verdict

There is a real, funded, named route, but not the one the deadline wants: the fastest defense money is a Flemish VLAIO defense call and an EDF disruptive-technology call that UGent (not the spinoff) enters, both months out, while the only thing even conceivably signable before 3 August is a soft academic letter of interest from the Royal Military Academy, so the IOF proposal should be graded on civilian demand and treat defense as a labelled future option, not a pre-deadline deliverable.

## 2. The three things that most changed my view

1. **The EDF call codes the memo carried from Gemini are wrong in a specific, checkable way, and the real ones are more open than feared.** The official SME call fiche's own change log (version 1.1, 25 Feb 2026) reads "Topic title corrected to delete -STEP". So `EDF-2026-LS-DIS-NT-STEP` and `EDF-2026-LS-DA-DIS-OTHR-STEP` as Gemini reported them are outdated: the `-STEP` suffix was struck. The live disruptive-tech call is `EDF-2026-LS-DIS-NT`, it is funded at 100%, and it needs only two entities from two countries, and it accepts public bodies. UGent can be a beneficiary. (Source: the call fiche PDF, verified below.)

2. **The IOF gate is real but it points the other way than the brief fears.** UGent's own policy says, verbatim, "The UGent does not cooperate in research that is solely militarily useful," while permitting dual-use research under conditions and a named committee. IOF is internal UGent money, so it cannot fund a purely-military product, but AEGIS is civilian-first (5G, base stations, MRI-adjacent) with a defense application, which is textbook dual-use. The consequence is not "the pivot is unfundable". It is "do not fund the military pivot from IOF, fund it from VLAIO or EDF, and keep the IOF story civilian-anchored". A defense letter of support pinned to the IOF proposal is a double-edged object, not a pure win.

3. **VLAIO stopped excluding weapons work in April 2025 and now runs a standing Flemish defense call that a single Belgian SME can enter alone.** This is the one instrument in the whole map that does not need a multi-country consortium, does not need a company with three years of accounts, and funds research organisations and companies both. It is the realistic first euro. (Source: VLAIO, verified below.)

## 4. European Defence Fund

### What is actually open

The 2026 work programme was adopted 17 December 2025, is worth about €1.01bn across 31 topics, and is organised as 9 thematic calls plus 1 non-thematic call for SMEs plus disruptive-technology actions. All 2026 calls opened 15 April 2026 and close 29 September 2026 at 17:00 Brussels time. Source: EDF WP 2026 factsheet (European Commission) and the individual call fiches.

| Call / topic | Identifier (verbatim) | What it funds | Consortium minimum | Rate | Budget | Deadline | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-thematic disruptive tech | `EDF-2026-LS-DIS-NT` | Any area of defence interest, TRL >= 4, 12-24 mo, <= €3M/project | 2 entities from 2 eligible countries, public or private legal entities | 100% | €27M call | 29 Sep 2026 17:00 CET | yes (call fiche) |
| Non-thematic development, SMEs | `EDF-2026-LS-DA-SME-NT` | SME development actions | 3 SMEs from 3 countries, coordinator must be an SME | 65% base + bonuses to 100% | €30M call | 29 Sep 2026 | yes (call fiche) |
| Non-thematic research, SMEs and research orgs | `EDF-2026-LS-DIS-RA-SMERO-NT` | Research actions for SMEs and research organisations | 3 entities from 3 countries (SMEs and/or research orgs) | 100% | €35M (EUDIS page) | 29 Sep 2026 | code + rate yes; budget from EUDIS page, not fiche |
| Gemini's reported codes | `EDF-2026-LS-DIS-NT-STEP`, `EDF-2026-LS-DA-DIS-OTHR-STEP` | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | NO. The `-STEP` suffix was deleted per the fiche change log v1.1. Do not use these. |

Note on the disruptive call name: the work programme lists two disruptive-tech topics, "New abilities in over-the-horizon sensing" and "Non-thematic actions targeting disruptive technologies for defence", under a €91M disruptive-technologies envelope. The non-thematic one is `EDF-2026-LS-DIS-NT` and is verified in full above. An `EDF-2026-LS-DA-DIS`-style fiche URL exists on the portal but I did not open it, so any `-OTHR` code is UNVERIFIED.

### Can a company that does not yet exist apply? No.

Two independent walls, both from the primary fiche text:

- **Legal entity by the deadline.** Every applicant must be a validated legal entity registered in the Participant Register by the submission deadline. A company that does not exist cannot register.
- **SME self-assessment needs closed accounts.** For SME-status topics, the "SME self-assessment must be based on the financial figures of the last closed accounting period and not older than 31/12/2024". A spinoff incorporated in 2026 has no qualifying closed accounting period, so it cannot self-certify as an SME for the 2026 SME calls even if it incorporates in time.

### Can UGent apply instead and subcontract? Yes, and this is the intended shape.

`EDF-2026-LS-DIS-NT` eligibility is "legal entities (public or private bodies)" with no SME restriction, so UGent qualifies directly. The `-SMERO-NT` research call explicitly names research organisations. So the minimum viable EDF consortium for Robin is UGent plus one entity in a second EDF country (EU member states plus Norway as the associated country), on the disruptive call, at 100% funding, up to €3M, decided over roughly six months. The spinoff, once it exists, can sit in as an affiliated entity or subcontractor. This is a genuine route, but it is a UGent-led route with a September deadline, not a spinoff-led one, and it is irrelevant to 3 August.

## 5. Belgian national route

### DIRS is a gateway, not primarily a cheque

The Defence, Industry and Research Strategy is run by the Royal Higher Institute for Defence (RHID/KHID/IRSD). Its own site frames it as coordinating the Belgian defense technological and industrial base and steering national participation into EDF, EDA and NATO STO. A widely reported figure is about €350M/yr for research, innovation and industrial production under the strategy, but that number is from a secondary outlet, not a Belgian MoD budget line, so treat it as indicative (UNVERIFIED). Practically, DIRS is the concierge that gets you into EDF and STO, not a standalone grant you win.

### VLAIO is the real Belgian instrument, and it is fast

| Item | Detail | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| Policy change | Since 4 April 2025 (Flemish Defence Plan) offensive weapon systems are no longer excluded from VLAIO R&D&I support | yes (VLAIO) |
| Instrument | "Oproep Defensie" defense call under the development/research project schemes | yes |
| 2025 round | €10M, deadline was 4 Nov 2025 (now past) | yes |
| 2026 round | "Continue oproep defensie 2026" (continuous call) exists | existence yes; page content UNVERIFIED (HTTP 403 to me) |
| Who can apply | Companies and research organisations, single applicant allowed (no multi-country consortium needed) | yes |
| Rate | Development 25% base up to ~60%, research 25-50% base up to 80% per partner, with SME and cooperation bonuses | yes |
| Ethics gate | Dossiers that are primarily military go to an "Ethical and Strategic Advisory Committee" | yes |

This is the single best-matched Belgian funding vehicle for the dual-use framing of AEGIS. It takes a single SME applicant, so Robin's spinoff can apply on its own once incorporated, no consortium hunt. The catch is that it is an application to prepare, not a signature you can wave at an IOF panel by August.

### RMA / Royal Military Academy

The relevant unit is the CISS department (Communication, Information, Systems and Sensors), site sic.rma.ac.be. Named professors verified via RMA and ResearchGate: Marijke Vandewal and Xavier Neyt. CISS does radar signal processing, electromagnetic and infrared signature modelling of ships, and mine-countermeasures work at EU and NATO level. Two honest caveats: their published EM work is signature/RCS-type, which is exactly the passive-scattering problem the brief says AEGIS structurally cannot do, so this is a "partner to explore adjacent problems" relationship, not "a buyer who needs your exact tool". There is no standing published mechanism for a Flemish university to partner with RMA that I could verify, so this runs on personal introduction, which is where Wout's network matters.

### The IOF gate, answered

UGent policy, verbatim: "The UGent does not cooperate in research that is solely militarily useful." Dual-use is permitted if civilian use of the results is preserved, there is no contribution to human rights violations, misuse is prevented, and the "Human Rights Policy and Dual Use Research Commission" is consulted. Because IOF is internal UGent money bound by this policy:

- IOF cannot be the funding vehicle for a purely-military RADHAZ product. If the IOF StarTT proposal were framed as a military product, it would trip the "solely militarily useful" wall.
- IOF can fund the civilian-anchored core (base-station and 5G EMF compliance, MRI-adjacent exposure), with defense as one downstream application. That framing is dual-use and passes, subject to the commission.
- Therefore the military pivot does not need, and should not be built on, IOF money. Fund it from VLAIO defense, EDF, or DIRS-brokered routes, and keep IOF civilian-first.

This is the answer to the brief's "hard gate" question: IOF money should not touch the defense pivot directly, but that is fine because better-matched vehicles exist, and it means a defense letter of support attached to the IOF file is a liability as much as an asset, because it can pull the proposal toward the "solely military" review it wants to avoid.

## 6. NATO

### STO is real and the RF-exposure lineage is exactly Robin's world

NATO STO runs collaborative research through panels including HFM (Human Factors and Medicine) and SET (Sensors and Electronics Technology). The precedent the brief hoped for is genuine: the HFM-189 Research Task Group evaluated RF/EMF personnel exposure limits and its conclusions fed IEEE C95.1-2345-2014, transposed into STANAG 2345. RF exposure of personnel is squarely an HFM-panel topic with a documented track record. So the "build defense-lab credibility without a product" claim survives contact with the sources.

### How a Belgian academic actually joins

| Step | Detail | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | Register via APPOINT / Science Connect (e-Appoint) for a Level 3 research team; Level 2 committees go via the coordinator directly | yes (sto.nato.int, dirs.be) |
| Belgian National Coordinator | Lieven Vandevondel, Lieven.vandevondel@mil.be | yes (dirs.be) |
| Endorsement | Your nation's principal HFM or SET panel member endorses you; national validation follows | yes |
| Timeline | Days to weeks for appointment; task groups themselves run about three years | yes |
| Cost | No membership fee; STO runs on the "costs lie where they fall" model, so your institution funds your time and travel | model inferred, not quoted |

The real constraint: joining needs an open activity that matches, or you propose an Exploratory Team, which itself needs national sponsorship. HFM-189 is historical, and I could not verify a currently-open RTG on coherent RF exposure or agile-array bioeffects (UNVERIFIED). So the concrete near-term move is an email to Vandevondel asking which HFM/SET activities are open and whether an Exploratory Team on agile-array exposure certification would be welcome. This yields credibility, not money, and not a customer letter.

### DIANA and the NATO Innovation Fund: reachable, but not now

- **DIANA.** Any incorporated company headquartered in a NATO nation can enter a DIANA challenge, with €100k non-dilutive plus up to €300k for Phase Two. But the 2026 cohort (150 innovators) is already selected, entry needs an incorporated company and a matching challenge area, and the Belgian test centre is VITO in sustainability/materials/biotech, which is a weak fit for RF sensing. Realistic for a future cycle once the company exists.
- **NIF.** A €1bn multi-sovereign VC fund (Belgium participates) investing equity in early-stage dual-use startups. It is dilutive, needs a company and a venture thesis, and is not a fit for a pre-revenue, no-company applicant.

## 9. The US question

Fantasy step at this stage, and the instrument is half-inverted in the memo. A Technical Assistance Agreement is a US-side instrument, administered by DDTC, that authorises a US company to furnish defense services or ITAR-controlled technical data to a foreign person, for items on the US Munitions List. Two problems for the memo's framing:

- Robin licensing his EU-origin solver to a US firm is not a TAA at all. That direction is an ordinary commercial software licence governed by EU and Belgian dual-use export control (Regulation 2021/821 and Flemish licensing), not by DDTC. A TAA would only enter if the US firm shared ITAR technical data back to Robin, for example real ship geometry or waveform tables.
- Either way it needs a willing cleared US firm to sponsor and file a DDTC agreement, a months-long process, for a pre-revenue EU spinoff with no product proven in their domain. There is no commercial reason a US firm does that now.

Realistic only after there is a validated product and a specific US partner who wants it. Not a 2026 move.
