# Oral brief for Filip

## The central point

Filip's challenge improved the strategy, but it should now produce a decision. The wider search found interesting electromagnetic research problems. It did not find a more mature customer problem, validation route, or product wedge than dosimetry.

The request is not that Filip believe AEGIS will certainly become a EUR 5 to 7 million company. The request is that he judge whether EUR 250,000 can remove the risks separating an existing technical asset from paid pilots, a licence, or a focused spin-off.

## Suggested sequence

1. "I took your concern seriously. I tested whether the method had a stronger commercial application outside dosimetry, and I also rebuilt the dosimetry case more narrowly."

2. "The result is not that every broader EM application is impossible. It is that none currently has the combination of technical maturity, buyer evidence, standards timing, and a validation path that dosimetry has."

3. "The dosimetry product is not a cheaper standalone seat for the same small group that buys Sim4Life. It is the workflow that screens beams, poses, distances, and antenna configurations, and selects what must go to FDTD and measurement."

4. "There are three different economic questions. Can the project justify EUR 250,000? Can it support a durable specialist business or licence? Can it eventually exceed ZMT's present scale? We should not require proof of the third before funding the experiment that answers the first two."

5. "The funded sensor and equine examples did not prove dominant market penetration in advance. They supplied a plausible buyer, route, staged evidence, and economics proportional to the grant. AEGIS should be judged on the same basis."

6. "A seven-figure specialist outcome is enough to justify the project. The credible larger case is EUR 5 to 7 million in years 7 to 9, but only if we sell an enterprise workflow and obtain one or two embedded distribution deals. I will not present that upside as a validated base forecast."

7. "The IOF is not two years of generic programming. The central research package is a blind comparison against qualified FDTD/FEM, an independent APD measurement campaign, an uncertainty model, and explicit accuracy and false-clearance gates."

8. "I am asking that broader generalisation stop being a prerequisite. We can preserve it as a bounded patent-priority-year experiment, but it should not block the dosimetry validation or continually reopen the company thesis."

9. "Before meeting Dries, Hendrik, or Keysight, I want us to specify what question that meeting is meant to answer. I am comfortable having the physics and business challenged. I do not want the product to be redefined in the room without a testable hypothesis or stop condition."

10. "If UGent does not want to support the dosimetry route, I can accept that. I need a concrete decision, the evidence that would change it, and a date."

## If Filip says the incumbent only makes EUR 2 to 3 million

"First, that figure is a low-confidence private-company estimate, not an audited market ceiling. More importantly, ZMT's current turnover does not define the value of one workflow built on top of its tools. The seat-only case may indeed be small. The larger case comes from enterprise device programmes, lab workflow, and embedded distribution."

Then return to proportionality:

"Even if the upper case never materialises, a EUR 1 million recurring-revenue specialist business, a meaningful OEM licence, or a strategic acquisition can justify a EUR 250,000 validation project. The project is precisely what determines which outcome is real."

Do not argue that AEGIS will definitely become larger than ZMT. That invites a debate neither side can resolve now. Argue that the downside is sufficient, the upside is material, and the project contains the gates needed to distinguish them.

## If the room becomes assertive or diffuse

Use one sentence to restore structure:

"Can we turn that objection into a test with a metric, responsible party, and decision date?"

For a broad Keysight proposition:

"Which named workflow and buyer would use this in the next 24 months, and what result would make them pay?"

For a technical challenge:

"That is currently outside the validated claim. The proposed work package either brings it inside with evidence or excludes it."

For another request to generalise:

"Is this a condition for validating the dosimetry product, or an independent opportunity? If it is independent, it should have its own owner and six-month commercial gate."

## What to say about ZMT

Externally, describe ZMT as a credible validation and distribution counterpart. A scientific co-validation can lead to a plugin, embedded licence, option, acquisition, or employment relationship. Do not promise any of those outcomes before ZMT has seen a matched benchmark.

Privately, wanting a well-paid role at ZMT is a legitimate preferred outcome. It offers salary, continuity in the technical field, and a team that understands the product. The risk is negotiating from dependence. Validation and protected IP create several possible outcomes and improve the terms of all of them.

Do not tell Filip that employment at ZMT is the commercial objective. Tell him that ZMT is one plausible licensee or strategic destination, while the IOF should preserve multiple routes until the evidence and terms are real.

## Material to keep out of the email

- Frustration with repeated scope changes.

- Judgments about the personalities of Dries or Hendrik.

- Personal salary, inventor-share, or acquisition preferences.

- Custorix or Valtorix.

- Claims that ZMT, Keysight, or another vendor will buy AEGIS.

- Claims of certification equivalence, universal accuracy, or a guaranteed EUR 5 to 7 million outcome.
