Pose-information-gain ablation for §VII.E, $M = 256$ panel-size sensitivity. Pose-aware vs.\ pose-ablate, same seed, plaza-specular path model, binding regime at $43\,$dBm conducted transmit power and $E_{\mathrm{RL}} = 6\,$V/m, on the $16\times 16$ panel. (a) ECDF of per-slot $\Delta$ sum-rate (aware $-$ ablate, Mbps), four precoders. MRT, WC back-off, and the per-body oracle coincide with $\Delta = 0$ by construction. The proposed multi-body ECBF is bimodal with wider tails than at $M = 64$: $20.4\%$ of slots at $\Delta\textsf{SR} < -1\,$Gbps (posed-Q in min-absorption fallback while the Cauchy variant remains feasible), $11.5\%$ at $\Delta\textsf{SR} > +1\,$Gbps (the opposite), $0.3\%$ within $|\Delta\textsf{SR}| < 100\,$Mbps. Median $\Delta\textsf{SR} = -438\,$Mbps, mean $-3.4\,$Gbps. (b) Per-body $P_{\mathrm{abs}}$ ECDF for the multi-body ECBF, aware vs.\ ablate. The pose-aware curve dominates: $3.41\%$ aware violations vs $8.44\%$ ablate ($2.5\times$ compliance gain, compressed from the $4.7\times$ at $M = 64$ because the higher-violation regime amplifies absolute fallout). The Cauchy envelope's looser feasible set lets the maximizer push absorbed power against the budget when the T-pose envelope under-estimates the true posed absorption ($99^{\mathrm{th}}$ percentile $5.96\,L_{\mathrm{RL}}$ for the ablate curve); the pose-aware solver, working against the tighter posed operator, holds margin at the cost of more frequent solver fallback ($88.5\%$ aware vs $74.9\%$ ablate, both with warm-started $\bm{\lambda}^{(t)}$ and $\textsf{max\_outer}=8$). The trade-off is qualitatively identical to $M = 64$ but with wider $\Delta\textsf{SR}$ variance per slot: the panel-size sweep argues the closing item of \cref{sec:multibody-ecbf} (uncap the Newton sweep, replace the Cauchy-on-tier-C envelope) becomes more urgent, not less, as $M$ grows.
