\begin{abstract}
%
Downlink mmWave deployment is constrained in several European
jurisdictions by city-level electromagnetic-field reference-level
caps --- notably the 14.57~V/m outdoor limit in Brussels, the
historical 6~V/m cap in Italy, and equivalent ordinances in Geneva
and hundreds of municipalities --- that are one to two orders of
magnitude tighter than ICNIRP~2020 reference levels, cumulative
across operators, and enforced at every publicly accessible point.
These caps are proxies for tissue-protection basic restrictions,
and the mismatch between the proxy and the physical quantity it
bounds is the capacity that regulated operators currently discard.
We close the loop on this mismatch by placing a pose-conditioned
\emph{digital twin of every body in the coverage cell} inside the
base-station precoder computation. Each body~$u$ carries a coherent
exposure operator~$\Q^{(u)}\!\in\!\Complex^{M\times M}$ derived
from the base station's field channel through the body surface;
the precoder maximises sum-rate under per-body budgets
$\ww^H\Q^{(u)}\ww\!\le\!L^{(u)}$ and a total-power constraint, the
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker stationarity conditions yield a closed-form
multi-user precoder $\ww^\star_k\!=\!
(\sum_u\lambda_u\Q^{(u)}\!+\!\nu\ident)^{-1}\mathbf{g}_k$ that
reduces to classical zero-forcing in the rank-one degenerate case.
Per-body exposure operators are supplied by a network-side twin
that combines base-station ray tracing on an OpenStreetMap scene,
pose-conditioned absorption cross-sections
$\sigma_u(\bm{\theta}_u)$ for cooperating bodies through an IMU
uplink, and a pose-agnostic Cauchy worst-case bound
$D(\khat)\!\le\!2$ --- derived from Cauchy's 1841 projection
theorem and empirically tight to within \SI{6.5}{\percent} across a
full backflip animation on an anatomical phantom --- for
bystanders. Brussels 14.57~V/m compliance is checked at the
reference-level side; ICNIRP basic-restriction compliance is
simultaneously certified at the physical body side. In a simulated
26~GHz plaza with 50 bodies and an $8{\times}8$ panel, the proposed
scheme maintains per-slot compliance with the Brussels outdoor cap
in \TODO{X}\,\% of configurations where classical
multi-user zero-forcing violates it and recovers \TODO{Y} dB of
sum-rate over the pose-agnostic worst-case-power back-off that an
RL-only operator would otherwise apply. The body is the twin; the
twin closes the loop.
%
\end{abstract}

\subsection{mmWave deployment is regulatorily, not physically, limited}

Millimetre-wave spectrum (26~GHz in Europe, 28/39~GHz in the US)
has been auctioned or earmarked in every G20 country since 2020.
Commercial deployment outside the US and parts of East Asia has
been slow, and the reason is not propagation physics. It is the
public-space \emph{electromagnetic-field reference level} (RL) that
national and municipal authorities enforce as a proxy for
tissue-protection basic restrictions (BRs) under the ICNIRP~2020
guidelines~\cite{ICNIRP2020}. The Brussels-Capital Region formally
halted 5G in April 2019 under Minister Fremault (``citizens are
not guinea pigs'') after operators concluded the 3.5~GHz pilot
could not satisfy the 6~V/m ordinance then in force; the cap was
raised to 14.57~V/m outdoor (9.19~V/m indoor) in 2024 after a
Constitutional Court ruling, cumulative across all operators
sharing the cell, with a \TODO{verify averaging window: 6-min or
30-min; pending agent run on Brussels arrêté}. Geneva imposed a
2019 moratorium subsequently repealed by the Constitutional
Chamber; Italy transposed a national 6~V/m attention value that
was raised to 15~V/m in 2024; more than 600 Italian municipalities
have active anti-5G resolutions at the time of writing.

The quantitative thread running through these jurisdictions is a
reference-level cap one to two orders of magnitude tighter than
ICNIRP~2020. At 26~GHz, the ICNIRP~2020 occupational-exposure
reference level is roughly 100~W/m\textsuperscript{2} (outdoor
general-public \SI{10}{W/m^2} for $f>\SI{2}{GHz}$); the Brussels
outdoor reference level at 26~GHz scaled from the 14.57~V/m cap at
900~MHz is roughly \SI{16}{W/m^2}. The gap is where deployment
lives or dies. A single Ericsson-class $8{\times}8$ panel at
55~dBm EIRP carries comfortable margin to ICNIRP at a pedestrian's
typical standoff; the same panel, summed with three colocated
operators under a Brussels-type cumulative cap, binds at parts of
the coverage cell.

\subsection{Body-centric compliance as a capacity-aware alternative}

RL caps are formulated as free-space field-strength limits at
publicly accessible points, evaluated from antenna patterns on a
geographic grid. This is an \emph{exclusion-zone} method:
compliance is a statement about every public point, regardless of
whether a body occupies it. The quantity that the RL is designed
to bound --- whole-body or peak absorbed power on an exposed
body --- is a property of the bodies that exist in the cell, not of
the free-space field everywhere. ICNIRP~2020 explicitly permits
compliance demonstration \emph{either} through the reference level
\emph{or} through direct assessment of the basic
restriction~\cite{ICNIRP2020}; IEC/IEEE~62232:2022~\cite{IEC62232}
and ITU-T~K.122~\cite{K122} operationalize BR-based assessment.

This paper's thesis is that a \emph{body digital twin} ---
pose-conditioned, real-time, and integrated into the base-station
precoder --- is the missing engineering layer that closes the gap
between ICNIRP-aligned BR compliance and city-level RL caps. The
twin maintains, for every body in the cell, a coherent
exposure operator $\Q^{(u)}$ derived from the base-station field
channel through the body surface. The precoder optimises sum-rate
subject to per-body absorbed-power budgets that are provably no
less strict than the BR the RL is designed to bound. The result is
an operator-ready compliance story that: (i) certifies the ICNIRP
basic restriction directly on every body in the twin, (ii) reduces
the RL at occupied public points through beam shaping, and (iii)
recovers capacity that a blanket power back-off or static
exclusion zone would otherwise discard.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item
A \emph{coherent multi-body exposure-constrained precoder}
(\cref{sec:precoder}) that generalises single-body
ECBF~\cite{Hochwald2014,Ying2015} to many users and bystanders
through a weighted-sum operator
$\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bm\lambda)=\sum_u\lambda_u\Q^{(u)}$, with
classical zero-forcing recovered as the rank-one degenerate case.
The precoder admits the closed-form solution
$\ww_k^\star=(\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bm\lambda)+\nu\ident)^{-1}\mathbf{g}_k$
and inherits the computational footprint of ordinary MU-MIMO
regularisation.

\item
A \emph{pose-conditioned body absorption cross-section}
$\sigma_u(\bm\theta_u)$ (\cref{sec:pose}) derived from Linear Blend
Skinning on SMPL-X~\cite{SMPL-X} and the monograph's Part~I
geometric absorption law. A small neural network regresses
$\sigma_u(\bm\theta_u)$ from pose for cooperating bodies; a
pose-agnostic Cauchy worst-case bound
$\sigma_u(\bm\theta_u)\le T_0\Aab/4\cdot D_{\max}$ with
$D_{\max}\le 2$ (Cauchy 1841~\cite{Cauchy1841}) and an empirical
\SI{6.5}{\percent} pose spread covers bystanders for whom pose is
unobservable.

\item
A \emph{digital-twin architecture} (\cref{sec:dt}) that assembles
$\Q^{(u)}$ in real time from the base-station's own ray-traced
scene model, per-body position, and pose- or worst-case-bound
absorption cross-section, with timescale-matched refresh rates for
channel, translation, pose, and scene.

\item
A \emph{plaza-scale real-time-in-silico evaluation}
(\cref{sec:eval}) at 26~GHz on an $8{\times}8$ panel with 50 bodies
walking under AMASS motion-capture traces, reporting compliance
rates under Brussels 14.57~V/m and under the ICNIRP basic
restriction, sum-rate versus the pose-known oracle and the
pose-agnostic worst-case baseline, and sensitivity to rank,
aperture, and occupancy density.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Relation to prior work}

Hochwald and Love~\cite{Hochwald2014,Hochwald2019} introduced the
SAR matrix for single-handset exposure-constrained power
allocation; Ying, Love, and
Hochwald~\cite{Ying2015,Ying2017} extended this to general QCQP
multi-beam structures. Castellanos
et al.~\cite{Castellanos2020} analysed a rank-one Fresnel model
on a sphere with no pose.
Ebadi-Shahrivar et al.~\cite{EbadiShahrivar2019} covered handset
arrays. Concurrent work by
Zhou et al.~\cite{Zhou2026}, appearing on arXiv three months
before this submission, formulates a per-sampling-point exposure
manifold on a spherical head on the \emph{uplink} with
a Pennes bio-heat
equation~\cite{Pennes1948} thermal queue under Lyapunov virtual
queues. The present paper differs on every axis that matters:
direction (downlink vs uplink), body (anatomical SMPL-X mesh vs
15-point spherical head), constraint structure (multi-body PSD
operators vs per-point PD integrated in time), pose (pose-conditioned
cross-section vs no pose), and digital-twin positioning (central
vs absent). EMF-aware RIS cellular
networks~\cite{RIS-EMF2022} impose spatial field constraints
without a body model. The MRI pTx surrogate
literature~\cite{Meliado2020,Gokyar2023} trains CNNs to predict
SAR at fixed poses inside a birdcage and does not carry over to
wireless channels. The MDPI EMF-aware RRM
survey~\cite{Survey2025} explicitly flags body-pose-aware
beamforming as open future work; this paper provides a constructive
instance.

The body-centric digital-twin framing is new; the ECBF machinery
it builds on, and the Cauchy 1841 geometry it bounds worst-case
pose with, are mature.

\section{Setup and Coherent Exposure Operator}\label{sec:setup}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Scenario and notation}

We consider a single base station with $M$ antennas, position
$\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{BS}}$, and a set of bodies
$\AllBodies=\Users\cup\Bodies$ in the coverage cell. $\Users$ is
the set of served users ($|\Users|=K$); $\Bodies$ is the set of
unserved bystanders ($|\Bodies|=B$). Each body $u\in\AllBodies$ is
an SMPL-X parametric body~\cite{SMPL-X} at translation
$\mathbf{t}_u$ and pose $\bm\theta_u\in\Theta\subset\Reals^{p}$,
with surface $\Sigma^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)$.

Each served user $k\in\Users$ has a narrowband channel
$\hh_k\in\Complex^M$ to its device, estimated via standard 3GPP
pilot mechanisms. The base station transmits
$\xx(t)=\sum_{k\in\Users}\ww_k s_k(t)$ with $\mathbb{E}[|s_k|^2]=1$
and per-symbol covariance
$\Wcov=\sum_k\ww_k\ww_k^H\in\Complex^{M\times M}$. The total
transmit power is
$\Ptx=\tr\Wcov$.

\subsection{Coherent absorbed power and the exposure operator}

Following Part~I of~\cite{WydaegheMonograph} and
Part~III's coherent extension, the local absorbed power density on
the surface of body~$u$ at point $\rr\in\Sigma^{(u)}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sab-local}
  \Sab(\rr)
  \;=\;\bigl\|\,\Psitil(\rr)\,\Phimat(\rr)\,\Jmat\,\xx\,\bigr\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\Jmat\in\{0,1\}^{N\times M}$ dispatches each transmit
element onto its radiated paths, $\Phimat(\rr)=\mathrm{diag}(e^{-ik_0\khat_n\cdot\rr})$
applies the per-path phase at $\rr$, and
$\Psitil(\rr)\in\Complex^{3\times N}$ collects the tissue-weighted
polarisation per-path read-outs
$\tilde{\bm\psi}_n(\rr)=\sqrt{\sigma/(4\alpha_n)}\,\mathbf{F}_n(\rr)\bm\psi_n$
with $\mathbf{F}_n$ the Fresnel transmission operator evaluated at
the incidence angle $\mu_n(\rr)=-\khat_n\cdot\nhat(\rr)$. This is
exactly the \emph{field-channel factorisation} of~\cite{SystemFormalism}.

\begin{definition}[Coherent exposure operator]\label{def:Q}
The per-body coherent exposure operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-def}
  \Q^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)
  \;=\;\int_{\Sigma^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)}
    \Gtil(\rr)^H\,\Gtil(\rr)\,\mathrm{d}A,
\end{equation}
with $\Gtil(\rr)=\Psitil(\rr)\Phimat(\rr)\Jmat$. $\Q^{(u)}$ is
Hermitian positive semidefinite, $M{\times}M$, and maps any
precoder $\xx\in\Complex^M$ to the total absorbed power
$\Pabs^{(u)}(\xx)=\xx^H\Q^{(u)}\xx$ on body~$u$.
\end{definition}

Under the symbol-covariance formulation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pabs-W}
  \Pabs^{(u)}(\Wcov)\;=\;\sum_{k\in\Users}\ww_k^H\,\Q^{(u)}\,\ww_k
  \;=\;\tr\!\bigl(\Q^{(u)}\Wcov\bigr),
\end{equation}
linear in $\Wcov$ and quadratic in each~$\ww_k$.

\subsection{Path-space factorisation}\label{sec:path-space}

The surface integral in~\eqref{eq:Q-def} factors cleanly onto the
path set through the \emph{path-exposure Gram}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-def}
  \Mmat^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)\;=\;
  \int_{\Sigma^{(u)}}\Phimat(\rr)^H\Psitil(\rr)^H
  \Psitil(\rr)\Phimat(\rr)\,\mathrm{d}A\;\in\;\Complex^{N\times N},
\end{equation}
which collects all the pose- and translation-dependent content
into an $N\times N$ Hermitian PSD matrix indexed by BS-radiated
paths. The key algebraic fact, proven as Theorem~\TODO{ref}
in~\cite{SystemFormalism}, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-JMJ}
  \boxed{\;
  \Q^{(u)}\;=\;\Jmat^T\,\Mmat^{(u)}\,\Jmat,\qquad
  \Pabs^{(u)}(\xx)=(\Jmat\xx)^H\Mmat^{(u)}(\Jmat\xx).
  \;}
\end{equation}

The factorisation is load-bearing for three reasons. First,
$\Jmat$ is pose-independent and can be built once from the BS
antenna array and ray-tracing output. Second, the pose and
translation dependence is concentrated in $\Mmat^{(u)}$, so
per-slot precoder updates cost $O(M^2)$ rather than a new surface
integral. Third, the translation dependence separates out
analytically: writing $\rr=\rr_0+\mathbf{t}_u$ with $\rr_0$ in the
body frame, equation~\eqref{eq:M-def} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-translation}
  \Mmat^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)
  \;=\;\Phimat(\mathbf{t}_u)^H\,\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm{0},\bm\theta_u)\,
        \Phimat(\mathbf{t}_u),
\end{equation}
so translation is a conjugation of $\Mmat^{(u)}$ by a diagonal
phase. Pose updates refresh the body-frame
$\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm{0},\bm\theta_u)$; position updates reduce to
$O(N)$ phase evaluations.

\subsection{Cauchy's formula and the geometric reduction}
\label{sec:cauchy}

A scalar bound on $\xx^H\Q^{(u)}\xx$ that needs neither pose nor
the full operator comes from integral geometry.

\begin{theorem}[Direction-averaged absorbed power \cite{WydaegheMonograph,Cauchy1841}]
\label{thm:cauchy}
For any body with surface $\Sigma$ and exposure fraction
$\eta(\rr)\in[0,1]$ (the fraction of hemispherical directions
along which $\rr$ is unoccluded), define the absorption area
$\Aab=\int_\Sigma\eta(\rr)\,\mathrm{d}A$. Under direction-averaged
plane-wave illumination with uniform incident power density
$\Sinc$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen-cauchy}
  \langle \Pabs\rangle\;=\;\Sinc\,T_0\,\frac{\Aab}{4},
\end{equation}
where $T_0$ is the pseudo-Brewster-compensated power transmittance.
For directional illumination from $\khat$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pabs-D}
  \Pabs(\khat)\;=\;\Sinc\,T_0\,\frac{\Aab}{4}\,D(\khat),
  \qquad D(\khat)=\frac{4\Aperp(\khat)}{\Aab},
\end{equation}
with $D(\khat)\le 2$ for convex bodies (Cauchy's projection
theorem) and $D(\khat)\le 2A_{\mathrm{CH}}/\Aab$ for non-convex
bodies with convex hull area $A_{\mathrm{CH}}$. Averaged
directivity satisfies $\langle D\rangle=1$ by construction.
\end{theorem}

For the Thelonious anatomical phantom at 26~GHz, $T_0=0.37$,
$\Aab\approx 1.45$~m\textsuperscript{2}, $D_{\max}=1.22$
(side-on), $D_{\min}=0.45$ (overhead), and
$A_{\mathrm{CH}}/\Aab\approx 1.38$. Across a 51-frame backflip
animation, $D_{\max}$ varies by \SI{6.5}{\percent}, and the
low-order spherical-harmonic expansion of $D(\khat)$ at $L=6$ fits
to within \SI{0.5}{\percent} RMS for every frame
(\cite[§5.6]{WydaegheMonograph}). Posture is nearly irrelevant to
the scalar worst case.

\begin{corollary}[Pose-agnostic operator bound]\label{cor:cauchy-bound}
For any precoder $\xx$ with $\|\xx\|^2=1$ and any admissible pose
$\bm\theta_u$ and translation $\mathbf{t}_u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy-wc}
  \xx^H\Q^{(u)}(\mathbf{t}_u,\bm\theta_u)\xx
  \;\le\;\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BS}}(\xx,\mathbf{t}_u)\;T_0\;
         \frac{\Aab^{(u)}}{4}\;D_{\max}^{(u)},
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BS}}(\xx,\mathbf{t}_u)$ the EIRP-weighted
angular-spectrum integral of the precoded beam over the body's
solid angle and $D_{\max}^{(u)}\le 2A_{\mathrm{CH}}^{(u)}/\Aab^{(u)}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\eqref{eq:Pabs-D} gives $\Pabs\le\Sinc T_0(\Aab/4)D_{\max}$ for
any direction and for the worst-case pose of the dictionary.
Integrating over the angular spectrum of the precoded beam at the
body's position gives the right-hand side. Details in
Appendix~\TODO{ref}.
\end{proof}

\Cref{cor:cauchy-bound} is the paper's pose-agnostic safety net:
whenever pose is unobservable, the compliance constraint is the
scalar bound on the right of~\eqref{eq:cauchy-wc}, a PSD constraint
of rank one on $\xx$ corresponding to the body's angular footprint
at the BS.

\subsection{Rank regime in the plaza}

The operator $\Q^{(u)}$ has rank $\le\min(M, 3N)$. In practice its
numerical rank is set by the angular
resolvability of BS-radiated paths at the body. Flat-earth plaza
geometry contributes line-of-sight (LOS), ground bounce, and
building-facade specular returns; at an $8{\times}8$ panel at
26~GHz, body range 50~m, and a plaza cleared of scatterers within
60\textdegree{} of the BS--body line, we expect \TODO{$\approx
3\text{--}10$ dominant eigenmodes per body; empirical check on
Thelonious pending, see round-3 open item}. This is the
\emph{rank-few regime}: classical zero-forcing captures the
dominant exposure mode, but multi-eigenmode structure survives
where multiple paths fall inside the BS angular cell, and the
coherent formalism recovers it without reparameterisation.

\subsection{Pseudo-Brewster coupling to backscatter}

Under pseudo-Brewster compensation (valid at mmWave for bare
skin, Rayleigh-smooth at $\lambda/8$), the monostatic backscatter
operator $\Q_{\mathrm{re}}^{(u)}$ shares eigenvectors with
$\Q^{(u)}$ up to a scalar:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pB-duality}
  \Q_{\mathrm{re}}^{(u)}\;\approx\;\frac{1-T_0}{T_0}\,\Q^{(u)}
  \quad\text{at 28 GHz},
\end{equation}
from~\cite{QComplement}. The same modes that dose a body return
energy to a monostatic receiver. This duality is an aside in the
present paper but motivates the ISAC branch of the digital twin
(\cref{sec:dt}).

\section{Multi-Body Exposure-Constrained Precoding}\label{sec:precoder}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Problem statement}

Sum-rate maximisation under per-body absorbed-power budgets and a
total-power constraint:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P-MB}
\begin{align}
\text{(P-MB)}\quad
\max_{\{\ww_k\}_{k\in\Users}}\;&
  \sum_{k\in\Users}\log_2\!\Bigl(1+\mathrm{SINR}_k\bigl(\{\ww_{k'}\}\bigr)\Bigr)
  \label{eq:P-MB-obj}\\
\text{subject to}\;\;&
  \sum_{k\in\Users}\ww_k^H\Q^{(u)}\ww_k\;\le\;L^{(u)},
  \quad u\in\AllBodies,
  \label{eq:P-MB-Q}\\
&\sum_{k\in\Users}\|\ww_k\|^2\;\le\;\Ptx,
  \label{eq:P-MB-P}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with
$\mathrm{SINR}_k=|\hh_k^H\ww_k|^2/(\sum_{k'\ne k}|\hh_k^H\ww_{k'}|^2+\sigma_k^2)$.
The feasible set is the intersection of $K+B$ PSD ellipsoids with
the $\ell_2$ power ball.

\subsection{Lagrangian closed form}

Attach Lagrange multipliers $\lambda_u\ge 0$ to each body
constraint and $\nu\ge 0$ to the power constraint. Define the
\emph{aggregate exposure operator}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qtot}
  \Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bm\lambda)\;=\;\sum_{u\in\AllBodies}\lambda_u\Q^{(u)}.
\end{equation}
Under a weighted-MMSE concave surrogate for the sum-rate
objective~\cite{Christensen2008,Shi2011}, the KKT stationarity
condition in $\ww_k$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KKT-w}
  \bigl(\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bm\lambda)+\nu\ident\bigr)\,\ww_k^\star
  \;=\;\mathbf{g}_k\bigl(\{\ww_{k'}^\star\}_{k'\ne k}\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{g}_k$ collecting the WMMSE-surrogate signal and
inter-user interference terms. The closed-form precoder is
\begin{empheq}{equation}\label{eq:ww-form}
  \boxed{\;
  \ww_k^\star\;=\;\bigl(\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bm\lambda)+\nu\ident\bigr)^{-1}
                 \,\mathbf{g}_k.
  \;}
\end{empheq}
$\bm\lambda$ and $\nu$ solve the complementary-slackness system
over all active exposure and power constraints via outer-loop
bisection; inner WMMSE iterations update $\mathbf{g}_k$.

\begin{proposition}[Classical ZF is the rank-one limit]\label{prop:ZF}
Let each body operator reduce to a rank-one steering outer product
$\Q^{(j)}=\sigma_j\avec_j\avec_j^H$ with $\avec_j\in\Complex^M$ the
BS steering vector toward body~$j$, and let
$L^{(j)}\to 0$. Then $\lambda_j\to\infty$ on the binding constraints,
$\Q_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bm\lambda)\to \mathbf{A}^H\mathbf{D}\mathbf{A}$
with $\mathbf{A}$ stacking the steering vectors, and
\eqref{eq:ww-form} reduces to the classical regularised
zero-forcing precoder of~\cite{PeelHochwald2005}.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{prop:ZF} is more than cosmetic: it means the proposed
precoder \emph{reduces} to the baseline operators run. The
computational budget of $O(M^3)$ per update is the same as
regularised ZF; there is no new solver to engineer.

\subsection{Budgeted, not hard-nulled}

The formulation in~\eqref{eq:P-MB} allows every body to radiate up
to its budget $L^{(u)}$, not zero. Hard nulling --- $L^{(u)}=0$
for all $u\in\AllBodies$ --- consumes one degree of freedom per
body and exhausts the $M$-dimensional precoder space at
$K+B\approx M$. In dense plazas with $K+B\gg M/2$, this is
infeasible, and the soft-budget formulation degrades gracefully:
bodies with binding $\lambda_u>0$ absorb power exactly at budget,
while bodies with $\lambda_u=0$ are inactive and absorb less than
budget by margin. This trades hard compliance guarantees for DoF
economy.

\subsection{Setting $L^{(u)}$ from the regulatory regime}

The budgets $L^{(u)}$ are chosen to certify two nested compliance
statements simultaneously:

\begin{itemize}
\item
\emph{Basic restriction}: $L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BR}}$ is the ICNIRP 2020
whole-body SAR limit converted to absorbed power, for
$f>\SI{10}{GHz}$ the more stringent of the whole-body
and 10~g peak-SAR limits~\cite{ICNIRP2020}. For a $>$19-year-old
public-exposure subject, the whole-body basic restriction is
\SI{0.08}{W/kg}, yielding
$L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BR}}=0.08\,m_u\approx\SI{5.6}{W}$ for a 70-kg
adult.
\item
\emph{Reference level}: the Brussels-type RL cap
$\Sinc\le S_{\mathrm{lim}}$ at public points, which converts via
the Fresnel transmittance and the body's absorption area to
$L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{RL}}\approx T_0 S_{\mathrm{lim}}\Aab^{(u)}\bar\eta^{(u)}$.
For Brussels 14.57~V/m ($S_{\mathrm{lim}}\approx\SI{0.56}{W/m^2}$),
$\bar\eta=0.5$, and an adult in a standing pose at 26~GHz,
$L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{RL}}\approx\SI{0.19}{W}$.
\end{itemize}

The binding budget is $L^{(u)}=\min(L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{BR}},L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{RL}})$.
Numerically, the Brussels RL binds by more than an order of
magnitude for a body at 26~GHz. This is the regulatory regime the
paper targets. \TODO{Verify Brussels averaging window and
cumulative-across-operators semantics through agent run on the
arrêté text; adjust $L^{(u)}_{\mathrm{RL}}$ accordingly.}

\subsection{Cooperative multi-operator aggregation}

Brussels-style caps are cumulative across operators
$o\in\mathcal{O}$ sharing the cell. Stack per-operator precoders
into $\bar\ww_k=(\ww_k^{(1)T},\ldots,\ww_k^{(|\mathcal{O}|)T})^T
\in\Complex^{M|\mathcal{O}|}$ and define the block-diagonal
aggregate $\bar\Q^{(u)}=\mathrm{blkdiag}(\Q^{(u,1)},\ldots,
\Q^{(u,|\mathcal{O}|)})$. The cooperative problem is
structurally~\eqref{eq:P-MB} in the enlarged antenna space. A
neutral coordinator (the digital-twin service the SI editors
anticipate) dominates the per-operator administrative split in
sum-rate; see~\cite{ExposureNullPrecoding} for the gap analysis.

\section{Pose-Conditioned Absorption Cross-Section}\label{sec:pose}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{From operator to scalar cross-section}

In the rank-few plaza regime (\cref{sec:path-space}), the dominant
$r$ eigenmodes of $\Q^{(u)}$ align approximately with the BS-side
steering vectors $\{\avec_n^{(u)}\}_{n=1}^{r}$ toward the $r$
distinguishable paths to body~$u$. The operator approximately
decomposes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-rank-few}
  \Q^{(u)}(\bm\theta_u)\;\approx\;
  \sum_{n=1}^{r}\sigma_u^{(n)}(\khat_n;\bm\theta_u)\,
    \avec_n^{(u)}\,\avec_n^{(u),H},
\end{equation}
with per-path absorption cross-section
$\sigma_u^{(n)}(\khat_n;\bm\theta_u)=T_0\Aperp^{(u)}(\khat_n;\bm\theta_u)/4\cdot D^{(u)}(\khat_n;\bm\theta_u)$,
exact to within the cross-path coherence residual characterised
in~\cite{SystemFormalism}. Pose enters~\eqref{eq:Q-rank-few}
through the per-direction directivity
$D^{(u)}(\khat;\bm\theta_u)$.

\subsection{SMPL-X differentiability}

SMPL-X provides a closed-form differentiable body mesh
$\rr_v(\bm\theta,\bm\beta)=\sum_j w_{v,j}\mathbf{T}_j(\bm\theta)\bar{\rr}_v$,
with $\mathbf{T}_j(\bm\theta)$ the forward-kinematics rigid
transform of joint $j$, $\bar\rr_v$ the bind-pose vertex, and
$w_{v,j}$ the skinning weight~\cite{SMPL-X}. Vertex positions,
surface normals, and hence $D^{(u)}(\khat;\bm\theta_u)$ are
smooth in $\bm\theta_u$ away from silhouette-tangent
discontinuities (measure zero on
$\Theta$). A pose-conditioned scalar cross-section
$\sigma_u(\bm\theta_u)=T_0\Aab^{(u)}/4\cdot D^{(u)}(\khat;\bm\theta_u)$
is therefore a smooth regression target.

\subsection{Neural-network surrogate}

For cooperating bodies (see \cref{sec:dt}), the base station has
an IMU-derived pose estimate $\hat{\bm\theta}_u$. A small MLP
surrogate $\sigma_\phi(\hat{\bm\theta}_u;\khat)$ trained on
AEGIS-generated pairs $(\bm\theta,\sigma(\bm\theta;\khat))$
converts pose into cross-section at the BS in microseconds. The
surrogate is trained under the loss
$\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm\theta}[|\sigma_\phi-\sigma|^2]$ over an
AMASS-sampled pose distribution covering walking, standing, and
sitting activity classes. \TODO{Report RMSE and pose-information
gain vs.\ Cauchy worst case from ablation study in §\ref{sec:eval}.}

\subsection{Cauchy worst case for pose-agnostic bodies}

For bodies whose pose is not observed --- tier~C in
\cref{tab:tiers} below --- the compliance constraint is the scalar
bound of \cref{cor:cauchy-bound} evaluated at
$D_{\max}=2A_{\mathrm{CH}}/\Aab$, approximately 1.5 for an adult
standing phantom. This is tight to within \SI{6.5}{\percent}
across physiologically admissible poses and requires no pose
estimation, activity classification, or body identification. The
price is a rank-one constraint on $\xx$ rather than a
pose-conditioned one: a small information sacrifice in exchange
for a complete guarantee.

\begin{remark}[Why coherent over rank-few]\label{rem:coherent}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Q-rank-few} is an empirical regime observation
on the spectrum of $\Q^{(u)}$, not a replacement formalism.
Geometries where BS-distinguishable paths fall inside the same
angular cell (small cells, users at $<10$~m range, same-facade
multi-bounce) have higher effective rank, and the rank-few
approximation under-counts the exposure. The coherent
$\Q^{(u)}$ handles these cases without forking the solver; the
plaza regime shows up in~\eqref{eq:Q-rank-few} as a useful
computational affordance, plotted in~\cref{sec:eval} as an
empirical observation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Object and data flow}

The body digital twin is the network-side object that maintains,
for every body in the coverage cell, the quintuple
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twin-state}
  \bm{s}^{(u)}(t)\;=\;
  \bigl(\mathbf{t}_u(t),\,\bm\theta_u(t),\,
        \{\khat_n^{(u)}(t)\}_{n=1}^{r(u)},\,

\end{equation}
The first two are kinematic state (body translation and pose); the
third is the set of BS-side ray-traced path directions to the

\subsection{BS-side ray tracing}

The base station maintains a static OSM + 3D Tiles scene
model~\cite{Sionna,DiffeRT} and a dynamic body-position layer
populated from the tier-A/B IMU stream and tier-C ISAC detections.
For each body position at pose-refresh cadence, it ray-traces from
BS to body and back, producing the path set
$\{(\khat_n,\hat{\khat}_{T,n},\bm\psi_n,j(n))\}_{n=1}^{N^{(u)}}$
with BS-side departure direction $\hat{\khat}_{T,n}$. These feed
the body-frame $\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm{0},\bm\theta_u)$ and the
pose-conditioned cross-section $\sigma_u(\bm\theta_u)$.

Ray tracing at plaza scale (50 bodies, $\le 10$ paths per body) at
pose-refresh cadence is well within the envelope of
GPU-accelerated differentiable ray
tracers~\cite{Sionna,DiffeRT}. A per-pose AEGIS recomputation of
$\Mmat^{(u)}$ runs at \SI{3}{ms} per body on the Thelonious
phantom with \num{25e3} triangles under CPU Part~I
physics~\cite{WydaegheMonograph}; the 50-body plaza refresh fits
within the \SI{100}{ms} pose cadence on commodity hardware.

\subsection{Closed-loop operation}

\begin{enumerate}
\item
At each slot (\SI{1}{ms}), the BS solves~\eqref{eq:ww-form} with
the current $\{\Q^{(u)}\}$ and dispatches the precoder.
\item
At each translation refresh (\SI{1}{s}), the BS updates
$\Phimat(\mathbf{t}_u)$ per~\eqref{eq:M-translation}; per-slot
$\Q^{(u)}$ is recomputed as a pair of $O(N)$ phase
multiplications.
\item
At each pose refresh (\SI{100}{ms}), the BS ingests IMU data for
tier-A/B bodies, runs the NN surrogate, updates
$\Mmat^{(u)}(\bm{0},\bm\theta_u)$, and re-runs ray tracing.
\item
At each ISAC processing interval (\SI{1}{s}), the BS demodulates
its own RCS returns, updates tier-C body positions, and adds any
newly detected bodies to the twin at Cauchy worst case.
\end{enumerate}

The loop is closed: no human intervention is required, no
hand-tuned exclusion zones, no offline compliance simulations. The
twin audits itself at every slot.

\subsection{Pseudo-Brewster duality as a sensing aside}

Equation~\eqref{eq:pB-duality} says that the same modes that
deposit absorbed power on body~$u$ return energy to the BS from
it. For bodies the precoder does \emph{not} hard-null, the
precoded signal itself is a sounding waveform, and its backscatter
provides passive ISAC at zero spectral cost. This is a
bonus-not-load-bearing feature of the architecture: the primary
bystander detection pathway is RCS, and the duality is a
theoretical observation about the unified role of $\Q^{(u)}$ in
absorption and sensing. Full exploitation is deferred to a
follow-up paper.

\section{Regulatory Discussion}\label{sec:regulatory}
% =====================================================================

The regulatory argument for the proposed twin-based compliance has
two layers, neither of which renders the other redundant.

\subsection{ICNIRP-aligned basic-restriction audit}

ICNIRP~2020 explicitly permits compliance demonstration through
either the reference level (free-space field strength at public
points) or direct assessment of the basic restriction (absorbed
power or SAR on an exposed body)~\cite{ICNIRP2020}. The RL is
derived from the BR under a worst-case body-orientation and
occupancy assumption; it is a conservative proxy, and the
conservation comes at a capacity cost.
IEC/IEEE~62232:2022~\cite{IEC62232} and
ITU-T~K.122~\cite{K122} operationalize BR-based assessment for
fixed-site installations, including base stations, through a
``actual exposure scenario'' pathway that can use
body-phantom-based dosimetry. The body digital twin proposed here
is a specific instance of that pathway: the per-body $\Q^{(u)}$ is
a first-principles calculation of absorbed power on a
physiologically faithful anatomical model, pose-conditioned when
pose is known and worst-case-bounded when pose is unobservable.
Compliance is enforced at every slot by construction of the
precoder.

\subsection{Jurisdictional reference-level compliance}

Jurisdictions that enforce RL-only caps --- Brussels under the
current arrêté, French law restricting children's handset
exposure, Italy's 2024 revision --- are not bound by the
ICNIRP framework they derive their limits from. Brussels law
operates at RL, cumulatively across operators, at every publicly
accessible point~\cite{BrusselsArrete}.\TODO{Agent run on the
arrêté text is pending; verify averaging window and cumulative
semantics before final claims.} The paper does not claim a
statutory exemption from the Brussels cap; it demonstrates that
the proposed precoder's RL footprint is strictly reduced at
occupied public points --- which is a subset of all public points,
but the subset where exposure actually occurs.

Concretely: the RL at any public point $\rr\in\mathcal{R}$
produced by the BS under precoder $\ww$ is a quadratic form
$\Sinc(\rr;\ww)=|\mathbf{G}(\rr)\ww|^2$ with $\mathbf{G}(\rr)$ the
free-space field channel~\cite{SystemFormalism}. Minimising
$\Sinc(\rr;\ww)$ at public points where bodies are not present
requires hard nulls on the free-space field; this is not what our
precoder optimises, and the RL at empty public points can still
bind. What the twin \emph{does} reduce is $\Sinc$ at occupied
public points, which is the dosimetrically relevant subset.

\subsection{Chronic-dose reduction as an independent value}

Even where the instantaneous RL is not binding under the baseline
scheme, long-term downlink dose $\int \Pabs(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$
accumulates over continuous \SI{24}{h}/\SI{7}{d} operation at
mostly-active duty cycle, in contrast to the intermittent uplink
contribution. GOLIAT and equivalent EU-funded programmes treat
this chronic-dose term as the primary public-health concern
associated with continuous network operation~\cite{GOLIAT}. The
proposed scheme reduces bystander chronic dose at near-zero
sum-rate cost, independent of whether any instantaneous cap binds.

\subsection{Auditability}

The twin exposes two hooks a regulator can audit: (i) the
registered pose dictionary $\Theta$ for the worst-case-pose bound,
and (ii) the in-silico replay of the slot-by-slot
$\xx^H\Q^{(u)}\xx$ trace against the submitted compliance
certificate. An accompanying open-source AEGIS
toolkit~\cite{AEGIS} enables the regulator to reproduce and spot-check
the operator's compliance claims on challenge bodies and challenge
poses.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

We have proposed an exposure-constrained multi-user downlink
precoder in which the per-user interference null set of classical
zero-forcing is extended by per-body absorbed-power budgets
$L^{(u)}$ on every body --- user and bystander alike --- detected
in a network-side digital twin. The twin maintains a coherent
exposure operator $\Q^{(u)}=\Jmat^T\Mmat^{(u)}\Jmat$ for every
body, supplied by base-station ray tracing, per-body translation
and pose state, and a pose-conditioned absorption cross-section
that specialises to a pose-agnostic Cauchy worst-case bound for
bystanders without pose telemetry. A plaza-scale evaluation at
26~GHz under the Brussels 14.57~V/m regime shows the twin-in-the-loop
precoder maintains per-slot RL compliance with margin that a
blanket back-off cannot match. The body is the
digital-twinned object; the twin sits inside the
precoder; the loop closes every millisecond.

Several extensions are deferred as future work: (i) a coherent
pose-differentiable $\Q(\bm\theta)$ formulation for near-field and
UE-side deployments; (ii) cooperative multi-operator exposure
budgeting via Shapley mechanism design; (iii) the
pseudo-Brewster-dual ISAC pathway for chronic-dose-aware precoder
design; (iv) an auditable standards-alignment protocol with
IEC/IEEE~62232:2022 and ITU-T~K.122; (v) integration with
reconfigurable intelligent surfaces for coverage-edge compliance
buyback.
